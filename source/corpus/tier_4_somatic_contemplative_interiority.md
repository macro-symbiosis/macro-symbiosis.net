## 0. Tier Architecture & Four-Part Functional Homology

Tier 4 maps the mechanics and nutrition of load-bearing connective tissue, the physiology of balance and support contact, neuro-attentional and autonomic regulation, and the regulation of affect through the microcirculation onto Spine $\mathcal{T}$. Three apertures (4.1, 4.2, 4.4) are empirical apertures declared in **Branch II** of r7, the bounded non-equilibrium steady state (`aperture_generation_guidance.md` §2.3). Each declares, in its §1, a domain, physical metric, cone speed, storage coefficient, free energy with datum, transport tensor, interior sink, drive, and Branch II evidence, and instantiates all nine coordinates $O_0 \to O_\Omega$ on that domain. Each declared domain is of one kind: a stored quantity (dissolved oxygen, or blood held in a compliant capillary bed) spreading through a living tissue, with supply crossing the boundary and consumption or loss, where declared, by a named interior sink. Aperture 4.3 is **provisionally homological** in this epoch: it declares no domain, maps all nine coordinates as conceptual correspondences, like Tier 1, and makes no empirical claim (§0.1 item 12).

The wider phenomena named in the aperture titles (posture, balance, attention, autonomic regulation, affect, resilience) enter the empirical mappings as boundary conditions, through the declared parameters, through the hysteresis record $\mathcal{H}$, or as illustrative context, and each §1 states which. No Tier 4 aperture claims that attention, affect, or any interior state obeys a spine law. Each aperture is formulated at the level of general physiology, biomechanics, and neuroscience, for vertebrate and mammalian tissues and for human populations studied at cohort level, without presupposing a single practitioner, practice, or site. No human-subject test is included in this epoch (`empirical_validation_protocol.md` §3.3).

### 0.1 Conventions Common to the Tier 4 Apertures

The following conventions apply to every empirical aperture of this tier (4.1, 4.2, 4.4); item 12 states how the provisionally homological Aperture 4.3 is mapped. Each aperture's §1 and coordinate blocks state its specific values and cite these items where they apply. Where an item repeats a convention of `tier_3_ecological_living_networks.md` §0.1, it is restated here so that this tier can be read on its own.

1. **Branch and windows.** Branch II is declared before any test, with the estimators of item 7 and §1, and no Tier 4 domain is re-evaluated in Branch I on the same data (closure (v)). Statements that hold in Branch I only (`r10` Decision 6) are never applied. Every observation window is a **relaxation window**: it begins at a declared $\tau_0$ from a prior steady state, a declared transient starts at $\tau_0$, the external data are then held within declared tolerances, and the window follows the domain as it settles.
   * In every empirical aperture the transient is a declared step of the boundary data in the direction that raises $\mathcal{F}$: a step increase of the supply-chamber oxygen (4.1), of the oxygen supplied at the dermal–epidermal junction (4.2), or of the pressure at the arteriolar boundary (4.4). Each declared balance law is a scalar parabolic equation whose sink is Lipschitz and non-decreasing in $\mathcal{F}$ (Michaelis–Menten, first order), so the comparison principle holds. The prior steady state is a subsolution for the raised data, so $\mathcal{F}(\cdot, \tau_0 + h) \ge \mathcal{F}(\cdot, \tau_0)$; the held data make the problem invariant under time translation, so $\mathcal{F}(\cdot, \tau + h) \ge \mathcal{F}(\cdot, \tau)$, that is, $\partial_\tau\mathcal{F} \ge 0$ at every point. Where the step is in fact a ramp (non-decreasing boundary data that reach their held value over a finite rise time, as for the in-vivo supply of 4.2 and the arteriolar dilation of 4.4), the same conclusion follows without time translation: $\partial_\tau\mathcal{F}$ obeys the linearized equation with zero initial data and non-negative boundary data, so it stays non-negative. Robin data (4.2's outflow face) do not change the argument, and the duct lumens of 4.2, which receive oxygen only from the tissue, form with it a cooperative system, for which the comparison principle also holds. The argument is exact on rigid domains. Under the optional torsional perturbation of 4.1 the operator depends on $\tau$ through $g(\tau)$ at the order of the shear strain, so there the conclusion holds only to that order, and 4.1 checks it from its oxygen record.
   * *Direction.* The opposite direction is excluded in each aperture, because it would break a declaration. In 4.4 a fall of capillary pressure shrinks the tissue ($\theta < 0$), which alone excludes it; physically it can also turn filtration into reabsorption, an interior source, which the declared datum form of the sink cannot represent (4.4 §1). In 4.2 a change of contact load compresses the epidermis ($\theta < 0$) or, on unloading, is followed by a reactive hyperaemia whose overshoot makes the supply data non-monotone. In 4.1 an oxygen step is osmotically negligible, so either direction keeps the volume; the raising direction is declared for the comparison argument, and a glucose step is not declared because it changes the bath's osmolarity, and a rise of osmolarity deswells cartilage ($\theta < 0$).
   * *Boundary and lateral exchange.* Every exchange of the declared field across an internal or lateral boundary is one-signed, out of $\mathcal{M}$: transcapillary filtration in 4.4 is a sink and never reabsorption (4.4 §1), and exchange across the walls of the epidermal ducts in 4.2 runs from the tissue into lumens that receive oxygen from nowhere else (4.2 §1). No compartment outside $\mathcal{M}$ discharges into it within a window, except across the declared supply faces.
   * The rate of change of the flux, $\partial_\tau J_W$, is not one-signed in general (item 9).
2. **Metric.** The physical metric is the pull-back of the Euclidean metric to the material points of the declared body under its deformation map. It is flat at every $\tau$, so $G_{\mu\nu} \equiv 0$ and $R_{\mu\nu} \equiv 0$ (`aperture_generation_guidance.md` §2.3 item 2). No Tier 4 aperture declares a non-Euclidean material metric in this epoch: the growth or remodelling material metric that the master table admits for 4.1 is weighed in 4.1 §1 and not declared. The curved configurations of the `r10` §5.11 and §5.13 witnesses are therefore realized by none of these apertures, and r7's curvature sector is exercised by no Tier 4 aperture; with Tiers 2 and 3, it is exercised by no empirical aperture of Tiers 2–4.
   * *Rigid domains (4.2, 4.4).* In a Cartesian chart $g_{\mu\nu} = \delta_{\mu\nu}$, so $\Omega \equiv 1$, $\hat{h} \equiv 0$, $\theta \equiv 0$, and $\sigma^\mu_{\;\nu} \equiv 0$. The tissue is declared rigid within a pre-declared tolerance on its measured volumetric strain. In the declared window direction any residual volume change is non-negative (epidermal thickness under held load in 4.2; vascular distension and filtration in 4.4), and windows in which the measured volume decreases beyond the tolerance are excluded, so that $\theta \ge 0$ is not violated. With $\sigma \equiv 0$ the $O_3$ shear law holds trivially, and these apertures support no shear-relaxation test.
   * *Volume-rigid domain (4.1).* The volumetric strain of the explant stays within a declared tolerance, so $\theta \equiv 0$ in the declared metric. Without the optional torsional perturbation, the shear strain also stays within tolerance and 4.1 is treated as rigid. With it, the metric is the pull-back under an isochoric twist: in a Cartesian material chart $g(\tau) = \mathbf{F}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{F}$ with $\det\mathbf{F} = 1$, so $\Omega \equiv 1$, $\theta \equiv 0$, $\exp\hat{h} = g$, and $\sigma^\mu_{\;\nu} \neq 0$. On that deforming domain every covariant quantity (Laplacian, Hessians, convexity) is evaluated with $g(\tau)$.
3. **Storage.** The r7 $O_4$ balance law carries unit storage: $\partial_\tau\mathcal{F}$ is the rate of change of the stored quantity per unit volume. A living medium stores its diffusing quantity $u$ with a coefficient $s$ (stored amount per unit volume per unit of $u$): for dissolved oxygen in 4.1 and 4.2, the tissue's water volume fraction times the partition coefficient of oxygen between tissue and bath water, with $u$ the bath-equivalent oxygen concentration (oxygen tension times its solubility in bath water), which is continuous across the tissue's faces; in 4.4, the compliance of the capillary bed per unit tissue volume. With $s$ uniform, the physical balance $s\,\partial_\tau u = \nabla_\alpha(k^{\alpha\beta}\nabla_\beta u) - r$ becomes the r7 law for $\mathcal{F} = \epsilon u$ (with $\epsilon$ a declared constant yield), $\mathfrak{K} = k/s$, and $\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{F}} = \epsilon r/s$, and the r7 flux is $J_W = (\epsilon/s)\,j$, with $j = -k\nabla u$ the physical flux. The factor $\epsilon/s$ is a declared conversion factor, estimated independently (closure (ii)). If $s$ varies in space, $s^{-1}\nabla_\alpha(k^{\alpha\beta}\nabla_\beta u) = \nabla_\alpha(s^{-1}k^{\alpha\beta}\nabla_\beta u) + s^{-2}k^{\alpha\beta}\nabla_\alpha s\,\nabla_\beta u$, and the last term is a drift term that the r7 balance law does not contain. Each aperture therefore declares **a single storage class**: $s$ uniform on $\mathcal{M}$ within a tolerance (a declared, checkable precondition). Adjacent media with a different coefficient (other zones of the cartilage in 4.1; the stratum corneum and the dermis in 4.2; arterioles, venules, and the interstitium in 4.4) lie outside $\mathcal{M}$ and enter as boundary data or compartments, because a jump in $s$ would make $J_W$ discontinuous where the physical flux is continuous, which the unit-storage law reads as an interface source or sink.
4. **Stress sector.** With $G_{\mu\nu} \equiv 0$, $T_{\mu\nu}^{\mathrm{mat}} \equiv 0$; its trace gives $\nabla_\alpha\xi^\alpha = 0$ for $n = 3$, and then $\nabla_{(\mu}\xi_{\nu)} = 0$, so $\xi$ is a Killing field. No Tier 4 medium has an identified global control field in this epoch, so Tier 4 declares $\eta^\lambda \equiv 0$, hence $\xi_\nu \equiv 0$ and $R = \kappa_G\nabla_\alpha\xi^\alpha = 0$, and the $O_4$ equivalence $\nabla^2\xi_\nu + R_\nu^{\;\alpha}\xi_\alpha = 0 \iff \nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu}^{\mathrm{mat}} = 0$ holds identically. The $\tau$-compatibility law then reduces to $\mathbb{L}_{\mathrm{Ein}}[\sigma]_{\mu\nu} = \frac{n-2}{n}(\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu\theta - g_{\mu\nu}\nabla^2\theta)$, and its trace, the traced elliptic equation, to $\frac{n-1}{n}\nabla^2\theta = \nabla^\alpha\nabla^\beta\sigma_{\alpha\beta}$. These are the Saint-Venant compatibility conditions of the strain rate, which every deformation of an embedded body satisfies because its metric stays flat; with $\theta \equiv 0$ (all Tier 4 domains) they read $\mathbb{L}_{\mathrm{Ein}}[\sigma] = 0$ and $\nabla^\alpha\nabla^\beta\sigma_{\alpha\beta} = 0$. The mechanical stresses of tissues (the swelling prestress of cartilage, myofascial tension, contact pressure, vessel-wall stress) are not $T_{\mu\nu}^{\mathrm{mat}}$; they enter as boundary conditions, through the declared parameters, and through $\mathcal{H}$. The partner fields $\eta_{i\to j}$ of $O_6$ (item 9) are distinct objects from the global $\eta$ of $O_4$.
5. **Cone speed and trajectories.** Each aperture declares $c_*$ as the longitudinal sound speed of its tissue. Rays of that signal are null for the declared cone and, like all null rays, are not trajectories of $O_2$ Eq. [3]. Every material speed in $\mathcal{M}$ (diffusive speeds, twist-recovery speeds, blood velocities) is $\ll c_*$, so $u^2 \ll 1$. The trajectory equation is displayed in physical units: both $u^2 = g_{\mu\nu}v^\mu v^\nu/c_*^2$ and the projection term $c_*^{-2}(g_{\alpha\beta}a^\alpha_{\mathrm{d}}v^\beta)v^\mu$ carry $c_*$, and with $c_* = 1$ the display reduces to the r7 form. All three empirical apertures instantiate Eq. [3] with $a^\mu_{\mathrm{d}} = 0$ and map no tracer. Bodily movement, postural sway, and the motion of blood cells are forced motions that no declared drive describes in this epoch.
6. **Currents.** In Branch II, $J_E^\mu = J_W^\mu/p_0$ is fixed. The expansion current $J_{\mathrm{geom}}$ enters only through its divergence $\theta$ and its boundary flux $dV_{\mathcal{M}}/d\tau$ (`r7` $O_0$ branch scope), and no supply current is ever mapped to it. In general $\nabla_\mu J_E^\mu = -p_0^{-1}(\partial_\tau\mathcal{F} + \mathcal{F}\theta + \mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{F}}) \neq \theta$. Breathing volume, cardiac stroke volume, and the pulsation of vessels are cyclic volume changes outside the declared fields and are never mapped to $\theta$.
7. **Measured dissipation.** The Branch II evidence requires $\lim\langle\Theta_E\rangle > 0$ to be estimated from measured dissipation, never from $\mathfrak{K}\nabla\mathcal{F}$. Integrating $-J_E^\mu\nabla_\mu\mathcal{F}$ by parts and using the $O_4$ balance law gives the identity
   $$p_0\dot{W}_{\mathrm{diss}} = \oint_{\partial\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{F}\,(-J_W^\mu)\,d\Sigma_\mu - \int_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{F}\,\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{F}}\sqrt{\vert g\vert}\,d^nx - \frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{d\tau}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{F}^2\sqrt{\vert g\vert}\,d^nx - \frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{F}^2\theta\sqrt{\vert g\vert}\,d^nx$$
   * Where an outflow face is held at the datum $\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{ref}} = 0$ (4.1's receiving face, 4.4's venular boundary), it contributes nothing. The outflow face of 4.2 (the base of the stratum corneum) is not at the datum, and its term is evaluated from the measured outflow and the measured $\mathcal{F}$ there; the terms of its duct walls are bounded, not measured (4.2 §1).
   * Each term is evaluated from measured $\mathcal{F}$, measured boundary fluxes, and measured or independently declared consumption; none uses the declared $\mathfrak{K}$. Each §1 names instruments and surfaces different from those of the $O_1$ flux-prediction test, so that the evidence is independent of that test; 4.2 states where this independence is weaker.
   * Because the identity uses the balance law, the estimate is conditional on it. Each §1 also names a directly measured physical dissipation whose sign the estimate must share. Every such dissipation is non-negative by the second law, so this comparison tests only the sign of the identity-based estimate, and it is stated as such. Only signs and $s_0$-independent rates of $\Theta_E$ are tested.
8. **Gate sector ($O_4$).** $\rho_{\mathrm{total}}(\tau)$ is a declared positive trace-class state on $L^2(\mathcal{M}, d\mathrm{vol}_g)$, and $Q$, $P$, and the $O_6$ marginals $Q_i$ and joints $Q_{ij}$ are densities of this declared state sector. No Tier 4 aperture identifies them with $\mathcal{F}$ or with any measured field in this epoch, and none makes an empirical claim about the gate (`empirical_validation_protocol.md` §3.3). What each domain's physics supplies is, first, the spectral setting: the Neumann eigenmodes of $-\nabla^2_g$ on $\mathcal{M}$, whose eigenvalues are fixed on rigid domains, drift by a fraction of the order of the shear strain under the torsional perturbation of 4.1, and converge as $g \to G_\infty$. Second, it supplies a physical low-pass behaviour of $\mathcal{F}$: under the balance law, fine spatial structure in $\mathcal{F}$ relaxes faster than coarse structure. That behaviour is mapped via Spine $\mathcal{T}$ to the gate and is not itself the gate. Temporal smoothing of fluctuating supply by the tissue is a property in time and is not the spatial gate. Attentional filtering is not a property of these domains; it is mapped homologically in 4.3 and never identified with the gate of an empirical aperture.
9. **Cover, overlaps, and coupling ($O_5$, $O_6$).**
   * The cover $\{\mathcal{U}_i\}$ consists of $\tau$-independent material subregions of $\mathcal{M}$ with Lipschitz boundaries, and every overlap $\mathcal{U}_{ij}$ is convex at $\tau_0$. Where $\partial\mathcal{M}$ has concave parts (the undulating dermal–epidermal junction and the duct walls in 4.2; the walls of the excised vessels in 4.4), each concave part lies in a single cover set, which need not be convex, and every overlap stays clear of the concave parts: r7 requires convex overlaps, not convex cover sets. Each $g^{(i)}$ is the restriction of the one physical metric, so $g^{(i)} = g^{(j)}$ on every overlap, the geometric mean returns the common metric ($g \# g = g$), and the gluing of $O_5$ Eq. [3] holds identically.
   * The anisotropy of a tissue lives in $\mathfrak{K}$, never in $g$. Effective-medium averages of diffusivities or conductances, including geometric-mean averages, are statements about $\mathfrak{K}$ and are not mapped to $O_5$ Eq. [1].
   * *Partner fields.* In every empirical aperture the partner fields are the rate of change of the free-energy flux read in the two orientations, $\eta_{i\to j}^\lambda = \partial_\tau J_W^\lambda$ and $\eta_{j\to i}^\lambda = -\partial_\tau J_W^\lambda$, defined on all of $\mathcal{M}$, so $\eta_{[i\to j]}^\lambda = \partial_\tau J_W^\lambda$. They decay with the transient, which gives the integrable coupling that Branch II requires; a steady flux would give $\eta \equiv 0$, and a sustained coupling field is never declared.
   * *Zero sets.* $\partial_\tau J_W$ is not one-signed in general. Where the supply boundary holds a value of $\mathcal{F}$ and an outflow boundary holds the datum, the flux near the supply decays after the step from an initial overshoot, so there $\partial_\tau J_W$ points toward the supply, while farther downstream the flux rises toward its new steady value, so there it points away from the supply. Between the two lies the zero set of $\partial_\tau J_W$. It travels during the early window, possibly overshooting its late position, and in the late single-mode regime it settles on a fixed surface. In a laterally uniform slab, a precondition that 4.1 declares for this purpose, that surface is the level at which the slowest relaxation mode of the linearized problem peaks; without lateral uniformity (4.2, with its ducts and undulating junction; 4.4, with its excised vessels) it is located only by the predicted solution. On the zero set $\eta_{[i\to j]} = 0$ and $J_{[i\to j]} = 0$, so a construction that included it would itself decide the non-degeneracy hypothesis there.
   * Each aperture therefore declares overlaps $\bar{\mathcal{U}}_{ij}$ that the zero set, predicted before the window from the independently estimated parameters, does not reach during the window. Before the transient arrives, the partner field on a downstream overlap is non-zero but exponentially small, so the check is made against a pre-declared detection threshold: each overlap's window starts once the predicted $\vert\eta_{[i\to j]}\vert$ exceeds that threshold on $\bar{\mathcal{U}}_{ij}$, and the data must then show $\vert\eta_{[i\to j]}\vert$ above it there (a declared, checkable precondition). On such an overlap $\eta_{[i\to j]}$ keeps one direction throughout the overlap's window, which each aperture states from the predicted solution.
   * *Precision potentials.* The potentials $\Phi^{(i)}$ are declared before data collection as quadratic potentials (in a Cartesian chart, or in the Cartesian material chart for 4.1, with Hessians computed with $g(\tau)$ under the torsional perturbation). Their trace-free parts $\hat{\Pi}^{(i)}$ cannot be positive definite, because their eigenvalues sum to zero, so $J_{[i\to j]} = \frac{1}{2}(\hat{\Pi}^{(i)} + \hat{\Pi}^{(j)})\eta_{[i\to j]}$ carries no sign relation to $\eta_{[i\to j]}$. Tier 4 declares $\frac{1}{2}(\hat{\Pi}^{(i)} + \hat{\Pi}^{(j)})$ non-singular, so that $J_{[i\to j]} \neq 0$ wherever $\eta_{[i\to j]} \neq 0$. What is claimed for $J_{[i\to j]}$ is its oddness under $i \leftrightarrow j$ and its non-vanishing on the declared overlaps, not a direction.
   * Tier 4 also declares the two trace-free Hessians **distinct**, $\hat{\Pi}^{(i)} \neq \hat{\Pi}^{(j)}$. With antisymmetric partner fields, $J_{i\to j} + J_{j\to i} = (\hat{\Pi}^{(j)} - \hat{\Pi}^{(i)})\eta_{[i\to j]}$. Equal Hessians would therefore remove the gradient term of `r7` $O_6$ Eq. [3] and leave $M^{(ij)}_{\mu\nu} = \gamma_M\big((J_{[i\to j]})_\mu(J_{[i\to j]})_\nu - \frac{1}{n}g_{\mu\nu}J_{[i\to j]}^\alpha(J_{[i\to j]})_\alpha\big)$, the trace-free part of a rank-one tensor, which is non-zero wherever $J_{[i\to j]} \neq 0$: the hypothesis would hold by construction. With distinct Hessians the gradient term depends on $\nabla\eta_{[i\to j]}$ and can cancel the quadratic term (`r10` Decision 4), so the construction neither forces nor forbids $M^{(ij)} \neq 0$, which remains a standing hypothesis.
   * On a connected domain the declared transient reaches every overlap for $\tau > \tau_0$, so every edge of $\mathcal{K}_0$ lies in $\mathcal{K}$ and $\phi_{\mathrm{perc}} = 1 > \phi_c$; empirically, an edge is counted when its partner field exceeds a pre-declared detection threshold on its overlap. The percolation condition therefore carries no separate empirical content on these domains.
   * $M^{(ij)} \neq 0$ and $\frac{d}{d\tau}\mathcal{I}(Q_i; Q_j) > 0$ are standing hypotheses of `r7` $O_6$, not consequences of diffusion or perfusion. In Branch II the transient decays as the domain settles, so each coupling-tensor boundary flux decays and is integrable on $[\tau_0, \infty)$; no sustained coupling is asserted. No Tier 4 aperture is among the systems of the current coupling test.
   * *Topology.* The explant of 4.1 is declared simply connected, so $H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathcal{M}) = 0$ and $\mathbf{J}_{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{harm}} \equiv 0$. The epidermis of 4.2 is pierced by ducts (and, at hair-bearing sites, by follicles), and the capillary bed of 4.4 by the excised vessel segments that pass through it, so their first Betti numbers $b_1(\mathcal{M})$ are in general positive and the spaces of harmonic Neumann fields $\mathbf{J}_{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{harm}}$ of `r7` $O_5$ Eq. [5] are non-trivial: a property of each domain's topology. Tier 4 identifies no transport current or measured field with them and makes no claim about them.
10. **Gauge sector and hysteresis ($O_7$, $O_0$).** Tier 4 declares the trivial gauge sector: $A_\mu \equiv 0$ and a uniform unit section $\hat{u}$. Then $F_{\mu\nu}^{\mathrm{YM}} \equiv 0$, $[F_{\mu\nu}^{\mathrm{YM}}, \hat{\chi}] = 0$ identically, and $\hat{\mathcal{E}}_\mu = (\partial_\mu\chi_E)\hat{u}$, which tends to $0$ in $C^1$ when $\sigma \to 0$ and $\theta \to 0$ in $C^2$ with $\liminf\langle\Theta_E\rangle > 0$. The gauge limit therefore carries no content beyond the shear and expansion limits, and $O_7$ gauge transport is untested in this epoch. In particular, no Tier 4 aperture makes a synchronization, alignment, or coherence claim (of tissues, of bodily rhythms, or of persons): such claims would belong to gauge transport (`aperture_generation_guidance.md` §5.5), not to $\chi_E$. The hysteresis record obeys the `r7` law $\partial_\tau\mathcal{H} = J_E^\mu\nabla_\mu q$ from a declared initial record $\mathcal{H}_0$, chosen convex because $O_7$ Eq. [2] requires $\nabla_\alpha\nabla_\beta\mathcal{H} \succ 0$ at every $\tau$. $\mathcal{H}$ then evolves through a state sector that is declared but not specified, so the declarations do not establish convexity at later $\tau$. Tier 4 therefore states it as a declared condition, with a sufficient form: the largest eigenvalue magnitude of $\nabla_\alpha\nabla_\beta(\mathcal{H} - \mathcal{H}_0)$ (with indices raised by $g$) stays below the smallest eigenvalue of $\nabla_\alpha\nabla_\beta\mathcal{H}_0$ on $\mathcal{M}$ for all $\tau \ge \tau_0$. Both Hessians are covariant Hessians of $g(\tau)$, evaluated at each $\tau$, which matters for 4.1 under the torsional perturbation. Then $\nabla_\alpha\nabla_\beta\mathcal{H} \succ 0$ persists. The alternative, $\nabla q \equiv 0$, would keep $\mathcal{H}$ static and convex, but would make the $O_\Omega$ generator condition hold trivially, so it is not adopted. The memory variables of each tissue (the loading and remodelling history of cartilage, epidermal thickening under habitual load, vascular adaptation) are mapped via Spine $\mathcal{T}$ to $\mathcal{H}$ and are not identified with it. $\hat{\rho}_g \to \frac{1}{n}\delta^\mu_\nu$ follows from $\hat{h} \to \hat{h}_\infty$, and the alignment $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\hat{\rho}_g \parallel \hat{\rho}_{\mathcal{H}}) \to 0$ then requires the Hessian of the record to become isotropic: an asymptotic condition that each aperture states and does not test.
11. **Finite windows and coverage.** No finite window exhibits a $\tau \to \infty$ limit. Each empirical aperture's claims are the within-window tests of `aperture_generation_guidance.md` §5.5 that its domain supports, and each $O_\Omega$ block lists them. In this epoch Aperture 4.1 is exercised by the shear-relaxation family, and 4.2 and 4.4 by no test (`empirical_validation_protocol.md` §3.3). No human-subject test is included, so the in-vivo forms that 4.2 and 4.4 admit are declared but not exercised. Everything else is stated as untested, not as confirmed.
12. **The provisionally homological aperture (4.3).** Aperture 4.3 maps neuro-attentional and autonomic regulation onto all nine coordinates as conceptual correspondences, like the Tier 1 grammars (`aperture_generation_guidance.md` §2.3). It declares no domain, metric, storage, free energy, transport tensor, sink, drive, or Branch II evidence, and makes no empirical claim. The bioelectric potentials of neural tissue are quasi-static: in volume conduction the potential follows its sources without a storage term, so r7's storage–diffusion balance law cannot represent it literally. For uniformity of the tier, each 4.3 block carries the same r7 spine-equation line as the empirical blocks, and its correspondences are read against the Branch II form, because the regulated systems it describes are bounded (a homological aperture may choose a branch form with a stated reason, `aperture_generation_guidance.md` §2.3); as a homology it declares no branch in the sense of closure (v), because nothing is evaluated. The shared line is read schematically: 4.3 declares no cone speed, so the r7 form with $c_* = 1$ applies to its trajectory equation, and the entry $J_E^\mu = J_W^\mu/p_0$ is the Branch II form being read, not a declared current. The same reading applies to any later homological aperture written on this pattern. Its correspondences are mapped via Spine $\mathcal{T}$ and are never reductive identities; none is evidence for a spine law, and none is used by an empirical aperture. Coordinates that hold only trivially or by construction in the empirical apertures carry no content in 4.3 either.

## Aperture 4.1: Myofascial Biotensegrity & Postural Mechanics ($\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{somatic}}$)

### 1. Epistemological & Physiological Scope
Aperture 4.1 maps the mechanics of the body's load-bearing tissues onto Spine $\mathcal{T}$: the tensioned myofascial continuum in which compression-bearing skeletal elements are suspended (biotensegrity), the joints and their articular cartilage through which posture transmits load, and the adaptation of these tissues to their loading history. Its empirical domain is the nutrition of avascular load-bearing tissue: oxygen supplied across a boundary diffuses through articular cartilage, which has no blood supply of its own, and is consumed by its sparse cells, the chondrocytes. Lexicon is strictly restricted to biomechanics, cartilage and connective-tissue physiology, mechanobiology, myofascial anatomy, and kinesiology.

**Declaration (`aperture_generation_guidance.md` §2.3; Branch II).**

* **Domain $\mathcal{M}$ ($n = 3$) and boundary $\partial\mathcal{M}$.** A cylindrical explant of articular cartilage, cut parallel to the articular surface from a single zone of the tissue so that its water volume fraction is uniform within tolerance (§0.1 item 3), and mounted in a two-chamber diffusion cell with its lateral surface sealed. $\partial\mathcal{M}$ consists of:
  * the articular-side face (supply), bathed by a well-stirred supply chamber whose medium stands in for synovial fluid, at a held oxygen concentration;
  * the deep-side face (outflow at the datum), bathed by a receiving chamber perfused with deoxygenated medium, so that its oxygen is held at the datum within tolerance;
  * the sealed lateral surface, closed to oxygen.

  All supply crosses the articular-side face; cartilage contains no vessels and no oxygen-producing process, so there are no interior sources. The explant is declared, rather than the joint in situ, because in situ the deep boundary of adult cartilage is nearly closed: the oxygen field would then have a zero-flux face, on which $\nabla\mathcal{F} = 0$, and no measurable outflow. The other zones of the cartilage, the subchondral bone, and the joint lie outside $\mathcal{M}$; mechanical loading enters as a boundary condition (a held axial grip load) and through $\mathcal{H}$.
* **Intervertebral disc (not declared in this epoch).** The disc, the other avascular load-bearing tissue of the body, is not a declared domain: in situ the disc is supplied from both endplates and from its periphery, so its nutrient field has an interior minimum at which $\nabla\mathcal{F} = 0$, and its nucleus, annulus, and cartilage endplates are distinct storage classes (§0.1 item 3).
* **Window.** A relaxation window (§0.1 item 1), opened from a prior steady state by a declared step increase of the supply chamber's oxygen tension, after which it is held within tolerance, as are the receiving-chamber datum, temperature, osmolarity, and the axial grip load. The grip load is applied before the prior steady state, and the explant's consolidation under it is complete within tolerance before $\tau_0$. An oxygen step is osmotically negligible, so the explant's volume stays within tolerance. A glucose window is not declared: a step of glucose changes the bath's osmolarity, and a rise of osmolarity deswells cartilage ($\theta < 0$).
  * *Optional torsional perturbation.* The explant, gripped between porous platens, may be twisted about its axis and released at a declared time $\tau_{\mathrm{r}}$ within such a window. The recovery of the twist then supplies a shear transient ($O_3$), while oxygen diffusion and uptake continue to supply the throughput. Releasing the twist late in the window, once the measured dissipation is constant within tolerance, lets the $\chi_E$ statement of $O_7$ be evaluated.
* **Metric.** Flat and volume-rigid (§0.1 item 2): the explant's thickness, measured by ultrasound or laser displacement, stays within a declared tolerance, so $\theta \equiv 0$. Without the torsional perturbation the shear strain also stays within tolerance, and $g_{\mu\nu} = \delta_{\mu\nu}$ in a Cartesian material chart. With it, $g(\tau) = \mathbf{F}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{F}$ is the pull-back under the twist, with $\det\mathbf{F} = 1$. For a twist angle $\Phi_{\mathrm{tw}}(z, \tau)$ about the axis, the deformation $(r, \varphi, z) \mapsto (r, \varphi + \Phi_{\mathrm{tw}}, z)$ is isochoric and maps each cross-section and the lateral cylinder onto themselves.
* **Cone speed.** $c_*$ is the longitudinal sound speed of cartilage. Diffusive speeds and twist-recovery speeds are $\ll c_*$.
* **Storage.** $s = \phi_{\mathrm{w}}\kappa_{\mathrm{O}}$: the explant's water volume fraction times the partition coefficient of oxygen between its interstitial water and bath water. It is declared uniform over $\mathcal{M}$ within tolerance (§0.1 item 3). In intact cartilage the water fraction falls with depth from the articular surface; that is why the explant is cut from a single zone.
* **Free energy $\mathcal{F}$ and datum.** $\mathcal{F} = c\,\epsilon_{\mathrm{O}}$ (J m⁻³), with $c$ the bath-equivalent oxygen concentration (oxygen tension times its solubility in bath water, mol m⁻³), continuous across the explant's faces, and $\epsilon_{\mathrm{O}} > 0$ the declared standard-state free-energy yield per mole of oxygen reduced in the oxidation of glucose (J mol⁻¹), from tabulated standards. This is the declared linear form of `aperture_generation_guidance.md` §2.3 item 3. The datum is zero ($\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{ref}} = 0$), held at the deep-side face, and $\mathcal{F} \ge 0$ holds trivially. The oxygen stored per unit volume is $s\,c$, so $\mathcal{F}$ is that stored oxygen times the declared conversion factor $\epsilon_{\mathrm{O}}/s$. The relaxation time $\ell^2/D$ across the explant's thickness $\ell$ is finite and resolvable.
* **Transport $\mathfrak{K}$.** $\mathfrak{K}^{\alpha\beta} = D^{\alpha\beta}$, the effective oxygen diffusivity tensor of the explant (its oxygen conductance over the storage coefficient $s$), parametric and depth-dependent: transversely isotropic about the face normal, with a declared linear dependence on depth across the explant, and the uniform isotropic diffusivity as the default. With $j$ the molar oxygen flux per unit bulk area, $J_W = \epsilon_{\mathrm{O}}j/s$; the conversion factor $\epsilon_{\mathrm{O}}/s$ is declared, with $\phi_{\mathrm{w}}$ measured on the explant itself after the window (wet and dry weights) and $\kappa_{\mathrm{O}}$ from an oxygen equilibration of the explant.
* **Sink $\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{F}}$.** Chondrocyte oxygen consumption with Michaelis–Menten kinetics, $\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{F}} = V_{\mathrm{m}}\mathcal{F}/(K_{\mathcal{F}} + \mathcal{F})$, with $K_{\mathcal{F}} = K_{\mathrm{m}}\epsilon_{\mathrm{O}}$ and $V_{\mathrm{m}}$ in the storage-normalized units of the declared law. $\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{F}}$ is continuous, non-negative, non-decreasing in $\mathcal{F}$, zero at the datum, and spread over the explant. $V_{\mathrm{m}}$ and $K_{\mathrm{m}}$ are estimated per closure (ii) from the same explant: from its oxygen uptake in a sealed respirometry chamber after the window (a different modality), or from the difference of supply and receiving fluxes at several held supply levels before the window (a declared perturbation regime). Cell density is declared uniform within tolerance.
* **Drive.** Default, $a^\mu_{\mathrm{d}} = 0$ (§0.1 item 5).
* **Growth or remodelling material metric (not declared in this epoch).** The master table admits an optional growth or remodelling material metric for this aperture. It has been weighed against the reasons for which 3.1 declined one, and it is not declared, for five reasons.
  1. Within a fixed window remodelling is excluded (the turnover of the collagen network takes years), so a remodelling metric would be $\tau$-independent: $\theta = \sigma = 0$ in it. The imposed twist, a viscoelastic deformation, would not appear in it, so the shear-relaxation test of this aperture (Annex B Test 1) requires the physical metric.
  2. r7 ties curvature to the control field. With $G_{\mu\nu} = \kappa_GT^{\mathrm{mat}}_{\mu\nu}$ and $R = \kappa_G\nabla_\alpha\xi^\alpha$ ($O_2$ Eq. [0]), $T^{\mathrm{mat}}_{\mu\nu} = \nabla_{(\mu}\xi_{\nu)} - \frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}\nabla_\alpha\xi^\alpha$ gives $R_{\mu\nu} = \kappa_G\nabla_{(\mu}\xi_{\nu)}$, with $\xi_\nu = \hat{\Pi}_{\nu\lambda}\eta^\lambda$ ($O_4$ Eq. [2]). No connective-tissue field is identified as the global control field $\eta$; myofascial tension and the swelling prestress are stresses, not control fields. A $\xi$ constructed from a declared material metric so as to satisfy this equation would satisfy the stress sector by construction.
  3. The incompatibility of cartilage's natural (stress-free) metric shows as residual strain, which is measured by cutting strips and observing their curl, a destructive method. Osmotic unloading in a hypertonic bath estimates it without cutting, but it changes the tissue's swelling state and would itself need validation as an estimator.
  4. That incompatibility arises mainly because swelling varies with depth across the zones. The declared single-zone explant has uniform composition within tolerance, so on this domain a declared material metric would be flat to the order of the composition tolerance: the single-storage-class restriction and a non-trivial material curvature pull in opposite directions.
  5. r7 uses one metric $g$ in every coordinate. Oxygen diffuses over physical distances, so a non-Euclidean material metric in the $O_4$ Laplacian, the Neumann spectrum, and the flux test would misstate the physical transport law unless $\mathfrak{K}$ were re-declared to compensate.

  A future declaration would need a material metric whose incompatibility is estimated by an independent, non-destructive modality on a domain where it is not flat to tolerance; an identified control field for which $R_{\mu\nu} = \kappa_G\nabla_{(\mu}\xi_{\nu)}$ is a tested hypothesis rather than a construction; and a statement, coordinate by coordinate, of which metric is used. Until then the stress sector of this aperture is that of §0.1 item 4, and the flat stress-sector reduction applies to it.
* **Branch II evidence (closure (v)).**
  * The trend of $\langle\theta\rangle$ and a bound on $\int\langle\theta\rangle\,d\tau$ come from the measured explant thickness, which must stay within the volume tolerance; for this volume-rigid domain the evidence is that tolerance check.
  * $\lim\langle\Theta_E\rangle > 0$ is estimated from measured dissipation (§0.1 item 7):
    * the supply term uses the oxygen balance of the supply chamber (the oxygen delivered to it minus that leaving it, from in-line sensors on its gas or medium supply) and $\mathcal{F}$ at the articular-side face from the chamber's own sensor;
    * the deep-side face, at the datum, contributes nothing;
    * the consumption term uses the declared, independently estimated $V_{\mathrm{m}}$ and $K_{\mathrm{m}}$ with $\mathcal{F}$ from a second, independent profiling record;
    * the storage term uses repeated profiles.

    The $O_1$ test uses the deep-side face, a different surface, and its own instruments.
  * The physical dissipation is the entropy production of oxygen diffusion, which is non-negative by the second law, so the comparison tests only the sign of the estimate.
* **Admissible domains** (declared, checkable preconditions):
  1. a viable, avascular explant with no interior oxygen source, and $\nabla\mathcal{F} \neq 0$ throughout $\bar{\mathcal{M}}$;
  2. a single storage class: water volume fraction and oxygen partition coefficient uniform within tolerance, checked after the window by wet and dry weights of sub-slices from two or more depths; cell density uniform within tolerance, checked histologically after the window;
  3. supply-face concentration held at its new level and deep-side face at the datum within tolerance, with the resistances of the chambers' unstirred layers and of the porous platens negligible against the explant's, by a declared estimate (stirring calibration);
  4. lateral surface sealed, with oxygen leakage across it below the flux tolerance;
  5. volumetric strain within the volume tolerance, with chamber osmolarity and grip load held, and consolidation under the grip load complete before $\tau_0$. Without the torsional perturbation, shear strain is also within tolerance. With it, the twist is recoverable, with shear strain $\ll 1$, and $\partial_\tau\mathcal{F} \ge 0$ is checked from the oxygen record (§0.1 item 1);
  6. $V_{\mathrm{m}}$, $K_{\mathrm{m}}$ constant within tolerance: glucose supplied from both chambers at a held, non-limiting level, pH and temperature held, and no change in the cells' metabolic state beyond tolerance within the window;
  7. no mechanical loading within the window other than the held grip load and the optional twist, so that transport is purely diffusive (no load-driven convection of interstitial fluid);
  8. for the one-dimensional statements of $O_6$ only: a laterally uniform explant and supply.
* **Titled phenomena outside the domain.**
  * *Myofascial biotensegrity.* The tensioned continuum of fascia, muscle, and ligament in which skeletal elements are suspended is context. Its tensions set the loads that joints transmit; these enter the explant only through its loading history ($\mathcal{H}_0$) and the held grip load.
  * *Posture and joint loading.* Habitual postural loading shapes cartilage composition and thickness between windows and enters through the declared parameters and $\mathcal{H}$. Cyclic loading pumps interstitial fluid and adds convective transport; it is excluded within windows (precondition 7) and is context.
  * *Synovial fluid and lubrication.* The supply chamber stands in for synovial fluid at the articular boundary; lubrication and the joint's synovial lining are context.
  * *Remodelling.* Adaptation of connective tissues to load (as summarized in Davis's and Wolff's laws) lies between windows and is mapped via Spine $\mathcal{T}$ to $\mathcal{H}$, not identified with it.
  * *Proprioception.* Sensing of posture and joint position is context.

Openness is carried by throughput: oxygen diffusing down its gradient and being consumed produces entropy at every point, so $\Theta_E > 0$ (by $O_1$), $\Theta_{\mathrm{tot}} = \Theta_E$, and $\tau_\Theta$ counts entropy production per $s_0$, while $\tau$ remains laboratory proper time. Under it, in the Cartesian material chart $\hat{g} = \delta$, $\Omega \equiv 1$ because the twist is isochoric ($\det g = 1$), and $\exp\hat{h} = g(\tau)$, so $\hat{h}$ is the logarithm of the right Cauchy–Green tensor of the twist, trace-free. The split isolates the volume element only algebraically; $\theta$ remains coupled to $\xi_\alpha = \hat{\Pi}_{\alpha\lambda}\eta^\lambda$ and $\sigma_{\alpha\beta}$ through the traced elliptic $\tau$-compatibility equation (`r10` Decision 2). On this domain ($G_{\mu\nu} \equiv 0$, $\xi \equiv 0$, $\theta \equiv 0$; §0.1 items 2 and 4) it reduces to $\nabla^\alpha\nabla^\beta\sigma_{\alpha\beta} = 0$, and the full law to $\mathbb{L}_{\mathrm{Ein}}[\sigma] = 0$: the Saint-Venant compatibility of an isochoric strain rate, which the twist satisfies because its metric stays flat. The swelling prestress of cartilage, the tension of its collagen network, and the tensions of the myofascial continuum are Cauchy stresses, not $T_{\mu\nu}^{\mathrm{mat}}$; biotensegrity is a statement about how such stresses are distributed, and it is context, not $G_{\mu\nu} = \kappa_GT_{\mu\nu}^{\mathrm{mat}}$. #### $O_3$ — Shear Damping & Hysteresis Consolidation
* **Spine Equations:** $\left.\frac{\partial g_{\mu\nu}}{\partial\tau}\right\vert_{N=1,\,\beta^\mu=0} = \frac{2}{n}\theta g_{\mu\nu} + 2\sigma_{\mu\nu}$; $\nabla_\tau\sigma^\mu_{\;\nu} \equiv \partial_\tau\sigma^\mu_{\;\nu} = -\gamma_\sigma\sigma^\mu_{\;\nu}$ ($\gamma_\sigma = \mathrm{const} > 0$, $\sigma^\mu_{\;\mu} = 0$) $\implies \lim_{\tau\to\infty}\Vert\sigma^\mu_{\;\nu}\Vert_{C^2(\mathcal{M})} = 0$; $\hat{h}(\tau, \mathbf{x}) \xrightarrow{C^2(\mathcal{M})} \hat{h}_\infty(\mathbf{x})$, $(\hat{g}_\infty(\mathbf{x}))_{\mu\nu} \equiv \hat{g}_{\mu\alpha}(\mathbf{x})(\exp\hat{h}_\infty(\mathbf{x}))^\alpha_{\;\nu}$ ($\det\hat{g}_\infty = \det\hat{g} = 1$); $V_{\mathrm{local}}(\tau) \equiv \int_{\mathcal{U}_i}\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{x}, \tau)\sqrt{\vert g\vert}\,d^nx$.
* **Physical Instantiation:** Without the torsional perturbation, $\sigma^\mu_{\;\nu}$ stays within tolerance of $0$, the shear law holds trivially for any $\gamma_\sigma > 0$, $\hat{h} = \hat{h}_\infty = 0$, and the aperture supports no shear-relaxation test. The relaxation of the oxygen field is a property of the $O_4$ balance law and is not mapped to $\sigma$. Under the perturbation, $\sigma^\mu_{\;\nu} = \frac{1}{2}g^{\mu\alpha}\partial_\tau g_{\alpha\nu}$ is the isochoric strain rate of the recovering explant, trace-free because $\theta \equiv 0$. If the twist recovers as a single overdamped exponential at a constant rate, every component of $\sigma$ decays at that rate, and the mixed-form shear law holds to first order in the twist strain; the second-order terms fall within a declared residual. That rate is $\gamma_\sigma$. It is not predicted from independent parameters: it is estimated per recovery trajectory, and the test is whether one constant rate fits within each trajectory, with the pre-declared precondition that $\sigma$ does not change sign during the window (the mixed-form law forbids oscillation). Recovery better described by several rates or by a power law would falsify the law for this system; whether it does is the content of the test. This is the aperture's contribution to the shear-relaxation family (`empirical_validation_protocol.md` §3.3; Annex B Test 1, system 1). As the twist recovers, $\hat{h} \to \hat{h}_\infty$, the logarithmic strain of any residual twist, and $(\hat{g}_\infty)_{\mu\nu} = \delta_{\mu\alpha}(\exp\hat{h}_\infty)^\alpha_{\;\nu}$ has unit determinant. $V_{\mathrm{local}}$ is the hysteresis content of a cover set. Lasting responses of the tissue to loading (changes of composition or of collagen architecture) lie between windows and enter through $\mathcal{H}_0$.

## Aperture 4.2: Vestibular Equilibrium & Sensorimotor Contact ($\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{kinesthetic}}$)

### 1. Epistemological & Physiological Scope
Aperture 4.2 maps the physiology of balance and of the body's contact with its support onto Spine $\mathcal{T}$: vestibular sensing of head motion and gravity, the sensorimotor control that keeps the centre of mass over the base of support, cutaneous sensing of contact, and the load that balance places on the skin of the contact. Its empirical domain is the oxygen supply of the skin under that load: oxygen from the dermal capillaries diffuses across the dermal–epidermal junction into the avascular epidermis and is consumed by keratinocytes, while the support occludes the skin from the air. Lexicon is strictly restricted to vestibular physiology, posturography, sensorimotor control, cutaneous physiology, and contact mechanics.

**Declaration (`aperture_generation_guidance.md` §2.3; Branch II).**

* **Domain $\mathcal{M}$ ($n = 3$) and boundary $\partial\mathcal{M}$.** The viable epidermis (the basal, spinous, and granular layers) of a load-bearing skin site in contact with a support, over a declared contact area, in one of two forms:
  * *in vivo*, at a support contact under a held load;
  * *ex vivo*, an epidermal sheet separated from its dermis, whose basal face is bathed by a medium at held oxygen and whose outer face rests on the support under a held load.

  $\partial\mathcal{M}$ consists of:
  * the dermal–epidermal junction (supply), across which oxygen enters from the papillary capillaries (in vivo) or the bathing medium (ex vivo); it undulates around the dermal papillae;
  * the outer face of the viable epidermis, at the base of the stratum corneum (outflow), across which oxygen passes through the stratum corneum to the support;
  * the walls of the epidermal ducts of sweat glands (and, at hair-bearing sites, of follicles) that pierce the epidermis (outflow, see below);
  * lateral faces at the edge of the declared contact area, closed within tolerance (ex vivo by a seal; in vivo by lateral uniformity of the contact, precondition 3).

  The stratum corneum is a different storage class (its water fraction is far lower) and lies outside $\mathcal{M}$ as a thin barrier compartment. The support is **occlusive** and holds the skin-surface oxygen at the datum (for example a contact plate flushed with an oxygen-free gas). Occlusion removes the supply of oxygen from the air, so all supply crosses the dermal–epidermal junction; the datum at the surface gives the field an outflow, without which the occluded face would be a zero-flux face on which $\nabla\mathcal{F} = 0$. The dermis, its capillaries, and the support lie outside $\mathcal{M}$. The epidermis contains no capillaries and no oxygen-producing process, so there are no interior sources. Balance control and contact loading enter as boundary conditions and through $\mathcal{H}$ (below).
* **Duct walls.** The duct lumens lie outside $\mathcal{M}$. With no sweat secretion within the window (precondition 5), the lumen fluid is static and receives oxygen only from the surrounding epidermis, and it drains toward the surface at the datum; exchange across a duct wall is therefore one-signed, out of $\mathcal{M}$, and never a source. These fluxes are not measured. Their total is bounded by a declared estimate (duct density, lumen cross-section, and the lumen's diffusive conductance to the surface) and must lie below the flux tolerance.
* **Window.** A relaxation window (§0.1 item 1), opened from a prior steady state by a declared increase of the oxygen supplied at the dermal–epidermal junction, after which the supply data, the contact load, and skin temperature are held within tolerance. The contact load is applied before the prior steady state, and the skin's creep under it is complete within tolerance before $\tau_0$ (precondition 6). Ex vivo the increase is a step of the bathing medium's oxygen. In vivo it is a monotone rise of capillary oxygen, for example after a step increase of the inspired oxygen fraction, which reaches the junction as a ramp (§0.1 item 1); windows in which the junction data overshoot (for example through hyperoxic vasoconstriction) are excluded. Windows that open with a change of contact load are excluded: loading compresses the epidermis ($\theta < 0$) and lowers the supply by compressing the capillaries, and unloading is followed by reactive hyperaemia, whose overshoot makes the supply data non-monotone.
* **Metric.** Flat and rigid (§0.1 item 2): $g_{\mu\nu} = \delta_{\mu\nu}$ in a Cartesian chart, with the epidermal thickness under the held load, measured by optical coherence tomography or high-frequency ultrasound, within the rigid tolerance.
* **Cone speed.** $c_*$ is the longitudinal sound speed of skin. Diffusive speeds are $\ll c_*$.
* **Storage.** $s = \phi_{\mathrm{w}}\kappa_{\mathrm{O}}$: the viable epidermis's water volume fraction times the partition coefficient of oxygen between the tissue and water (which includes oxygen dissolved in cell lipids). It is declared uniform over $\mathcal{M}$ within tolerance (§0.1 item 3). The water fraction is roughly constant through the viable layers and falls steeply in the stratum corneum, which is why the stratum corneum lies outside $\mathcal{M}$.
* **Free energy $\mathcal{F}$ and datum.** $\mathcal{F} = c\,\epsilon_{\mathrm{O}}$ (J m⁻³), with $c$ the bath-equivalent oxygen concentration and $\epsilon_{\mathrm{O}}$ the declared standard-state yield per mole of oxygen, as in 4.1. The datum is zero ($\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{ref}} = 0$), held at the skin surface, and $\mathcal{F} \ge 0$ holds trivially. The relaxation time $\ell^2/D$ across the viable epidermis is finite and resolvable.
* **Transport $\mathfrak{K}$.** $\mathfrak{K}^{\alpha\beta} = D^{\alpha\beta}$, the layered effective oxygen diffusivity tensor of the viable epidermis (its conductance over $s$): transversely isotropic about the local normal to the skin surface, with declared values in the basal, spinous, and granular layers joined smoothly across declared transition zones, so $\mathfrak{K} \in C^1$. A uniform isotropic diffusivity is the default. With $j$ the molar flux per unit area, $J_W = \epsilon_{\mathrm{O}}j/s$; the conversion factor $\epsilon_{\mathrm{O}}/s$ is declared, with $\phi_{\mathrm{w}}$ from a confocal Raman water profile of the same site (non-invasive) and $\kappa_{\mathrm{O}}$ from tabulated or separately measured partition data.
* **Outflow boundary.** The stratum corneum is represented as a thin barrier of declared oxygen conductance $h_{\mathrm{SC}}$ between the outer face of $\mathcal{M}$ and the surface held at the datum: the outflow obeys the Robin condition $-J_W\cdot\hat{n} = (h_{\mathrm{SC}}/s)\,\mathcal{F}$ there, with the barrier's own storage neglected (precondition 4). $h_{\mathrm{SC}}$ is estimated independently, from the surface oxygen flux and the oxygen beneath the stratum corneum in a declared perturbation regime before the window.
* **Sink $\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{F}}$.** Keratinocyte oxygen consumption with Michaelis–Menten kinetics, $\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{F}} = V_{\mathrm{m}}\mathcal{F}/(K_{\mathcal{F}} + \mathcal{F})$, with $K_{\mathcal{F}} = K_{\mathrm{m}}\epsilon_{\mathrm{O}}$, continuous, non-negative, non-decreasing in $\mathcal{F}$, and zero at the datum. $V_{\mathrm{m}}$ and $K_{\mathrm{m}}$ are estimated per closure (ii): ex vivo from respirometry of the same sheet after the window; in vivo from the response of the surface oxygen flux of the same site to declared steps of the support's oxygen level before the window (a declared perturbation regime).
* **Drive.** Default, $a^\mu_{\mathrm{d}} = 0$ (§0.1 item 5). Postural sway and balance corrections move the body, not material of $\mathcal{M}$ along trajectories; no balance-control drive is declared in this epoch.
* **Branch II evidence (closure (v)).**
  * The trend of $\langle\theta\rangle$ and a bound on $\int\langle\theta\rangle\,d\tau$ come from the measured epidermal thickness, which must stay within the rigid tolerance.
  * $\lim\langle\Theta_E\rangle > 0$ is estimated from measured dissipation (§0.1 item 7):
    * the outflow-face term uses the oxygen taken up by the support (from a second, independent sensor set on its flushing gas), which equals the outflow across the outer face of $\mathcal{M}$ within the barrier-storage bound, with $\mathcal{F}$ there from an optical oxygen measurement beneath the stratum corneum;
    * the supply term uses the junction inflow: ex vivo from the depletion of the bathing medium; in vivo from the oxygen balance of $\mathcal{M}$ (outflow plus declared consumption plus storage change), with $\mathcal{F}$ at the junction from an optical measurement at its depth;
    * the duct-wall terms are bounded by the declared estimate, not measured;
    * the consumption term uses the declared, independently estimated $V_{\mathrm{m}}$ and $K_{\mathrm{m}}$ with $\mathcal{F}$ from a second profiling record, and the storage term uses repeated profiles.
  * *Weaker independence.* The $O_1$ test uses the surface flux into the support. Ex vivo the evidence's supply term uses a different surface (the bathing medium). In vivo the evidence uses the same surface and quantity, measured by a second, independent sensor set: it is independent of the $O_1$ instruments but not of the measured quantity, as in 3.3, and the duct terms are bounded rather than measured.
  * The physical dissipation is the entropy production of oxygen diffusion, which is non-negative by the second law, so the comparison tests only the sign of the estimate.
* **Admissible domains** (declared, checkable preconditions):
  1. viable, avascular epidermis with no interior oxygen source, and $\nabla\mathcal{F} \neq 0$ throughout $\bar{\mathcal{M}}$;
  2. a single storage class: the water fraction of the viable epidermis uniform within tolerance, checked by its confocal Raman profile at two or more depths;

  6. epidermal thickness within the rigid tolerance, with creep of the skin under the held contact load complete within tolerance before $\tau_0$ (checked from the thickness record before the window), so that no thinning ($\theta < 0$) continues into it;
  7. $V_{\mathrm{m}}$, $K_{\mathrm{m}}$ constant within tolerance: isothermal conditions and no change of the epidermis's metabolic state within the window.
* **Titled phenomena outside the domain.**
  * *Vestibular equilibrium.* The vestibular organs, their sensing of head acceleration and gravity, and the reflexes they drive lie outside $\mathcal{M}$ and are context.
  * *Balance control and postural sway.* Sensorimotor control keeps the centre of mass over the base of support by shifting the centre of pressure. * **Homological Mapping:** Directed influence between brain regions (effective connectivity, in which one region's activity shapes another's more than the reverse) corresponds to the odd phase-lead 1-form $J_{[i\to j]} = -J_{[j\to i]}$, which reverses sign when the pair is read the other way. Temporal lead–lag statistics between scalar recordings are not spatial 1-form fields, and the correspondence implies no estimator. The paired antagonism of the sympathetic and parasympathetic branches, which act in opposite senses on the same organs, corresponds to the opposite-signed extreme eigenvalues $m_+ > 0 > m_-$ of the trace-free operator $M^{(ij)}$ under the non-degeneracy hypothesis, with $m_+ \neq -m_-$ in general, as autonomic balance is not a symmetric see-saw. The growth of shared information between coupled regions corresponds to the flux-positivity hypothesis $\frac{d}{d\tau}\mathcal{I}(Q_i; Q_j) > 0$. Both remain standing hypotheses of r7, and in the Branch II form the coupling must be transient (integrable), so the correspondence is with episodes of coupling that resolve, not with sustained coupling.

* **Spine Equations:** $D_\mu = \partial_\mu - i g_{\mathrm{YM}}\,\mathrm{ad}_{A_\mu}$; $\Vert\hat{\mathcal{E}}_\mu\Vert_{C^1(\mathcal{M})} \to 0 \implies [F_{\mu\nu}^{\mathrm{YM}}, \hat{\chi}] \to 0$; $\chi_E \equiv 1 + \frac{n\,\sigma^\mu_{\;\nu}\sigma^\nu_{\;\mu}}{\theta^2 + \langle\Theta_E\rangle_{\mathcal{M}}^2} \to 1$, $\hat{\chi} \equiv \chi_E\hat{u}$ (Branch II); $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\hat{\rho}_g(\tau) \parallel \hat{\rho}_{\mathcal{H}}(\tau)) \to 0$.

* **Spine Equations:** Branch II ($H_0 = 0$): $\theta \xrightarrow[\tau\to\infty]{C^2(\mathcal{M})} 0$ with $\int_{\tau_0}^{\infty}\langle\theta\rangle_{\mathcal{M}}\,d\tau < \infty$ and $\langle\Theta_E\rangle_{\mathcal{M}} \to \Theta_\infty > 0$; $\sigma^\mu_{\;\nu} \to 0$; $\partial_\tau g_{\mu\nu} \to 0$; $V_{\mathcal{M}} \to V_\infty$ and $\mathcal{I}(\tau) \to s_0V_\infty$; $\left.\hat{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathrm{stat}}(\mathbf{x})\Psi[g_{\mu\nu}, \mathcal{H}]\right\vert_{\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{sol}}[g]} = (J_E^\lambda\nabla_\lambda q)\frac{\delta\Psi}{\delta\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{x})} \xrightarrow[\tau\to\infty]{} 0 \iff J_E^\lambda\nabla_\lambda q \to 0 \iff \frac{\partial\mathcal{H}}{\partial\tau} \to 0$ (with $\vert\delta\Psi/\delta\mathcal{H}\vert$ bounded and bounded away from $0$ uniformly as $\tau \to \infty$).
* **Homological Mapping:** Homeostatic and allostatic regulation hold a nervous system and its body in a bounded, sustained state of activity: never static equilibrium, which for living tissue is death, and never a strict periodic return, although rhythms of sleep and waking recur. ## Aperture 4.4: Affective Regulation & Psychophysiological Resilience ($\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{affective}}$)

### 1. Epistemological & Physiological Scope
Aperture 4.4 maps the bodily side of affect onto Spine $\mathcal{T}$: the autonomic expression of emotion and arousal, its regulation, and the capacity of physiological systems to recover after stressors (psychophysiological resilience). A principal route by which affective and autonomic states act on tissues is the tone of small arteries and arterioles, which sets how blood is distributed through the microcirculation. Its empirical domain is the perfusion of a capillary bed: blood enters from the feeding arterioles, spreads through the capillary network, which stores blood in its compliance, and leaves through the draining venules, while part of the plasma filters into the tissue. Lexicon is strictly restricted to affective neuroscience, autonomic psychophysiology, and microvascular physiology.

**Declaration (`aperture_generation_guidance.md` §2.3; Branch II).**

* **Domain $\mathcal{M}$ ($n = 3$) and boundary $\partial\mathcal{M}$.** A tissue region containing a capillary bed, treated at the network scale as a porous medium, with the lumens and walls of the feeding arterioles and draining venules that run through it excised from $\mathcal{M}$, together with any larger vessels. The capillaries and the post-capillary segments of a single compliance class form the medium. $\partial\mathcal{M}$ consists of:
  * the arteriolar boundary (supply): the walls of the terminal arterioles where the capillaries arise, across which blood enters at a pressure set by arteriolar tone;
  * the venular boundary (outflow at the datum): the walls of the collecting venules into which the capillaries drain, at venular pressure $p_{\mathrm{ven}}$, held within tolerance;
  * the remaining boundary, closed: tissue surfaces, or surfaces across which no blood crosses within tolerance, located by a declared perfusion measurement.

  All supply crosses the arteriolar boundary; the capillary bed contains no other inflow, so there are no interior sources. The arterioles, venules, and interstitium have compliance or storage of a different class (§0.1 item 3) and lie outside $\mathcal{M}$; the interstitium receives the filtrate, which lymphatics drain outside $\mathcal{M}$. The domain may be observed in vivo by intravital microscopy, or in an isolated perfused tissue preparation.
* **Window.** A relaxation window (§0.1 item 1), opened from a prior steady state by a declared step (or monotone ramp) increase of the pressure at the arteriolar boundary, after which arteriolar pressure, venular pressure, and temperature are held within tolerance. The increase may come from dilation of the feeding arterioles (withdrawal of vasoconstrictor tone, or a vasodilator applied to the feeding arterioles only) or, in a perfused preparation, from a raised perfusion pressure. Capillary pressure then rises at every point, so filtration rises and remains a sink, and the tissue's volume change is non-negative (vascular distension and filtrate). Windows that open with a fall of arteriolar pressure are excluded, because falling capillary pressure shrinks the tissue ($\theta < 0$); that alone justifies the exclusion. Physically, a fall can also turn filtration into reabsorption from the interstitium, an interior source. The declared datum form cannot show this: with $p_{\mathrm{f}} = p_{\mathrm{ven}}$ and $p \ge p_{\mathrm{ven}}$ the declared filtration never reverses. This second reason therefore rests on the physical Starling balance, not on the declared law, and it is why the declared form would misrepresent such a window. Windows in which myogenic or metabolic autoregulation returns the arteriolar pressure beyond tolerance are excluded.
* **Metric.** Flat and rigid (§0.1 item 2): $g_{\mu\nu} = \delta_{\mu\nu}$ in a Cartesian chart, with the tissue volume, measured by plethysmography or tissue thickness, within the rigid tolerance; any residual change is non-negative in the declared direction.
* **Cone speed.** $c_*$ is the longitudinal sound speed of the tissue. Blood velocities in the microcirculation are $\ll c_*$.
* **Storage.** $C_{\mathrm{v}}$: the compliance of the capillary bed per unit tissue volume, the blood volume stored per unit volume per unit rise of capillary pressure (m³ m⁻³ Pa⁻¹). It is declared uniform over $\mathcal{M}$ within tolerance and linear over the window's range of pressures (§0.1 item 3).
* **Free energy $\mathcal{F}$ and datum.** $\mathcal{F} = p - p_{\mathrm{ven}}$ (J m⁻³), with $p$ the network-scale capillary blood pressure. The datum is the venular pressure, held at the venular boundary. Blood flows from the arteriolar to the venular boundary, so $p \ge p_{\mathrm{ven}}$ throughout $\mathcal{M}$ is a checkable precondition (precondition 2), not a triviality. $\mathcal{F}$ is linear in the stored blood: the blood stored per unit volume above the datum is $C_{\mathrm{v}}\mathcal{F}$, so $\mathcal{F}$ is that stored blood times the declared conversion factor $1/C_{\mathrm{v}}$. The relaxation time $\ell^2C_{\mathrm{v}}/k_{\mathrm{b}}$ is finite, and it is required to exceed the time resolution of the pressure instruments by a declared factor (precondition 8).
* **Transport $\mathfrak{K}$.** $\mathfrak{K}^{\alpha\beta} = k_{\mathrm{b}}^{\alpha\beta}/C_{\mathrm{v}}$, with $k_{\mathrm{b}}^{\alpha\beta}$ the network-scale (Darcy) conductance tensor of the capillary bed for blood at its effective viscosity, parametric: for example one conductance along and one across the dominant capillary orientation (as along muscle fibres), with isotropic conductance as the default. With $q$ the Darcy blood flux (m s⁻¹), $J_W = q/C_{\mathrm{v}}$; $1/C_{\mathrm{v}}$ is the declared conversion factor.
* **Sink $\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{F}}$.** Transcapillary filtration, first order relative to the datum: $\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{F}} = k_{\mathrm{f}}(\mathcal{F} - \mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{ref}}) \ge 0$, with $k_{\mathrm{f}} = L_{\mathrm{p}}S_{\mathrm{v}}/C_{\mathrm{v}}$ ($L_{\mathrm{p}}$ the hydraulic conductivity of the capillary wall, $S_{\mathrm{v}}$ the capillary surface area per unit tissue volume). The physical filtration follows the Starling balance of hydrostatic and oncotic pressures across the wall, which vanishes at some capillary pressure $p_{\mathrm{f}}$; the first-order datum form declares $p_{\mathrm{f}} = p_{\mathrm{ven}}$ within tolerance, a declared idealization. It is admitted only under the binding closure, and it is rejected if the default fits at least as well (closure (iv)). $L_{\mathrm{p}}S_{\mathrm{v}}$ is estimated per closure (ii) by a declared method on the same tissue (for example the filtration response to venous pressure steps before the window, a declared perturbation regime), and $p_{\mathrm{f}}$ is checked against $p_{\mathrm{ven}}$ in the same regime.
* **Drive.** Default, $a^\mu_{\mathrm{d}} = 0$ (§0.1 item 5). Red cells move with the blood under pressure and drag; no drag drive is declared, and no cell is mapped as a tracer.
* **Branch II evidence (closure (v)).**
  * The trend of $\langle\theta\rangle$ and a bound on $\int\langle\theta\rangle\,d\tau$ come from the measured tissue volume, which must stay within the rigid tolerance.
  * $\lim\langle\Theta_E\rangle > 0$ is estimated from measured dissipation (§0.1 item 7):
    * the supply term uses the inflow through the feeding arterioles (red-cell velocimetry times lumen cross-section in vivo, or the perfusion flow of a perfused preparation) and $\mathcal{F}$ at the arteriolar boundary from servo-null micropressure measurement;
    * the venular boundary, at the datum, contributes nothing;
    * the consumption term uses the declared, independently estimated $k_{\mathrm{f}}$ with $\mathcal{F}$ from a second set of capillary pressure measurements;
    * the storage term uses repeated pressure measurements.

    The $O_1$ test uses an interior section and its own instruments.
  * The physical dissipation is the viscous dissipation of blood flow, $q\cdot(-\nabla p)$ per unit volume. Under the declarations it equals the r7 dissipation density $-J_E^\mu\nabla_\mu\mathcal{F}$ times the positive constant $p_0C_{\mathrm{v}}$, and its measured sign must agree with that of the estimate.
* **Admissible domains** (declared, checkable preconditions):
  1. no interior inflow (no arteriovenous shunts or feeding vessels inside $\mathcal{M}$ beyond the excised ones), and $\nabla\mathcal{F} \neq 0$ throughout $\bar{\mathcal{M}}$;
  2. capillary pressure above venular pressure throughout $\mathcal{M}$, and net filtration non-negative everywhere (no reabsorption), with interstitial hydrostatic and oncotic pressures constant within tolerance over the window;
  3. a single compliance class: $C_{\mathrm{v}}$ uniform within tolerance, checked by the blood-volume response to a pressure step in two or more subregions;
  4. no capillary recruitment or derecruitment within the window (perfused capillary density constant within tolerance, by intravital microscopy), and a steady distribution of haematocrit, so that $k_{\mathrm{b}}$ is constant;
  5. arteriolar and venular boundary pressures held within tolerance after the step, with residual cardiac pulsation, respiratory modulation, and vasomotion at the arteriolar boundary within tolerance; windows with vasomotion beyond tolerance are excluded;
  6. tissue volume within the rigid tolerance, isothermal conditions within tolerance;
  7. the closed parts of the boundary carry no blood flux within tolerance;
  8. a relaxation time $\ell^2C_{\mathrm{v}}/k_{\mathrm{b}}$ that exceeds the time resolution of the pressure instruments by a declared factor, so that the field is resolvably stored and not quasi-static.
* **Titled phenomena outside the domain.**
  * *Affect and its regulation.* Emotions, their appraisal, and their regulation are processes of the person, outside $\mathcal{M}$; they are context. Their autonomic expression reaches the bed through sympathetic vasoconstrictor tone on the feeding arterioles, which sets the arteriolar boundary data. Within windows those data are held, so affect enters only through their held level and through the step that opens the window.
  * *Autonomic rhythms.* Heart-rate variability, respiratory modulation, and vasomotion are temporal quantities; they are excluded from windows by tolerance and are never mapped to $\theta$, $\sigma$, or any spatial operator.
  * *Psychophysiological resilience.* Recovery of physiological variables after a stressor is a property of the person's regulatory systems, outside $\mathcal{M}$. It is not mapped to $\gamma_\sigma$, to the relaxation of the pressure field, or to any rate of this aperture. Repeated stress shapes the microvasculature between windows (remodelling, changes of capillary density), which enters through the declared parameters and is mapped via Spine $\mathcal{T}$ to $\mathcal{H}$, not identified with it.
  * *Lymphatic drainage and oedema.* Drainage of the filtrate and the accumulation of interstitial fluid lie outside $\mathcal{M}$; windows end before interstitial pressure changes beyond tolerance (precondition 2).

The bed's structural adaptation (capillary density, vessel calibre, the history of its tone) is mapped via Spine $\mathcal{T}$ to $\mathcal{H}$ and is not identified with it; it enters each window through $\mathcal{H}_0$.

* **Physical Instantiation:** The maintained arteriovenous pressure difference, together with filtration, holds the bed far from equilibrium. With $J_E^\mu = -p_0^{-1}(k_{\mathrm{b}}^{\mu\nu}/C_{\mathrm{v}})\nabla_\nu\mathcal{F}$, $J_E^\mu\nabla_\mu\mathcal{F} < 0$ holds identically wherever $\nabla\mathcal{F} \neq 0$ (precondition 1), so $\dot{s}_{\mathrm{prod}} > 0$ and $\dot{W}_{\mathrm{diss}} > 0$. Blood flows down its pressure gradient from the arteriolar to the venular boundary, in the direction of $J_E^\mu$. $\dot{W}_{\mathrm{ext}} = 0$, so $\dot{W}_{\mathrm{tot}} = \dot{W}_{\mathrm{diss}} > 0$. The empirical content of $O_1$ is the flux-prediction test (`aperture_generation_guidance.md` §2.3, §5.5 item 6). $k_{\mathrm{b}}$ is estimated per closure (ii) from the bed's pressure–flow relation at several steady perfusion pressures before the window (a declared perturbation regime), and $C_{\mathrm{v}}$ from the blood-volume response to a pressure step measured optically (a different modality). The declared law must predict $J_W^{\mathrm{meas}} = q^{\mathrm{meas}}/C_{\mathrm{v}}$ across a declared interior section $S$, with $q^{\mathrm{meas}}$ from the summed flows of the capillaries crossing $S$ (red-cell velocity and lumen cross-section by intravital microscopy, per unit area), and $\nabla\mathcal{F}$ from micropressure measurements on both sides of $S$; neither uses the other or the declared $k_{\mathrm{b}}$ or $k_{\mathrm{f}}$. No current test exercises this aperture (`empirical_validation_protocol.md` §3.3).

The split isolates the volume element only algebraically; $\theta$ remains coupled to $\xi_\alpha = \hat{\Pi}_{\alpha\lambda}\eta^\lambda$ and $\sigma_{\alpha\beta}$ through the traced elliptic $\tau$-compatibility equation (`r10` Decision 2), whose two sides vanish identically here (§0.1 items 2 and 4). Vessel-wall stresses and the tone of vascular smooth muscle are Cauchy stresses and active tensions, not $T_{\mu\nu}^{\mathrm{mat}}$; tone acts through the arteriolar boundary data. The architecture of the network enters through $k_{\mathrm{b}}$ and the declared geometry of $\mathcal{M}$, not through $g$. $\Pi_{\mu\nu}$ is the Hessian of a declared precision potential and enters only the coupling sector ($O_6$). Eq. [3] is instantiated with the default drive and maps no tracer.

#### $O_4$ — Free-Energy Balance, Spectral Low-Pass Gating & Boundary Stress Conservation
* **Spine Equations:** $\frac{\partial\mathcal{F}}{\partial\tau} + \mathcal{F}\theta = -\nabla_\alpha J_W^\alpha - \mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{F}}$, $J_W^\alpha \equiv -\mathfrak{K}^{\alpha\beta}\nabla_\beta\mathcal{F}$, $\mathfrak{K}^{\alpha\beta} = \mathfrak{K}^{\beta\alpha} \succ 0$, $\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{F}} \ge 0$; $\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{\Lambda_{\mathrm{gate}}} \equiv \mathbf{1}_{(-\nabla^2 \le \Lambda_{\mathrm{gate}}^2)}$; $Q(\mathbf{x}, \tau) = \langle\mathbf{x}\vert\rho_{\mathrm{local}}(\tau)\vert\mathbf{x}\rangle > 0$; $\dot{W}_{\mathrm{var}} = \frac{1}{t_0\beta_{\mathrm{th}}}D_{\mathrm{KL}}(Q \parallel P) > 0$; $\nabla^2\xi_\nu + R_\nu^{\;\alpha}\xi_\alpha = 0 \iff \nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu}^{\mathrm{mat}} = 0$.

Compliance gives the finite relaxation time, so the field is stored, not quasi-static (precondition 8). The anisotropic $k_{\mathrm{b}}$ and the filtration law depart from the default and satisfy the binding closure, and each is rejected if the default fits at least as well (closure (iv)). The gate sector follows §0.1 item 8: the Neumann eigenmodes of $-\nabla^2$ on the rigid tissue are fixed, so the gate does not drift, and the faster decay of fine spatial structure in the pressure field is mapped via Spine $\mathcal{T}$ to the gate without being the gate. The selective permeability of the capillary wall to water and solutes is a property of the sink's parameters, not the spatial gate. The stress-conservation equivalence holds identically (§0.1 item 4).

#### $O_5$ — Riemannian Geometric Mean Gluing, Percolation & Harmonic Circulation
* **Spine Equations:** $g_{\mu\nu}^{(ij)} \equiv (g^{(i)} \# g^{(j)})_{\mu\nu}$; $\mathcal{U}_{ij}$ geodesically convex for $g^{(ij)}(\tau) \; \forall \tau \ge \tau_0$; $\phi_{\mathrm{perc}}(\mathcal{K}) = \vert\mathcal{K}\vert/\vert\mathcal{K}_0\vert > \phi_c$; $\Delta_{\mathrm{Hodge}}\mathbf{J}_{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{harm}} = 0$; $\partial_\tau\sqrt{\vert g^{(i)}\vert} \ge 0$; $g_{\mu\nu}^{(i)} \xrightarrow{C^2(\mathcal{U}_i)} G_{\infty,\mu\nu}^{(i)} \succ 0$ (standing hypothesis with $H_0 = 0$).
* **Physical Instantiation:** The cover consists of $\tau$-independent subregions of the tissue: one cover set around each excised vessel segment, containing its wall (a concave part of $\partial\mathcal{M}$), and further sets that are intersections of $\mathcal{M}$ with strictly convex bodies clear of those walls. Every overlap is declared clear of the concave parts and convex on the rigid metric (§0.1 item 9). Each $g^{(i)}$ is the restriction of $g$, so the gluing holds identically, $\partial_\tau\sqrt{\vert g^{(i)}\vert} = 0$, and $g^{(i)} = G_\infty^{(i)}$ at every $\tau$ ($H_0 = 0$). Every overlap is crossed by the transient, so $\phi_{\mathrm{perc}} = 1 > \phi_c$. The connectivity of the capillary network is a property of the bed that enters through $k_{\mathrm{b}}$: a network below its percolation threshold has no network-scale conductance and is not an admissible domain. That connectivity is mapped via Spine $\mathcal{T}$ to the condition $\phi_{\mathrm{perc}} > \phi_c$ and is not identified with it; anastomoses between vascular territories are context. Each excised vessel segment that passes through $\mathcal{M}$ adds a non-contractible loop, so $b_1(\mathcal{M}) \ge 1$ in general, and harmonic Neumann fields circulate around these segments. No blood flow or measured field is identified with them, and no claim is made about them (§0.1 item 9).

#### $O_6$ — Asymmetric Phase-Lead Coupling & Trace-Free Deformation Operator
* **Spine Equations:** $(J_{[i\to j]})_\nu = -(J_{[j\to i]})_\nu$; $M^{(ij)}(\mathbf{x}) \neq 0 \; \forall \mathbf{x} \in \bar{\mathcal{U}}_{ij}$ (hypothesis) $\wedge \; \mathrm{Tr}\,M^{(ij)} = 0 \implies m_+ > 0 > m_-$; $\frac{d}{d\tau}\mathcal{I}(Q_i; Q_j) = k_{\mathcal{I}}\oint_{\partial\mathcal{U}_{ij}} S_{\mu\nu}^{(ij)}\hat{n}^\mu\hat{n}^\nu\,dA > 0$ (hypothesis, $\langle i,j\rangle \in \mathcal{K}$), with this boundary flux integrable on $[\tau_0, \infty)$ (Branch II).
* **Physical Instantiation:** Take two adjacent cover sets along the flow path between a feeding arteriole and a draining venule, sharing an overlap $\mathcal{U}_{ij}$.
