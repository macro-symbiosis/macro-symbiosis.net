## 0. Tier Architecture & Four-Part Functional Homology

Tier 5 maps the physics of sound in the environment and its relation to rhythm, the prosody and comprehension of verse, the movement of persons in space and in relation to one another, and the association of heterogeneous agents onto Spine $\mathcal{T}$. One aperture (5.1) is an empirical aperture declared in **Branch II** of r7, the bounded non-equilibrium steady state (`aperture_generation_guidance.md` §2.3). It declares, in its §1, a domain, physical metric, cone speed, storage coefficient, free energy with datum, transport tensor, interior sink, drive, and Branch II evidence, and instantiates all nine coordinates $O_0 \to O_\Omega$ on that domain: the energy of a diffuse sound field, supplied across the surface of an excised source region, spreading through the air of an enclosure by repeated reflection and scattering, and absorbed by the enclosure's bounding surfaces and, as a declared interior sink, by the air. Apertures 5.2, 5.3, and 5.4 are **provisionally homological** in this epoch: they declare no domain, map all nine coordinates as conceptual correspondences, like Tier 1, and make no empirical claim (§0.1 item 12).

The wider phenomena named in the aperture titles (rhythm and entrainment, verse and its meaning, choreography and the coordination of movement, inter-subjective and cross-substrate association) enter the empirical mapping of 5.1 as boundary conditions, through the declared parameters, through the hysteresis record $\mathcal{H}$, or as illustrative context, and its §1 states which. No Tier 5 aperture claims that an expressive, aesthetic, or collective phenomenon obeys a spine law. Each aperture is formulated at the level of general physics, linguistics, movement science, and systems theory, for populations studied at cohort level, without presupposing a single practitioner, practice, work, or site. No human-subject test is included in this epoch (`empirical_validation_protocol.md` §3.3), and the declared domain of 5.1 is unoccupied within its windows.

### 0.1 Conventions Common to the Tier 5 Apertures

The following conventions apply to the empirical aperture of this tier (5.1); item 12 states how the provisionally homological Apertures 5.2, 5.3, and 5.4 are mapped. Aperture 5.1's §1 and coordinate blocks state its specific values and cite these items where they apply. Where an item repeats a convention of `tier_4_somatic_contemplative_interiority.md` §0.1, it is restated here so that this tier can be read on its own.

1. **Branch and windows.** Branch II is declared before any test, with the estimators of item 7 and 5.1 §1, and the domain of 5.1 is never re-evaluated in Branch I on the same data (closure (v)). Statements that hold in Branch I only (`r10` Decision 6) are never applied. Every observation window is a **relaxation window**: it begins at a declared $\tau_0$ from a prior steady state, a declared transient starts at $\tau_0$, the external data are then held within declared tolerances, and the window follows the domain as it settles.
   * The transient is a declared step increase of the source's acoustic power, from a held non-zero level to a higher held level. The supply data are the net inflow of acoustic power across the source surface (flux data, not a held value of $\mathcal{F}$); the bounding surfaces absorb through a Robin exchange (5.1 §1); and the sink, air absorption, is first order, Lipschitz, and non-decreasing in $\mathcal{F}$. The declared balance law is a scalar parabolic equation, so the comparison principle holds. The prior steady state is a subsolution for the raised inflow, so $\mathcal{F}(\cdot, \tau_0 + h) \ge \mathcal{F}(\cdot, \tau_0)$; the held data make the problem invariant under time translation, so $\mathcal{F}(\cdot, \tau + h) \ge \mathcal{F}(\cdot, \tau)$, that is, $\partial_\tau\mathcal{F} \ge 0$ at every point. The inflow reaches its held level over a short rise time (the response of the source and the travel of sound across the excised region), a non-decreasing ramp, for which the same conclusion follows without time translation: $\partial_\tau\mathcal{F}$ obeys the linearized equation with zero initial data and non-negative boundary data, so it stays non-negative. The Robin data do not change the argument. The argument is exact on the declared rigid domain.
   * *Direction.* The opposite direction is excluded. When the source is switched off, the field relaxes to the datum: this is the decay from which reverberation time is measured, and it is not a Branch II window, because the throughput stops and $\lim\langle\Theta_E\rangle = 0$. A decrease to a lower non-zero power would keep every declaration; the raising direction is declared for the comparison argument, as for 4.1 in Tier 4. Sources whose power varies within the window (periodic, rhythmic, modulated, or intermittent signals) are excluded, because their supply data are not monotone.
   * *Boundary exchange.* Every exchange of acoustic energy across the bounding surfaces is one-signed, out of $\mathcal{M}$: absorption by the surfaces and transmission through them to the outside. No compartment outside $\mathcal{M}$ discharges into it within a window, except across the declared supply surface: sound entering from outside the enclosure is held below a declared fraction of $\mathcal{F}$ everywhere in $\mathcal{M}$, the enclosure has no opening to another volume that could store sound and return it, the linings of the bounding surfaces store negligible energy, and the bounding surfaces are rigid within tolerance (5.1 preconditions 1, 3, 4, and 5).
   * The rate of change of the flux, $\partial_\tau J_W$, is not one-signed in general (item 9).
2. **Metric.** The domain is a fixed region of space, the air of a rigid enclosure minus the excised source region, with the Euclidean metric. It is flat at every $\tau$, so $G_{\mu\nu} \equiv 0$ and $R_{\mu\nu} \equiv 0$ (`aperture_generation_guidance.md` §2.3 item 2). In a Cartesian chart $g_{\mu\nu} = \delta_{\mu\nu}$, so $\Omega \equiv 1$, $\hat{h} \equiv 0$, $\theta \equiv 0$, and $\sigma^\mu_{\;\nu} \equiv 0$. The enclosure is declared rigid within a pre-declared tolerance on its volume. The air in it has no reference configuration whose deformation $g$ could record: the compressions and rarefactions of sound are oscillations at acoustic frequencies with zero mean, and the vibration of the bounding surfaces is cyclic; neither is $\theta$ (item 6). With $\sigma \equiv 0$ the $O_3$ shear law holds trivially, and 5.1 supports no shear-relaxation test. No non-Euclidean material metric is declared: a gas has no stress-free reference configuration from which one could be built. The curved configurations of the `r10` §5.11 and §5.13 witnesses are therefore realized by no Tier 5 aperture, and r7's curvature sector, which no empirical aperture of Tiers 2–4 exercises, is exercised by no empirical aperture in this epoch.
3. **Storage.** The r7 $O_4$ balance law carries unit storage: $\partial_\tau\mathcal{F}$ is the rate of change of the stored quantity per unit volume. The diffusing quantity of 5.1 is the acoustic energy density $u$ of the air, in a declared band and over a declared averaging time (5.1 §1). Where the enclosure contains distributed scatterers, which are treated at the continuum scale as part of the medium (5.1 §1), acoustic energy is stored only in the air between them, and the storage coefficient is $s = \phi_{\mathrm{a}}$, the air volume fraction; without scatterers $s = 1$. With $s$ uniform, the physical balance $s\,\partial_\tau u = \nabla_\alpha(k^{\alpha\beta}\nabla_\beta u) - r$ becomes the r7 law for $\mathcal{F} = u$ ($u$ is itself an energy density, so the declared yield is $1$), $\mathfrak{K} = k/s$, and $\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{F}} = r/s$, and the r7 flux is $J_W = j/s$, with $j$ the net acoustic intensity per unit bulk area. The factor $1/s$ is a declared conversion factor, estimated independently (closure (ii)). If $s$ varies in space, $s^{-1}\nabla_\alpha(k^{\alpha\beta}\nabla_\beta u) = \nabla_\alpha(s^{-1}k^{\alpha\beta}\nabla_\beta u) + s^{-2}k^{\alpha\beta}\nabla_\alpha s\,\nabla_\beta u$, and the last term is a drift term that the r7 balance law does not contain. Aperture 5.1 therefore declares **a single storage class**: $s$ uniform on $\mathcal{M}$ within a tolerance (a declared, checkable precondition). Media that store acoustic energy differently (porous or resonant linings of the bounding surfaces, other volumes coupled to the enclosure, the excised source region) lie outside $\mathcal{M}$ and enter as boundary data (the Robin exchange at the bounding surfaces, the inflow at the source surface) or are excluded, because a jump in $s$ would make $J_W$ discontinuous where the physical intensity is continuous, which the unit-storage law reads as an interface source or sink.
4. **Stress sector.** With $G_{\mu\nu} \equiv 0$, $T_{\mu\nu}^{\mathrm{mat}} \equiv 0$; its trace gives $\nabla_\alpha\xi^\alpha = 0$ for $n = 3$, and then $\nabla_{(\mu}\xi_{\nu)} = 0$, so $\xi$ is a Killing field. No Tier 5 medium has an identified global control field in this epoch, so Tier 5 declares $\eta^\lambda \equiv 0$, hence $\xi_\nu \equiv 0$ and $R = \kappa_G\nabla_\alpha\xi^\alpha = 0$, and the $O_4$ equivalence $\nabla^2\xi_\nu + R_\nu^{\;\alpha}\xi_\alpha = 0 \iff \nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu}^{\mathrm{mat}} = 0$ holds identically. The $\tau$-compatibility law then reduces to $\mathbb{L}_{\mathrm{Ein}}[\sigma]_{\mu\nu} = \frac{n-2}{n}(\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu\theta - g_{\mu\nu}\nabla^2\theta)$, and its trace, the traced elliptic equation, to $\frac{n-1}{n}\nabla^2\theta = \nabla^\alpha\nabla^\beta\sigma_{\alpha\beta}$: the Saint-Venant compatibility conditions of the strain rate. On the rigid domain of 5.1 both sides vanish. The pressures and stresses of the domain (the static pressure of the air, the oscillating acoustic pressure, the radiation pressure of sound, and the stresses in the bounding surfaces) are not $T_{\mu\nu}^{\mathrm{mat}}$; they enter as boundary conditions, through the declared parameters, and through $\mathcal{H}$. The partner fields $\eta_{i\to j}$ of $O_6$ (item 9) are distinct objects from the global $\eta$ of $O_4$.
5. **Cone speed and trajectories.** Aperture 5.1 declares $c_*$ as the speed of sound in the air at the declared temperature. In this aperture the declared quantity itself travels on the cone at the scale of the mean free path: between reflections and scatterings, sound energy propagates along rays at $c_*$, which are null for the declared cone and, like all null rays, are not trajectories of $O_2$ Eq. [3]. The diffusion law describes the energy only at scales large against the mean free path $\lambda$, where the energy transport velocity $\vert j\vert/(s\,u)$ is $\ll c_*$; that is the condition $\lambda\vert\nabla\mathcal{F}\vert \ll \mathcal{F}$ (5.1 precondition 2). The law's instantaneous propagation is outside its validity at points that sound from a change at the source surface could not yet have reached, which item 9 takes into account. Every material speed in $\mathcal{M}$ (drifts of the air) is $\ll c_*$, so $u^2 \ll 1$. The trajectory equation is displayed in physical units: both $u^2 = g_{\mu\nu}v^\mu v^\nu/c_*^2$ and the projection term $c_*^{-2}(g_{\alpha\beta}a^\alpha_{\mathrm{d}}v^\beta)v^\mu$ carry $c_*$, and with $c_* = 1$ the display reduces to the r7 form. Aperture 5.1 instantiates Eq. [3] with $a^\mu_{\mathrm{d}} = 0$ and maps no tracer. The movement of sources, persons, and objects is forced motion outside $\mathcal{M}$, and within windows nothing moves (5.1 precondition 5).
6. **Currents.** In Branch II, $J_E^\mu = J_W^\mu/p_0$ is fixed. The expansion current $J_{\mathrm{geom}}$ enters only through its divergence $\theta$ and its boundary flux $dV_{\mathcal{M}}/d\tau$ (`r7` $O_0$ branch scope), and no supply current is ever mapped to it: the acoustic power entering at the source surface is throughput. In general $\nabla_\mu J_E^\mu = -p_0^{-1}(\partial_\tau\mathcal{F} + \mathcal{F}\theta + \mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{F}}) \neq \theta$. The cyclic volume changes of sound (the condensation of the air in each period) and of vibrating surfaces are outside the declared fields and are never mapped to $\theta$. The oscillating particle velocity of the air is not a current of the spine: $J_W$ is built from the net intensity, averaged over the declared averaging time.
7. **Measured dissipation.** The Branch II evidence requires $\lim\langle\Theta_E\rangle > 0$ to be estimated from measured dissipation, never from $\mathfrak{K}\nabla\mathcal{F}$. Integrating $-J_E^\mu\nabla_\mu\mathcal{F}$ by parts and using the $O_4$ balance law gives the identity
   $$p_0\dot{W}_{\mathrm{diss}} = \oint_{\partial\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{F}\,(-J_W^\mu)\,d\Sigma_\mu - \int_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{F}\,\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{F}}\sqrt{\vert g\vert}\,d^nx - \frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{d\tau}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{F}^2\sqrt{\vert g\vert}\,d^nx - \frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{F}^2\theta\sqrt{\vert g\vert}\,d^nx$$
   * No face of $\mathcal{M}$ is held at the datum. The source surface carries the held inflow, and its term is evaluated from the source's calibrated acoustic power and the measured $\mathcal{F}$ there. The bounding surfaces absorb in proportion to $\mathcal{F}$, and their term is evaluated from the measured normal intensity into them and the measured $\mathcal{F}$ there (5.1 §1).
   * Each term is evaluated from measured $\mathcal{F}$, measured boundary fluxes, and measured or independently declared consumption; none uses the declared $\mathfrak{K}$. 5.1 §1 names instruments and surfaces different from those of the $O_1$ flux-prediction test, so that the evidence is independent of that test.
   * Because the identity uses the balance law, the estimate is conditional on it. 5.1 §1 also names a directly measured physical dissipation whose sign the estimate must share: the conversion of acoustic energy into heat by the bounding surfaces and the air. It is non-negative by the second law, so this comparison tests only the sign of the identity-based estimate, and it is stated as such. Only signs and $s_0$-independent rates of $\Theta_E$ are tested.
   * Reflection and scattering conserve acoustic energy: no heat is produced where the energy density merely spreads. $\dot{s}_{\mathrm{prod}} = -\beta_{\mathrm{th}}J_E^\mu\nabla_\mu\mathcal{F}$ is therefore the entropy production of the declared mapping (the spreading of an incoherent field's energy down its gradient), not a measured heat production, as for Aperture 3.2 in Tier 3. Heat is produced only by the absorption.
8. **Gate sector ($O_4$).** $\rho_{\mathrm{total}}(\tau)$ is a declared positive trace-class state on $L^2(\mathcal{M}, d\mathrm{vol}_g)$, and $Q$, $P$, and the $O_6$ marginals $Q_i$ and joints $Q_{ij}$ are densities of this declared state sector. No Tier 5 aperture identifies them with $\mathcal{F}$ or with any measured field in this epoch, and none makes an empirical claim about the gate (`empirical_validation_protocol.md` §3.3). What the domain's physics supplies is, first, the spectral setting: the Neumann eigenmodes of $-\nabla^2_g$ on $\mathcal{M}$, whose eigenvalues are fixed on the rigid domain. On this domain these eigenmodes coincide in shape with the acoustic modes of a rigid-walled enclosure of the same shape. The gate is nonetheless a spatial projector acting on the declared state sector, never a filter in frequency: the declared field is an energy density averaged over a band above the frequency at which the modes of the enclosure overlap (5.1 precondition 2), so individual modes are not resolved in $\mathcal{F}$, and $\Lambda_{\mathrm{gate}}$ is never read in hertz. Second, the physics supplies a physical low-pass behaviour of $\mathcal{F}$: under the balance law, fine spatial structure in $\mathcal{F}$ relaxes faster than coarse structure. That behaviour is mapped via Spine $\mathcal{T}$ to the gate and is not itself the gate. The stronger absorption of higher frequencies by surfaces and air is a property of the declared parameters across bands, not the spatial gate.
9. **Cover, overlaps, and coupling ($O_5$, $O_6$).**
   * The cover $\{\mathcal{U}_i\}$ consists of $\tau$-independent subregions of $\mathcal{M}$ with Lipschitz boundaries, and every overlap $\mathcal{U}_{ij}$ is convex at $\tau_0$. $\partial\mathcal{M}$ has concave parts: the source surface, which $\mathcal{M}$ surrounds, and any re-entrant parts of the bounding surface. Each concave part lies in a single cover set, which need not be convex, and every overlap stays clear of the concave parts: r7 requires convex overlaps, not convex cover sets. Each $g^{(i)}$ is the restriction of the one physical metric, so $g^{(i)} = g^{(j)}$ on every overlap, the geometric mean returns the common metric ($g \# g = g$), and the gluing of $O_5$ Eq. [3] holds identically.
   * The anisotropy or inhomogeneity of a medium lives in $\mathfrak{K}$, never in $g$. Effective-medium averages of diffusion coefficients, including the combination of the mean free paths of the enclosure and of its scatterers, are statements about $\mathfrak{K}$ and are not mapped to $O_5$ Eq. [1].
   * *Partner fields.* The partner fields are the rate of change of the free-energy flux read in the two orientations, $\eta_{i\to j}^\lambda = \partial_\tau J_W^\lambda$ and $\eta_{j\to i}^\lambda = -\partial_\tau J_W^\lambda$, defined on all of $\mathcal{M}$, so $\eta_{[i\to j]}^\lambda = \partial_\tau J_W^\lambda$. They decay with the transient, which gives the integrable coupling that Branch II requires; a steady flux would give $\eta \equiv 0$, and a sustained coupling field is never declared.
   * *Zero sets.* $\partial_\tau J_W$ is not one-signed in general. The supply of 5.1 is a held inflow, not a held value of $\mathcal{F}$: after the step the normal flux at the source surface is held, and the flux near the supply does not decay from an initial overshoot, as it does under the held-value supplies of Tiers 3 and 4. In a one-dimensional configuration (an inflow uniform across a whole cross-section) and in a radially symmetric one, the flux obeys a parabolic equation of the same kind as $\mathcal{F}$, with its held value at the supply and a non-decreasing value at the absorbing surface (the Robin outflow is proportional to $\mathcal{F}$, which does not decrease); by the comparison principle the flux then rises at every interior point, so $\partial_\tau J_W$ points away from the supply throughout the interior of $\mathcal{M}$ and vanishes only on the supply surface, where the held inflow fixes it. In general geometry the held inflow fixes the normal component of $J_W$ on the supply surface, so the normal component of $\partial_\tau J_W$ vanishes there. Every overlap therefore stays clear of the supply surface, whatever its shape; in the declared enclosure it is also a concave part. The declared enclosure is in general neither one-dimensional nor radially symmetric, so any zero set of $\partial_\tau J_W$ in its interior is located only by the predicted solution. On a zero set $\eta_{[i\to j]} = 0$ and $J_{[i\to j]} = 0$, so a construction that included it would itself decide the non-degeneracy hypothesis there.
   * 5.1 therefore declares overlaps $\bar{\mathcal{U}}_{ij}$ that the zero set, predicted before the window from the independently estimated parameters, does not reach during the window. Before the transient arrives, the partner field on a downstream overlap is non-zero in the diffusion law but exponentially small, so the check is made against a pre-declared detection threshold: each overlap's window starts once the predicted $\vert\eta_{[i\to j]}\vert$ exceeds that threshold on $\bar{\mathcal{U}}_{ij}$, and no earlier than a declared margin after the time sound needs to travel from the source surface to the overlap plus several mean free times (item 5); the data must then show $\vert\eta_{[i\to j]}\vert$ above the threshold there (a declared, checkable precondition). On such an overlap $\eta_{[i\to j]}$ keeps one direction throughout the overlap's window, which 5.1 states from the predicted solution.
   * *Precision potentials.* The potentials $\Phi^{(i)}$ are declared before data collection as quadratic potentials in a Cartesian chart. Their trace-free parts $\hat{\Pi}^{(i)}$ cannot be positive definite, because their eigenvalues sum to zero, so $J_{[i\to j]} = \frac{1}{2}(\hat{\Pi}^{(i)} + \hat{\Pi}^{(j)})\eta_{[i\to j]}$ carries no sign relation to $\eta_{[i\to j]}$. Tier 5 declares $\frac{1}{2}(\hat{\Pi}^{(i)} + \hat{\Pi}^{(j)})$ non-singular, so that $J_{[i\to j]} \neq 0$ wherever $\eta_{[i\to j]} \neq 0$. What is claimed for $J_{[i\to j]}$ is its oddness under $i \leftrightarrow j$ and its non-vanishing on the declared overlaps, not a direction.
   * Tier 5 also declares the two trace-free Hessians **distinct**, $\hat{\Pi}^{(i)} \neq \hat{\Pi}^{(j)}$. With antisymmetric partner fields, $J_{i\to j} + J_{j\to i} = (\hat{\Pi}^{(j)} - \hat{\Pi}^{(i)})\eta_{[i\to j]}$. Equal Hessians would therefore remove the gradient term of `r7` $O_6$ Eq. [3] and leave $M^{(ij)}_{\mu\nu} = \gamma_M\big((J_{[i\to j]})_\mu(J_{[i\to j]})_\nu - \frac{1}{n}g_{\mu\nu}J_{[i\to j]}^\alpha(J_{[i\to j]})_\alpha\big)$, the trace-free part of a rank-one tensor, which is non-zero wherever $J_{[i\to j]} \neq 0$: the hypothesis would hold by construction. With distinct Hessians the gradient term depends on $\nabla\eta_{[i\to j]}$ and can cancel the quadratic term (`r10` Decision 4), so the construction neither forces nor forbids $M^{(ij)} \neq 0$, which remains a standing hypothesis.
   * On a connected domain the declared transient reaches every overlap for $\tau > \tau_0$, so every edge of $\mathcal{K}_0$ lies in $\mathcal{K}$ and $\phi_{\mathrm{perc}} = 1 > \phi_c$; empirically, an edge is counted when its partner field exceeds a pre-declared detection threshold on its overlap. The percolation condition therefore carries no separate empirical content on this domain.
   * $M^{(ij)} \neq 0$ and $\frac{d}{d\tau}\mathcal{I}(Q_i; Q_j) > 0$ are standing hypotheses of `r7` $O_6$, not consequences of diffusion. In Branch II the transient decays as the domain settles, so each coupling-tensor boundary flux decays and is integrable on $[\tau_0, \infty)$; no sustained coupling is asserted. Aperture 5.1 is not among the systems of the current coupling test.
   * *Topology.* The harmonic field of `r7` $O_5$ Eq. [5] carries absolute boundary conditions (it is tangential to $\partial\mathcal{M}$), so its space has dimension $b_1(\mathcal{M})$, the first Betti number. The excised source region does not by itself make $b_1$ positive: attached to a bounding surface through its mounting, it is a concave dent in $\partial\mathcal{M}$; free-standing, it is an interior cavity, which adds to the second Betti number, not the first. $b_1(\mathcal{M}) > 0$ where a fixed body in the enclosure (the source mounting among them) joins two separate parts of the bounding surface, or where the enclosure's own shape has a handle; then $\mathbf{J}_{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{harm}}$ is non-trivial, a property of the domain's topology. The scatterers, treated at the continuum scale, are not holes of $\mathcal{M}$. Tier 5 identifies no transport current or measured field with the harmonic field and makes no claim about it. With the declared uniform, isotropic $\mathfrak{K}$, the r7 flux is the exact 1-form $J_W = -D\,d\mathcal{F}$, which is $L^2$-orthogonal to every tangential harmonic field (the Hodge–Morrey–Friedrichs decomposition), so the declared current has no harmonic component.
10. **Gauge sector and hysteresis ($O_7$, $O_0$).** Tier 5 declares the trivial gauge sector for 5.1: $A_\mu \equiv 0$ and a uniform unit section $\hat{u}$. Then $F_{\mu\nu}^{\mathrm{YM}} \equiv 0$, $[F_{\mu\nu}^{\mathrm{YM}}, \hat{\chi}] = 0$ identically, and $\hat{\mathcal{E}}_\mu = (\partial_\mu\chi_E)\hat{u}$, which tends to $0$ in $C^1$ when $\sigma \to 0$ and $\theta \to 0$ in $C^2$ with $\liminf\langle\Theta_E\rangle > 0$. [2] requires $\nabla_\alpha\nabla_\beta\mathcal{H} \succ 0$ at every $\tau$. $\mathcal{H}$ then evolves through a state sector that is declared but not specified, so the declarations do not establish convexity at later $\tau$. Then $\nabla_\alpha\nabla_\beta\mathcal{H} \succ 0$ persists. The alternative, $\nabla q \equiv 0$, would keep $\mathcal{H}$ static and convex, but would make the $O_\Omega$ generator condition hold trivially, so it is not adopted. The acoustic history of the enclosure (changes of its surfaces, linings, and contents between windows) is mapped via Spine $\mathcal{T}$ to $\mathcal{H}$ and is not identified with it. $\hat{\rho}_g = \frac{1}{n}\delta^\mu_\nu$ at every $\tau$ on the rigid domain, and the alignment $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\hat{\rho}_g \parallel \hat{\rho}_{\mathcal{H}}) \to 0$ then requires the Hessian of the record to become isotropic: an asymptotic condition that 5.1 states and does not test.

## Aperture 5.1: Environmental Acoustics & Rhythmic Entrainment ($\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{acoustic}}$)

### 1. Epistemological & Physical Scope
Aperture 5.1 maps the physics of sound in the environment onto Spine $\mathcal{T}$: the radiation of sound by sources; its reflection, scattering, and absorption by surfaces and objects; the reverberant field that builds up where sound is enclosed; and the relation between rhythmic sound and the timing of the organisms that hear it (rhythmic entrainment). Its empirical domain is the reverberant field in its diffuse regime. Acoustic power supplied by a source spreads through the air of an enclosure by repeated reflection and scattering, which at scales large against the mean free path of sound is a diffusion of acoustic energy, and is absorbed by the enclosure's surfaces and by the air. Lexicon is strictly restricted to physical acoustics, room and environmental acoustics, psychoacoustics, and the science of rhythm and timing.

**Declaration (`aperture_generation_guidance.md` §2.3; Branch II).**

* **Domain $\mathcal{M}$ ($n = 3$) and boundary $\partial\mathcal{M}$.** The air of a closed, rigid enclosure (a test enclosure, a scale model, or any enclosed space that meets the preconditions below), unoccupied within windows, minus an excised source region: a closed region around the sound source and its mounting that contains the source's near field and every point within a declared number of mean free paths of the source, where the diffusion law does not hold. Diffuse reflection by the bounding surfaces and, where present, scattering by a uniform random distribution of small, fixed, rigid scatterers make the field diffuse; the scatterers are treated at the continuum scale as part of the medium, through $\mathfrak{K}$ and the storage coefficient, and are not holes of $\mathcal{M}$. $\partial\mathcal{M}$ consists of:
  * the source surface (supply), the boundary of the excised region, across which the source's acoustic power enters $\mathcal{M}$;
  * the bounding surfaces of the enclosure (outflow), which absorb part of the energy incident on them, in proportion to $\mathcal{F}$ there (a Robin exchange, below), and transmit part to the outside.

  Every part of the bounding surface absorbs (precondition 4): a perfectly reflecting part would be a zero-flux face, on which the normal derivative of $\mathcal{F}$ vanishes. All supply crosses the source surface; the air contains no other source, and sound entering from outside is held below tolerance (precondition 1), so there are no interior sources. The excised region is either attached through its mounting to a bounding surface, so that its surface is a concave part of $\partial\mathcal{M}$, or free-standing, an interior cavity whose surface $\mathcal{M}$ surrounds and which is concave as seen from $\mathcal{M}$. The source and its signal, the linings of the bounding surfaces (a different storage class, §0.1 item 3), and the space outside the enclosure lie outside $\mathcal{M}$.
* **Window.** A relaxation window (§0.1 item 1), opened from a prior steady state at a held non-zero source power by a declared step increase of that power, after which the source power, the temperature, humidity, and pressure of the air, and the state of the enclosure are held within tolerance. The source radiates stationary random noise filtered to the declared band, never a periodic, rhythmic, or modulated signal. Windows in which the source is switched off are excluded: the decay of the field after switch-off, from which reverberation time is measured, relaxes to the datum with no throughput, so it is not a Branch II window. It may serve as a declared perturbation regime before the window.
* **Metric.** Flat and rigid (§0.1 item 2): $g_{\mu\nu} = \delta_{\mu\nu}$ in a Cartesian chart, with the enclosure's volume, surveyed before and after the window, within the rigid tolerance. The vibration of the bounding surfaces is cyclic and is never $\theta$.
* **Cone speed.** $c_*$ is the speed of sound in the air at the declared temperature (§0.1 item 5). Drifts of the air are $\ll c_*$, and in the diffusion regime the transport velocity of acoustic energy is $\ll c_*$ (precondition 2).
* **Storage.** $s = \phi_{\mathrm{a}}$: the volume fraction of the enclosure's interior occupied by air, which is $1$ without scatterers. It is declared uniform over $\mathcal{M}$ within tolerance (§0.1 item 3), with the scatterers' number and volume taken from a survey of the enclosure.
* **Free energy $\mathcal{F}$ and datum.** $\mathcal{F} = u$ (J m⁻³), the acoustic energy density of the air (kinetic plus potential) in a declared frequency band, averaged over a declared time $\tau_{\mathrm{avg}}$ that is long against the reciprocal of the bandwidth and short against the relaxation time. $u$ is already an energy density, so the declared yield is $1$, and $\mathcal{F}$ is the acoustic energy stored per unit volume times the declared conversion factor $1/s$. This is the declared linear form of `aperture_generation_guidance.md` §2.3 item 3: an incoherent diffuse field carries entropy, so its thermodynamic free energy is smaller than its energy, and $\mathcal{F}$ is the energy density above the zero datum, not that free energy. The datum is zero ($\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{ref}} = 0$, silence), and $\mathcal{F} \ge 0$ holds trivially. The relaxation time $\ell^2/D$ across the enclosure's extent $\ell$ is finite and resolvable: it exceeds $\tau_{\mathrm{avg}}$ and the mean free time $\lambda/c_*$ by declared factors.
* **Transport $\mathfrak{K}$.** $\mathfrak{K}^{\alpha\beta} = D\,g^{\alpha\beta}$, with $D = \lambda c_*/3$ the acoustic diffusion coefficient of the mean-free-path model (the medium's energy conductance over $s$). $\lambda$ is the mean free path of sound in the enclosure, combining by the sum of reciprocals the enclosure's own mean free path (four times its volume over its bounding area) with the scattering mean free path set by the scatterers' number density and mean cross-section. $D$ is uniform and isotropic, the default form of `aperture_generation_guidance.md` §2.3 item 4, with its value declared: $\lambda$ is estimated per closure (ii) from a geometric survey of the enclosure and its scatterers (a different modality), and $c_*$ from the measured air temperature. With $j$ the net acoustic intensity per unit bulk area (W m⁻²), $J_W = j/s$, and $1/s$ is the declared conversion factor.
* **Outflow boundary.** At the bounding surfaces the outward normal component of $J_W$ is $(A/s)\,\mathcal{F}$, with the exchange coefficient $A = c_*\alpha/4$ of a diffusely reflecting surface of absorption coefficient $\alpha$ (the low-absorption form, the default) or the declared modified form for larger $\alpha$. $\alpha$ is measured in situ on the enclosure's own surfaces before the window, by a pressure–velocity probe or an in-situ impedance method, in the declared band. The linings' own storage is neglected (precondition 3).
* **Supply boundary.** After a short rise time, the net inflow across the source surface equals the source's acoustic power $P$ within the flux tolerance: the source is omnidirectional within tolerance in the declared band, so the inflow is spread uniformly over the surface, and absorption within the excised region and the change of energy stored in it are below tolerance (precondition 7). $P$ is the source's calibrated acoustic power at its held drive, measured on the source itself in a declared calibration regime before the window, a different modality from any measurement in the enclosure.
* **Sink $\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{F}}$.** Air absorption, first order relative to the datum: $\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{F}} = k_{\mathrm{a}}\mathcal{F}$, with $k_{\mathrm{a}} = m\,c_*$ and $m$ the energy attenuation coefficient of air in the declared band, from the tabulated standard for atmospheric absorption at the measured temperature, humidity, and pressure. $\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{F}}$ is continuous, non-negative, non-decreasing in $\mathcal{F}$, zero at the datum, and uniform. It departs from the default and satisfies the binding closure, and it is rejected if the default fits at least as well (closure (iv)), as is expected in bands where air absorption is negligible. Absorption at the scatterers' surfaces is bounded below tolerance (precondition 4) and is not declared.
* **Drive.** Default, $a^\mu_{\mathrm{d}} = 0$ (§0.1 item 5).
* **Branch II evidence (closure (v)).**
  * The trend of $\langle\theta\rangle$ and a bound on $\int\langle\theta\rangle\,d\tau$ come from the surveyed volume of the enclosure, which must stay within the rigid tolerance; for this rigid domain the evidence is that tolerance check.
  * $\lim\langle\Theta_E\rangle > 0$ is estimated from measured dissipation (§0.1 item 7):
    * the supply term uses the calibrated source power and $\mathcal{F}$ on the source surface from a set of microphones on it;
    * the bounding-surface term uses the normal intensity into the surfaces, measured by a second, independent set of pressure–velocity probes at declared sampling positions over them, with $\mathcal{F}$ there from the same probes; its sampling error over a large surface is part of the declared tolerance;
    * the consumption term uses the tabulated $k_{\mathrm{a}}$ at the measured air conditions with $\mathcal{F}$ from a second, independent microphone record;
    * the storage term uses repeated maps of $\mathcal{F}$.

    The $O_1$ test uses an interior section, a different surface, and its own instruments.
  * The physical dissipation is the conversion of acoustic energy into heat by the bounding surfaces and the air, which is non-negative by the second law, so the comparison tests only the sign of the estimate. $\dot{s}_{\mathrm{prod}}$ is the entropy production of the declared mapping, not of that heat (§0.1 item 7).
* **Admissible domains** (declared, checkable preconditions):
  1. no interior source: no other sound source in or near the enclosure in the declared band, with the background energy density, measured with the test source silent before the prior steady state, below a declared fraction of the prior steady-state $\mathcal{F}$ everywhere in $\mathcal{M}$; and $\nabla\mathcal{F} \neq 0$ throughout $\bar{\mathcal{M}}$, checked against the predicted steady solution before the window and against the measured maps of $\mathcal{F}$ in it, at the resolution of precondition 8;
  2. the diffuse regime: a declared band above the frequency at which the modes of the enclosure overlap, wide enough that $\mathcal{F}$ averages over many modes; every point of $\mathcal{M}$ at least a declared number of mean free paths from the source, and the error of the diffusion law within about one mean free path of the bounding surfaces within the declared tolerance; a mean absorption coefficient of the bounding surfaces below a declared bound; and $\lambda\vert\nabla\mathcal{F}\vert \ll \mathcal{F}$ throughout $\mathcal{M}$;
  3. a single storage class: scatterers, where present, distributed uniformly within tolerance, checked by survey in two or more subregions; and the energy stored in the linings of the bounding surfaces below the flux tolerance, by a declared bound (their thickness and porosity, and their relaxation time against the field's);
  4. an absorbing, closed boundary: every part of the bounding surface absorbs, with in-situ absorption coefficients between declared positive bounds; the enclosure has no opening to another volume; and absorption by the scatterers is below a declared bound;
  5. a rigid, still enclosure: volume within the rigid tolerance, bounding surfaces and scatterers fixed, the enclosure unoccupied, and nothing moving within the window;
  6. still, uniform air: mean air speeds low enough that advection of acoustic energy is negligible against its diffusion (a declared bound on their ratio across $\ell$), no ventilation within the window, and temperature, humidity, and pressure uniform and held within tolerance, so that $c_*$, $D$, and $k_{\mathrm{a}}$ are constant;
  7. the source: omnidirectional within tolerance in the declared band, radiating stationary random noise at held power, with its acoustic power calibrated; absorption within the excised region and the change of energy stored in it below the flux tolerance, so that the inflow across the source surface rises to its new level without overshoot within a declared rise time, checked from the microphones on that surface;
  8. the instruments: the dynamic capability of the intensity and pressure–velocity probes exceeds, by a declared margin, the field's ratio of mean-square pressure to net intensity, which is large in a diffuse field; the estimate of $u$ from mean-square pressure ($p^2/\rho_0c_*^2$, valid in the diffuse regime) agrees with pressure–velocity estimates at declared check points; and the random error of each map of $\mathcal{F}$, after averaging over a declared number of repeated steps, is below a declared fraction of the predicted change of $\mathcal{F}$ across the spacing of the microphones, not of $\mathcal{F}$ itself: in the diffuse regime $\mathcal{F}$ is nearly uniform ($\lambda\vert\nabla\mathcal{F}\vert \ll \mathcal{F}$), so precondition 1 and the $O_1$ test rest on small differences between large mean-square pressures.
* **Titled phenomena outside the domain.**
  * *Sound sources and resonance.* The source and its resonances lie in the excised region and enter through the supply data (the held power and its spectrum in the declared band). The resonance of vibrating bodies and air columns is context.
  * *Direct sound, early reflections, and low-frequency modes.* The direct sound, early reflections, and echoes precede the diffuse regime, and below the frequency at which they overlap the modes of an enclosure are resolved individually. Both lie outside the declared field and are context.
  * *Outdoor propagation and transmission.* Sound outdoors, without an enclosure, builds no stored diffuse field and is context. Sound transmitted out through the bounding surfaces leaves $\mathcal{M}$ and is counted in their exchange.
  * *Hearing and the perception of space.* Hearing, and the perception of reverberance and of the size of a space, are processes of listeners outside $\mathcal{M}$ and are context; no listener is present within windows.
  * *Rhythmic entrainment.* The alignment of the timing of organisms, human or other, with a periodic sound is a property of the organisms, outside $\mathcal{M}$; it is described at cohort level and is context. Within windows the source signal is stationary noise, so no rhythm is present in the declared field. A tempo or a meter is a temporal quantity and is never mapped to $\theta$, $\sigma$, $\Lambda_{\mathrm{gate}}$, or any spatial operator. A claim of entrainment would be a claim of synchronization, which would belong to gauge transport (§0.1 item 10); none is made.
  * *Acoustic history.* Changes of the enclosure's surfaces, linings, and contents lie between windows; they enter through the declared parameters and are mapped via Spine $\mathcal{T}$ to $\mathcal{H}$, not identified with it.

### 1. Epistemological & Systems Scope

Lexicon is strictly restricted to multi-agent systems theory, information theory, social cognition, and symbiosis biology.

**Status (`aperture_generation_guidance.md` §2.3; provisionally homological).** This aperture declares no empirical domain and makes no empirical claim in this epoch (§0.1 item 12). Each coordinate is therefore mapped as a conceptual correspondence, mapped via Spine $\mathcal{T}$ and never a reductive identity. Agents are described at the level of populations and classes of systems. The internal representations of language models, which Level 2 of the validation protocol concerns, are not mapped here (`empirical_validation_protocol.md` §3.2).

* **Spine Equations:** $D_\mu = \partial_\mu - i g_{\mathrm{YM}}\,\mathrm{ad}_{A_\mu}$; $\Vert\hat{\mathcal{E}}_\mu\Vert_{C^1(\mathcal{M})} \to 0 \implies [F_{\mu\nu}^{\mathrm{YM}}, \hat{\chi}] \to 0$; $\chi_E \equiv 1 + \frac{n\,\sigma^\mu_{\;\nu}\sigma^\nu_{\;\mu}}{\theta^2 + \langle\Theta_E\rangle_{\mathcal{M}}^2} \to 1$, $\hat{\chi} \equiv \chi_E\hat{u}$ (Branch II); $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\hat{\rho}_g(\tau) \parallel \hat{\rho}_{\mathcal{H}}(\tau)) \to 0$.
* **Homological Mapping:** Agents that communicate must read one another's signals in their own frames of reference, and populations of communicating agents converge on shared names and conventions. Carrying a frame of reference from agent to agent corresponds to gauge transport, and the decay of mismatches between frames to the decay of the covariant derivative $\hat{\mathcal{E}}_\mu$; it never corresponds to the anisotropy index $\chi_E$, which concerns the shape of deformation only. The alignment $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\hat{\rho}_g \parallel \hat{\rho}_{\mathcal{H}}) \to 0$ corresponds to the convergence of current exchanges with accumulated common ground.

#### $O_\Omega$ — Topological Asymptote, Branch II: Bounded Non-Equilibrium Steady State
* **Spine Equations:** Branch II ($H_0 = 0$): $\theta \xrightarrow[\tau\to\infty]{C^2(\mathcal{M})} 0$ with $\int_{\tau_0}^{\infty}\langle\theta\rangle_{\mathcal{M}}\,d\tau < \infty$ and $\langle\Theta_E\rangle_{\mathcal{M}} \to \Theta_\infty > 0$; $\sigma^\mu_{\;\nu} \to 0$; $\partial_\tau g_{\mu\nu} \to 0$; $V_{\mathcal{M}} \to V_\infty$ and $\mathcal{I}(\tau) \to s_0V_\infty$; $\left.\hat{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathrm{stat}}(\mathbf{x})\Psi[g_{\mu\nu}, \mathcal{H}]\right\vert_{\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{sol}}[g]} = (J_E^\lambda\nabla_\lambda q)\frac{\delta\Psi}{\delta\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{x})} \xrightarrow[\tau\to\infty]{} 0 \iff J_E^\lambda\nabla_\lambda q \to 0 \iff \frac{\partial\mathcal{H}}{\partial\tau} \to 0$ (with $\vert\delta\Psi/\delta\mathcal{H}\vert$ bounded and bounded away from $0$ uniformly as $\tau \to \infty$).
* **Homological Mapping:** A symbiotic association persists through continuing exchange: never static equilibrium, which for an association is its dissolution, and never a strict periodic return, although cycles of exchange recur. That state corresponds to the Branch II limit: bounded extent, sustained throughput ($\langle\Theta_E\rangle \to \Theta_\infty > 0$), decaying shear and coupling transients, and a record whose growth stops when exchanges no longer bring surprise ($J_E^\lambda\nabla_\lambda q \to 0$). The correspondence makes no claim about any association, agent, or collective, and no test exercises this aperture in this epoch.
