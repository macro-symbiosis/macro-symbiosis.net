    Macro-Symbiotic Tensor (\(O_0 \to O_\Omega\)): Design Rationale & Notation Companion¶

    Companion to: foundational-manifold-2026-10-20-r7.json (companion revision r10). The r7 JSON generalizes r6 under Design Decision 6; every r6 equation is recovered as the Branch I default, and §5.1–§5.12 below verify that case. §5.1–§5.12 are unchanged from r9 except one correction: the reference-prior normalization of §5.4 (and the overlap density of §5.8) is now stated in the \(\sqrt{\vert g\vert}\)-weighted form, which repairs the prior that §5.10 and §5.11 inherit from §5.4, where \(Q\) is non-uniform and r9's unweighted condition did not normalize \(P\).

    1. Purpose of This Document¶

    This companion document records the modeling decisions, physical intuition, and standardized mathematical notation underlying the nine-coordinate specification in foundational-manifold-2026-10-20-r7.json (and its r6 special case). It connects the systems-theoretic intent of the framework to the differential-geometric equations.

    2. Core Modeling Decisions & Design Rationale¶

    Decision 1: Homothetic (Conformally Stationary) Closure at the Asymptote (\(O_\Omega \to O_0\))¶

    • Conceptual Intent: The system is permanently open to environmental flux (\(\dot{W}_{\mathrm{ext}} > 0\)) and continuously expands its capacity, yet at the asymptotic horizon (\(O_\Omega\)) it achieves a stable, stationary form that closes the loop back to \(O_0\).
    • Mathematical Implementation: A metric cannot be strictly static (\(\partial_\tau g_{\mu\nu} = 0\)) while its volume is still growing (\(\theta = \nabla_\mu J_{\mathrm{geom}}^\mu > 0\), Branch I). Therefore, \(O_\Omega\) is modeled as a homothetic (self-similar) state: as \(\tau \to \infty\), the expansion scalar approaches a positive constant (\(\theta \to n H_0 > 0\)) while internal shear decays to zero (\(\sigma^\mu_{\;\nu} \to 0\)). The physical volume grows without bound (\(V_{\mathcal{M}}(\tau) \sim e^{n H_0 \tau} \to \infty\); precisely, \(e^{-2H_0\tau}g_{\mu\nu} \xrightarrow{C^2(\mathcal{M})} G_{\infty,\mu\nu} \succ 0\) by \(O_5\) Eq. 4), sustaining positive boundary work (\(\dot{W}_{\mathrm{ext}} > 0\)), while the conformally rescaled metric \(\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu} = \Omega^{-2}g_{\mu\nu} \to \hat{g}_\infty\) becomes time-independent (\(\partial_\tau \tilde{g}_{\mu\nu} \to 0\)).
    • Mixed-Index Limit: Because the lower-index metric components \(g_{\mu\nu}\) grow as \(e^{2H_0\tau}\) (with \(e^{-2H_0\tau}g_{\mu\nu} \to G_{\infty,\mu\nu}\), so the Christoffel symbols stay bounded in \(C^1\)), asymptotic convergence in \(O_\Omega\) is written in mixed \((1,1)\) form (\(\frac{1}{2}g^{\mu\alpha}\partial_\tau g_{\alpha\nu} \to H_0 \delta^\mu_\nu\)), which is scale-invariant.

    Decision 2: Separating Extrinsic Expansion (\(\theta, K_{\mu\nu}\)) from Environmental Stiffness (\(\Pi_{\mu\nu}\)) (\(O_0, O_2, O_3\))¶

    • Conceptual Intent: Navigating a rigid external environment (\(O_2\)) should guide trajectories without crushing internal growth (\(O_3\)).
    • Mathematical Implementation:
    • Synchronous Normal Gauge (\(N = 1, \beta^\mu = 0\)): All volumetric growth is carried once by the normal extrinsic expansion scalar \(\theta \equiv -N K = \frac{1}{2}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\tau g_{\mu\nu} = \nabla_\mu J_{\mathrm{geom}}^\mu \ge 0\) (\(> 0\) in Branch I; r6 wrote \(\nabla_\mu J_E^\mu\), and Decision 6 separates the currents), avoiding any double-counting between coordinate velocity and metric growth.
    • Unimodular Conformal-Weyl Factorization: The spatial metric is factored non-perturbatively as \(g_{\mu\nu} = \Omega^2 \hat{g}_{\mu\alpha}(\exp \hat{h})^\alpha_{\;\nu}\) with \(\det \hat{g} = 1\) and \(\mathrm{Tr}(\hat{h}) = 0\). Because \(\det(\exp \hat{h}) = 1\) at all finite shear amplitudes, the volume element \(\sqrt{\vert g\vert} = \Omega^n\) depends only on the conformal factor \(\Omega\) (a scalar density of weight \(1/n\) in the fixed reference chart), isolating the volume element from anisotropic shear (\(\hat{h}\)). The factorization is a change of variables, so it does not decouple \(\theta\) from environmental stiffness: since \(R = \kappa_G \nabla_\alpha \xi^\alpha\) with \(\xi_\alpha = \hat{\Pi}_{\alpha\lambda}\eta^\lambda\), the trace of the \(\tau\)-compatibility law gives the elliptic equation \(\frac{2(n-1)}{n}\nabla^2\theta + \frac{2R}{n}\theta = -\kappa_G\,\partial_\tau(\nabla_\alpha\xi^\alpha) + 2\nabla^\alpha\nabla^\beta\sigma_{\alpha\beta} - 2\sigma^{\alpha\beta}R_{\alpha\beta}\), which ties expansion to precision.

    Decision 3: Local Subsystem Metrics (\(g^{(i)}\)) vs. the Glued Macroscopic Metric (\(g \equiv g^{\mathrm{macro}}\)) (\(O_3 \to O_5\))¶

    • Conceptual Intent: Individual subsystems (\(O_4\)) bring distinct internal geometries (\(g^{(i)}\)) into shared overlap zones (\(O_5\)), blending them into a coherent macroscopic whole (\(O_7\)).
    • Mathematical Implementation:
    • Fixed Open Cover (\(\mathcal{M} = \bigcup_i \mathcal{U}_i\)): The coordinate domains \(\mathcal{U}_i\) and their intersections \(\mathcal{U}_{ij} = \mathcal{U}_i \cap \mathcal{U}_j\) are fixed in coordinate space, while their physical Riemannian volumes (\(\int \sqrt{\vert g^{(i)}\vert}\,d^n x\)) dilate over time. This allows the Reynolds transport theorem in \(O_4\) to hold cleanly without moving-coordinate-boundary terms.
    • Riemannian Geometric Mean (\(g^{(i)} \# g^{(j)}\)): On each overlap \(\mathcal{U}_{ij}\), the two subsystem metrics are interpolated via their positive-definite geometric mean \(g_{\mu\nu}^{(ij)} = (g^{(i)} \# g^{(j)})_{\mu\nu}\) and glued over all geometric overlaps \(\mathcal{K}_0 = \{\langle i,j\rangle : \mathcal{U}_i \cap \mathcal{U}_j \neq \emptyset\}\) via a smooth, time-independent partition of unity (\(\psi_i, \psi_{ij} \ge 0\)) into the single global metric \(g_{\mu\nu} \equiv g_{\mu\nu}^{\mathrm{macro}}\). Percolation is measured separately on the actively coupled subgraph \(\mathcal{K} = \{\langle i,j\rangle \in \mathcal{K}_0 : \eta_{[i\to j]} \not\equiv 0\}\) through the fraction \(\phi_{\mathrm{perc}}(\mathcal{K}) = \vert\mathcal{K}\vert/\vert\mathcal{K}_0\vert > \phi_c\).
    • Avoiding Overdetermination: The exact differential evolution laws (\(O_2\)–\(O_4\)) are imposed on the glued global metric \(g_{\mu\nu}\), while individual subsystem metrics \(g_{\mu\nu}^{(i)}\) carry, as standing hypotheses, the asymptotic homothety conditions with a common expansion rate \(H_0\) (\(e^{-2H_0\tau}g^{(i)}_{\mu\nu} \to G^{(i)}_{\infty,\mu\nu} \succ 0\) and \(\frac{1}{2}(g^{(i)})^{\mu\alpha}\partial_\tau g^{(i)}_{\alpha\nu} \to H_0\delta^\mu_\nu\), both in \(C^2(\mathcal{U}_i)\)). The glued metric inherits them, \(e^{-2H_0\tau}g_{\mu\nu} \xrightarrow{C^2(\mathcal{M})} G_{\infty,\mu\nu} \succ 0\) (\(O_5\) Eq. 4); \(O_6\) uses this for the volume shares \(c_{ij} \in (0,1]\) and \(O_\Omega\) uses it to bound the Christoffel symbols. The tilde is reserved for the unit-determinant rescaling \(\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu} = \Omega^{-2}g_{\mu\nu} \to \hat{g}_{\infty,\mu\nu}\); the \(e^{-2H_0\tau}\)-rescaled limits are written \(G_\infty\).

    Decision 4: Pairwise Joint Density (\(Q_{ij}\)), Overlap Divergence (\(Q_i^{\vert ij}\)), and Asymmetric Lead-Follow Coupling (\(\eta_{[i\to j]}\)) (\(O_6\))¶

    • Conceptual Intent: Coupled partners should sustain positive mutual information flow (\(\frac{d}{d\tau}\mathcal{I} > 0\)) through an asymmetric coupling (\(\eta_{[i\to j]} \not\equiv 0\)), even as their local states align (\(D_{\mathrm{KL}} \to 0\)). Only the magnitude of the asymmetry enters the dynamics; the equations cannot tell which partner leads.
    • Mathematical Implementation:
    • Joint vs. Overlap-Conditional Distributions: Each pair of coupled subsystems carries a joint distribution \(Q_{ij}\) on \(\mathcal{U}_i \times \mathcal{U}_j\) with marginals \(Q_i\) and \(Q_j\), defining mutual information \(\mathcal{I}(Q_i; Q_j) = D_{\mathrm{KL}}(Q_{ij} \parallel Q_i \otimes Q_j)\). Meanwhile, distributional alignment is measured on their shared overlap \(\mathcal{U}_{ij}\) via normalized conditional densities \(Q_i^{\vert ij}\), allowing \(D_{\mathrm{KL}}(Q_i^{\vert ij} \parallel Q_j^{\vert ij}) \to 0\) while \(\mathcal{I}(Q_i; Q_j)\) continues to grow.
    • Quadratic Asymmetric Coupling in \(M_{\mu\nu}^{(ij)}\): To ensure the antisymmetric phase-lead \(\eta_{[i\to j]}^\lambda = \frac{1}{2}(\eta_{i\to j}^\lambda - \eta_{j\to i}^\lambda)\) drives the stretching-and-folding operator \(M_{\mu\nu}^{(ij)}\) even when two partners have identical precision stiffness (\(\hat{\Pi}^{(i)} = \hat{\Pi}^{(j)}\)), \(M_{\mu\nu}^{(ij)}\) includes a trace-free quadratic term in the precision-symmetrized phase-lead 1-form \(J_{[i\to j]} = -J_{[j\to i]}\). This 1-form is odd under \(i \leftrightarrow j\) (its sign records the leader), but it enters \(M^{(ij)}\) only through its even outer product, so \(M^{(ij)}\), \(S^{(ij)}\), and \(d\mathcal{I}/d\tau\) are leader-follower symmetric. It generally differs from \(\frac{1}{2}(J_{i\to j} - J_{j\to i})\), with equality when \(\hat{\Pi}^{(i)} = \hat{\Pi}^{(j)}\). Because the quadratic term can still cancel against the conformal-Killing term, \(M^{(ij)}(\mathbf{x}) \neq 0 \; \forall \mathbf{x} \in \bar{\mathcal{U}}_{ij}\) and \(d\mathcal{I}/d\tau > 0\) are stated as explicit hypotheses. Since \(\mathrm{Tr}\,M^{(ij)} = 0\), the mixed eigenvalues have both signs and sum to zero (\(m_+ > 0 > m_-\)); they need not be equal in magnitude.

    Decision 5: Smooth Anisotropy Index (\(\chi\)) and Type-\((1,1)\) Density Endomorphisms (\(\hat{\rho}_g, \hat{\rho}_{\mathcal{H}}\)) (\(O_7\))¶

    • Conceptual Intent: The macroscopic network relaxes its deformation toward isotropy (\(\chi \to 1\)), tracked covariantly by a gauge field, and aligns its active geometry (\(g_{\mu\nu}\)) with its accumulated structural memory (\(\mathcal{H}\)). (Local stretching and folding belong to the relational edge, \(M^{(ij)}\) in \(O_6\); \(\chi\) measures the anisotropy of the global deformation \(\mathcal{D}\).)
    • Mathematical Implementation:
    • \(C^2\), Kink-Free Anisotropy Scalar: Instead of a ratio of maximum-to-minimum eigenvalues (which creates non-differentiable kinks where eigenvalues cross), anisotropy is measured by the kink-free invariant \(\chi \equiv \frac{n\,\mathrm{Tr}(\mathcal{D}^2)}{(\mathrm{Tr}\,\mathcal{D})^2} = 1 + \frac{n\,\sigma^\mu_{\;\nu}\sigma^\nu_{\;\mu}}{\theta^2} \ge 1\), which is \(C^2\) (it inherits the regularity of \(\partial_\tau g_{\mu\nu} \in C^2\)) and converges to \(1\) as shear decays (Branch I, where \(\theta \to nH_0 > 0\); in Branch II the index is \(\chi_E\), Decision 6).
    • Type-\((1,1)\) Endomorphisms for Relative Entropy: Because matrix logarithms and traces are only coordinate-invariant for \((1,1)\) mixed tensors, \(g_{\alpha\nu}\) and \(\nabla_\alpha \nabla_\nu \mathcal{H}\) have their first index raised by the asymptotic inverse reference metric \(\hat{g}_\infty^{\mu\alpha}\) (the density weights cancel in the normalized ratio) to form unit-trace density operators \((\hat{\rho}_g)^\mu_{\;\nu}\) and \((\hat{\rho}_{\mathcal{H}})^\mu_{\;\nu}\).

    Decision 6: Throughput Separated from Expansion; Declared Drive, Transport, and Sink; Two Asymptotic Branches (r7, r10)¶

    Motivation. In r6, one current \(J_E^\mu\) both expands the metric volume (\(\theta = \nabla_\mu J_E^\mu > 0\)) and carries the free-energy throughput (\(J_E^\mu\nabla_\mu\mathcal{F} < 0\)), the free-energy balance law has isotropic constant diffusivity and no interior sink, test trajectories are force-free, and the asymptote requires unbounded homothetic expansion (\(H_0 > 0\)). These are the laws of a homogeneous, sink-free, perpetually inflating medium. Bounded open systems with interior consumption, anisotropic transport, or driven trajectories cannot satisfy them literally. r7 generalizes exactly these points and nothing else.

Every r6 identity of the form \(\theta = \nabla_\mu J_E^\mu\) now reads \(\theta = \nabla_\mu J_{\mathrm{geom}}^\mu\), including \(\partial_\tau\ln\Omega = \theta/n\) (\(O_2\) Eq. [2]) and the \(u_{\mathrm{exp}}\) flux identity (\(O_7\) Eq. [3]); statements of \(\theta > 0\) carry the qualifier "\(> 0\) in Branch I". Three currents are distinguished: * \(J_{\mathrm{geom}}^\mu\), the expansion current: \(\theta = \nabla_\mu J_{\mathrm{geom}}^\mu \ge 0\) and \(dV_{\mathcal{M}}/d\tau = \oint J_{\mathrm{geom}}^\mu\,d\Sigma_\mu\). * \(J_W^\alpha = -\mathfrak{K}^{\alpha\beta}\nabla_\beta\mathcal{F}\), the free-energy transport current of \(O_4\). * \(J_E^\mu\), the throughput current entering \(O_0\) Eq. In Branch I both \(\theta\) and \(\Theta_E\) drive \(\tau_\Theta\) (r6 stated no such clock), and in Branch II \(\Theta_E\) alone does. Power splits into expansion work \(\dot{W}_{\mathrm{ext}} = p_0\dot{V}_{\mathcal{M}} \ge 0\) (unchanged symbol and formula, \(> 0\) in Branch I) and dissipative power \(\dot{W}_{\mathrm{diss}} = \int(-J_E^\mu\nabla_\mu\mathcal{F})\sqrt{\vert g\vert}\,d^nx > 0\), with \(\dot{W}_{\mathrm{tot}} > 0\).     5.5 Relational edge¶

    • Local potentials. \(\Phi^{(1)} = \Phi^{(2)} = \frac{1}{2}\left((x^1)^2 - (x^2)^2\right)\) on all of \(\mathcal{M}\). Then \(\hat{\Pi}^{(j)}_{\mu\nu} = \mathrm{diag}(1,-1,0)\), which is already \(g\)-trace-free.

    • Derived 1-forms. \(\eta_{[1\to 2]} = a\,\partial_1 \not\equiv 0\). \(J_{1\to 2} + J_{2\to 1} = 0\), so the first term of \(M^{(12)}\) vanishes. \(J_{[1\to 2]} = a\,dx^1\), and \(F^{(1\to 2)} = F^{(2\to 1)} = 0\).
    • Coupling tensor. \(S^{(12)}_{\mu\nu} = M^{(12)}_{\mu\nu} = \gamma_M a^2\left(\delta_{\mu 1}\delta_{\nu 1} - \frac{1}{3}\delta_{\mu\nu}\right)\).
    • It is trace-free and nonzero at every point of \(\bar{E}\).
    • Its mixed eigenvalues are \(e^{-2H_0\tau}\gamma_M a^2\,(\tfrac{2}{3}, -\tfrac{1}{3}, -\tfrac{1}{3})\), so \(m_+ > 0 > m_-\) with \(m_+ \neq -m_-\).
    • Boundary flux. With \(\hat{n}^\mu = e^{-H_0\tau}\nu^\mu\) and \(dA = e^{2H_0\tau}dA_\delta\):
    • \(\oint_{\partial E} S^{(12)}_{\mu\nu}\hat{n}^\mu\hat{n}^\nu \, dA = \gamma_M a(\tau)^2\,C_E\), where \(C_E \equiv \oint_{\partial E}(\nu_1^2 - \tfrac{1}{3}) \, dA_\delta\).

    • For an oblate \(E\) with short axis along \(x^1\), \(C_E > 0\); numerically, \(C_E \approx 3.64\) for semi-axes \((0.2, 1, 1)\).
    • Subsystem densities. \(Q_1 = 1/\mathrm{Vol}_g(\mathcal{M})\) and \(Q_2 = 1/\mathrm{Vol}_g(E)\) (uniform). Then \(Q_1^{\vert 12} = Q_2^{\vert 12}\) (uniform on \(E\)), so \(D_{\mathrm{KL}}(Q_1^{\vert 12} \parallel Q_2^{\vert 12}) = 0\) for all \(\tau\).

    • Joint density (sketch). Partition \(\mathcal{M}\) and \(E\) into \(m\) cells each, of equal relative volume. Set \(Q_{12} = (1-w)\,Q_1 \otimes Q_2 + w\,m\,(Q_1 \otimes Q_2)\,\mathbf{1}_{\text{matched cells}}\).
    • For every \(w \in [0,1]\) this has exact marginals \(Q_1, Q_2\) and satisfies \(Q_{12} \ll Q_1 \otimes Q_2\).
    • Its mutual information increases continuously from \(0\) to \(\ln m\) as \(w\) goes from 0 to 1.
    • Interpolating between nested refinements \(m \to km\) in the same way extends the range to \([0, \infty)\), so a choice of \((m, w)\) as a function of \(\tau\) realizes the prescribed \(\mathcal{I}(\tau)\). The densities are piecewise constant, which the specification permits (no regularity is imposed on \(Q_{ij}\)).
    • Saturation and volume share. Setting \(s_0 = k_{\mathcal{I}}\gamma_M a_0^2 C_E / (3H_0\,\mathrm{Vol}_\delta(\mathcal{M}))\) gives \(\mathcal{I}/(s_0 V_{\mathcal{M}}) \to 1\). Also \(V_{1\cup 2}/V_{\mathcal{M}} = 1 \in (0,1]\).
