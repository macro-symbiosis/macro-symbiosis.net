    0. Tier Architecture & Four-Part Functional Homology¶

    Tier 4 maps the mechanics and nutrition of load-bearing connective tissue, the physiology of balance and support contact, neuro-attentional and autonomic regulation, and the regulation of affect through the microcirculation onto Spine \(\mathcal{T}\). Three apertures (4.1, 4.2, 4.4) are empirical apertures declared in Branch II of r7, the bounded non-equilibrium steady state (aperture_generation_guidance.md §2.3). Each declares, in its §1, a domain, physical metric, cone speed, storage coefficient, free energy with datum, transport tensor, interior sink, drive, and Branch II evidence, and instantiates all nine coordinates \(O_0 \to O_\Omega\) on that domain. Each declared domain is of one kind: a stored quantity (dissolved oxygen, or blood held in a compliant capillary bed) spreading through a living tissue, with supply crossing the boundary and consumption or loss, where declared, by a named interior sink. Aperture 4.3 is provisionally homological in this epoch: it declares no domain, maps all nine coordinates as conceptual correspondences, like Tier 1, and makes no empirical claim (§0.1 item 12).

    The wider phenomena named in the aperture titles (posture, balance, attention, autonomic regulation, affect, resilience) enter the empirical mappings as boundary conditions, through the declared parameters, through the hysteresis record \(\mathcal{H}\), or as illustrative context, and each §1 states which. No Tier 4 aperture claims that attention, affect, or any interior state obeys a spine law. Each aperture is formulated at the level of general physiology, biomechanics, and neuroscience, for vertebrate and mammalian tissues and for human populations studied at cohort level, without presupposing a single practitioner, practice, or site. No human-subject test is included in this epoch (empirical_validation_protocol.md §3.3).

    0.1 Conventions Common to the Tier 4 Apertures¶

    The following conventions apply to every empirical aperture of this tier (4.1, 4.2, 4.4); item 12 states how the provisionally homological Aperture 4.3 is mapped. Each aperture's §1 and coordinate blocks state its specific values and cite these items where they apply. Where an item repeats a convention of tier_3_ecological_living_networks.md §0.1, it is restated here so that this tier can be read on its own.

    1. Branch and windows. Branch II is declared before any test, with the estimators of item 7 and §1, and no Tier 4 domain is re-evaluated in Branch I on the same data (closure (v)). Statements that hold in Branch I only (r10 Decision 6) are never applied. Every observation window is a relaxation window: it begins at a declared \(\tau_0\) from a prior steady state, a declared transient starts at \(\tau_0\), the external data are then held within declared tolerances, and the window follows the domain as it settles.
    2. In every empirical aperture the transient is a declared step of the boundary data in the direction that raises \(\mathcal{F}\): a step increase of the supply-chamber oxygen (4.1), of the oxygen supplied at the dermal–epidermal junction (4.2), or of the pressure at the arteriolar boundary (4.4). Each declared balance law is a scalar parabolic equation whose sink is Lipschitz and non-decreasing in \(\mathcal{F}\) (Michaelis–Menten, first order), so the comparison principle holds. The prior steady state is a subsolution for the raised data, so \(\mathcal{F}(\cdot, \tau_0 + h) \ge \mathcal{F}(\cdot, \tau_0)\); the held data make the problem invariant under time translation, so \(\mathcal{F}(\cdot, \tau + h) \ge \mathcal{F}(\cdot, \tau)\), that is, \(\partial_\tau\mathcal{F} \ge 0\) at every point. Where the step is in fact a ramp (non-decreasing boundary data that reach their held value over a finite rise time, as for the in-vivo supply of 4.2 and the arteriolar dilation of 4.4), the same conclusion follows without time translation: \(\partial_\tau\mathcal{F}\) obeys the linearized equation with zero initial data and non-negative boundary data, so it stays non-negative. Robin data (4.2's outflow face) do not change the argument, and the duct lumens of 4.2, which receive oxygen only from the tissue, form with it a cooperative system, for which the comparison principle also holds. The argument is exact on rigid domains. Under the optional torsional perturbation of 4.1 the operator depends on \(\tau\) through \(g(\tau)\) at the order of the shear strain, so there the conclusion holds only to that order, and 4.1 checks it from its oxygen record.
    3. Direction. The opposite direction is excluded in each aperture, because it would break a declaration. In 4.4 a fall of capillary pressure shrinks the tissue (\(\theta < 0\)), which alone excludes it; physically it can also turn filtration into reabsorption, an interior source, which the declared datum form of the sink cannot represent (4.4 §1). In 4.2 a change of contact load compresses the epidermis (\(\theta < 0\)) or, on unloading, is followed by a reactive hyperaemia whose overshoot makes the supply data non-monotone. In 4.1 an oxygen step is osmotically negligible, so either direction keeps the volume; the raising direction is declared for the comparison argument, and a glucose step is not declared because it changes the bath's osmolarity, and a rise of osmolarity deswells cartilage (\(\theta < 0\)).
    4. Boundary and lateral exchange. Every exchange of the declared field across an internal or lateral boundary is one-signed, out of \(\mathcal{M}\): transcapillary filtration in 4.4 is a sink and never reabsorption (4.4 §1), and exchange across the walls of the epidermal ducts in 4.2 runs from the tissue into lumens that receive oxygen from nowhere else (4.2 §1). No compartment outside \(\mathcal{M}\) discharges into it within a window, except across the declared supply faces.
    5. The rate of change of the flux, \(\partial_\tau J_W\), is not one-signed in general (item 9).
    6. Metric. The physical metric is the pull-back of the Euclidean metric to the material points of the declared body under its deformation map. It is flat at every \(\tau\), so \(G_{\mu\nu} \equiv 0\) and \(R_{\mu\nu} \equiv 0\) (aperture_generation_guidance.md §2.3 item 2). No Tier 4 aperture declares a non-Euclidean material metric in this epoch: the growth or remodelling material metric that the master table admits for 4.1 is weighed in 4.1 §1 and not declared. The curved configurations of the r10 §5.11 and §5.13 witnesses are therefore realized by none of these apertures, and r7's curvature sector is exercised by no Tier 4 aperture; with Tiers 2 and 3, it is exercised by no empirical aperture of Tiers 2–4.
    7. Rigid domains (4.2, 4.4). In a Cartesian chart \(g_{\mu\nu} = \delta_{\mu\nu}\), so \(\Omega \equiv 1\), \(\hat{h} \equiv 0\), \(\theta \equiv 0\), and \(\sigma^\mu_{\;\nu} \equiv 0\). The tissue is declared rigid within a pre-declared tolerance on its measured volumetric strain. In the declared window direction any residual volume change is non-negative (epidermal thickness under held load in 4.2; vascular distension and filtration in 4.4), and windows in which the measured volume decreases beyond the tolerance are excluded, so that \(\theta \ge 0\) is not violated. With \(\sigma \equiv 0\) the \(O_3\) shear law holds trivially, and these apertures support no shear-relaxation test.
    8. Volume-rigid domain (4.1). The volumetric strain of the explant stays within a declared tolerance, so \(\theta \equiv 0\) in the declared metric. Without the optional torsional perturbation, the shear strain also stays within tolerance and 4.1 is treated as rigid. With it, the metric is the pull-back under an isochoric twist: in a Cartesian material chart \(g(\tau) = \mathbf{F}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{F}\) with \(\det\mathbf{F} = 1\), so \(\Omega \equiv 1\), \(\theta \equiv 0\), \(\exp\hat{h} = g\), and \(\sigma^\mu_{\;\nu} \neq 0\). On that deforming domain every covariant quantity (Laplacian, Hessians, convexity) is evaluated with \(g(\tau)\).
    9. Storage. The r7 \(O_4\) balance law carries unit storage: \(\partial_\tau\mathcal{F}\) is the rate of change of the stored quantity per unit volume. A living medium stores its diffusing quantity \(u\) with a coefficient \(s\) (stored amount per unit volume per unit of \(u\)): for dissolved oxygen in 4.1 and 4.2, the tissue's water volume fraction times the partition coefficient of oxygen between tissue and bath water, with \(u\) the bath-equivalent oxygen concentration (oxygen tension times its solubility in bath water), which is continuous across the tissue's faces; in 4.4, the compliance of the capillary bed per unit tissue volume. With \(s\) uniform, the physical balance \(s\,\partial_\tau u = \nabla_\alpha(k^{\alpha\beta}\nabla_\beta u) - r\) becomes the r7 law for \(\mathcal{F} = \epsilon u\) (with \(\epsilon\) a declared constant yield), \(\mathfrak{K} = k/s\), and \(\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{F}} = \epsilon r/s\), and the r7 flux is \(J_W = (\epsilon/s)\,j\), with \(j = -k\nabla u\) the physical flux. The factor \(\epsilon/s\) is a declared conversion factor, estimated independently (closure (ii)). If \(s\) varies in space, \(s^{-1}\nabla_\alpha(k^{\alpha\beta}\nabla_\beta u) = \nabla_\alpha(s^{-1}k^{\alpha\beta}\nabla_\beta u) + s^{-2}k^{\alpha\beta}\nabla_\alpha s\,\nabla_\beta u\), and the last term is a drift term that the r7 balance law does not contain. Each aperture therefore declares a single storage class: \(s\) uniform on \(\mathcal{M}\) within a tolerance (a declared, checkable precondition). Adjacent media with a different coefficient (other zones of the cartilage in 4.1; the stratum corneum and the dermis in 4.2; arterioles, venules, and the interstitium in 4.4) lie outside \(\mathcal{M}\) and enter as boundary data or compartments, because a jump in \(s\) would make \(J_W\) discontinuous where the physical flux is continuous, which the unit-storage law reads as an interface source or sink.
    10. Stress sector. With \(G_{\mu\nu} \equiv 0\), \(T_{\mu\nu}^{\mathrm{mat}} \equiv 0\); its trace gives \(\nabla_\alpha\xi^\alpha = 0\) for \(n = 3\), and then \(\nabla_{(\mu}\xi_{\nu)} = 0\), so \(\xi\) is a Killing field. No Tier 4 medium has an identified global control field in this epoch, so Tier 4 declares \(\eta^\lambda \equiv 0\), hence \(\xi_\nu \equiv 0\) and \(R = \kappa_G\nabla_\alpha\xi^\alpha = 0\), and the \(O_4\) equivalence \(\nabla^2\xi_\nu + R_\nu^{\;\alpha}\xi_\alpha = 0 \iff \nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu}^{\mathrm{mat}} = 0\) holds identically. The \(\tau\)-compatibility law then reduces to \(\mathbb{L}_{\mathrm{Ein}}[\sigma]_{\mu\nu} = \frac{n-2}{n}(\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu\theta - g_{\mu\nu}\nabla^2\theta)\), and its trace, the traced elliptic equation, to \(\frac{n-1}{n}\nabla^2\theta = \nabla^\alpha\nabla^\beta\sigma_{\alpha\beta}\). These are the Saint-Venant compatibility conditions of the strain rate, which every deformation of an embedded body satisfies because its metric stays flat; with \(\theta \equiv 0\) (all Tier 4 domains) they read \(\mathbb{L}_{\mathrm{Ein}}[\sigma] = 0\) and \(\nabla^\alpha\nabla^\beta\sigma_{\alpha\beta} = 0\). The mechanical stresses of tissues (the swelling prestress of cartilage, myofascial tension, contact pressure, vessel-wall stress) are not \(T_{\mu\nu}^{\mathrm{mat}}\); they enter as boundary conditions, through the declared parameters, and through \(\mathcal{H}\). The partner fields \(\eta_{i\to j}\) of \(O_6\) (item 9) are distinct objects from the global \(\eta\) of \(O_4\).
    11. Cone speed and trajectories. Each aperture declares \(c_*\) as the longitudinal sound speed of its tissue. Rays of that signal are null for the declared cone and, like all null rays, are not trajectories of \(O_2\) Eq. [3]. Every material speed in \(\mathcal{M}\) (diffusive speeds, twist-recovery speeds, blood velocities) is \(\ll c_*\), so \(u^2 \ll 1\). The trajectory equation is displayed in physical units: both \(u^2 = g_{\mu\nu}v^\mu v^\nu/c_*^2\) and the projection term \(c_*^{-2}(g_{\alpha\beta}a^\alpha_{\mathrm{d}}v^\beta)v^\mu\) carry \(c_*\), and with \(c_* = 1\) the display reduces to the r7 form. All three empirical apertures instantiate Eq. [3] with \(a^\mu_{\mathrm{d}} = 0\) and map no tracer. Bodily movement, postural sway, and the motion of blood cells are forced motions that no declared drive describes in this epoch.
    12. Currents. In Branch II, \(J_E^\mu = J_W^\mu/p_0\) is fixed. The expansion current \(J_{\mathrm{geom}}\) enters only through its divergence \(\theta\) and its boundary flux \(dV_{\mathcal{M}}/d\tau\) (r7 \(O_0\) branch scope), and no supply current is ever mapped to it. In general \(\nabla_\mu J_E^\mu = -p_0^{-1}(\partial_\tau\mathcal{F} + \mathcal{F}\theta + \mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{F}}) \neq \theta\). Breathing volume, cardiac stroke volume, and the pulsation of vessels are cyclic volume changes outside the declared fields and are never mapped to \(\theta\).
    13. Measured dissipation. The Branch II evidence requires \(\lim\langle\Theta_E\rangle > 0\) to be estimated from measured dissipation, never from \(\mathfrak{K}\nabla\mathcal{F}\). Integrating \(-J_E^\mu\nabla_\mu\mathcal{F}\) by parts and using the \(O_4\) balance law gives the identity $\(p_0\dot{W}_{\mathrm{diss}} = \oint_{\partial\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{F}\,(-J_W^\mu)\,d\Sigma_\mu - \int_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{F}\,\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{F}}\sqrt{\vert g\vert}\,d^nx - \frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{d\tau}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{F}^2\sqrt{\vert g\vert}\,d^nx - \frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{F}^2\theta\sqrt{\vert g\vert}\,d^nx\)$
    14. Where an outflow face is held at the datum \(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{ref}} = 0\) (4.1's receiving face, 4.4's venular boundary), it contributes nothing. The outflow face of 4.2 (the base of the stratum corneum) is not at the datum, and its term is evaluated from the measured outflow and the measured \(\mathcal{F}\) there; the terms of its duct walls are bounded, not measured (4.2 §1).
    15. Each term is evaluated from measured \(\mathcal{F}\), measured boundary fluxes, and measured or independently declared consumption; none uses the declared \(\mathfrak{K}\). Each §1 names instruments and surfaces different from those of the \(O_1\) flux-prediction test, so that the evidence is independent of that test; 4.2 states where this independence is weaker.
    16. Because the identity uses the balance law, the estimate is conditional on it. Each §1 also names a directly measured physical dissipation whose sign the estimate must share. Every such dissipation is non-negative by the second law, so this comparison tests only the sign of the identity-based estimate, and it is stated as such. Only signs and \(s_0\)-independent rates of \(\Theta_E\) are tested.
    17. Gate sector (\(O_4\)). \(\rho_{\mathrm{total}}(\tau)\) is a declared positive trace-class state on \(L^2(\mathcal{M}, d\mathrm{vol}_g)\), and \(Q\), \(P\), and the \(O_6\) marginals \(Q_i\) and joints \(Q_{ij}\) are densities of this declared state sector. No Tier 4 aperture identifies them with \(\mathcal{F}\) or with any measured field in this epoch, and none makes an empirical claim about the gate (empirical_validation_protocol.md §3.3). What each domain's physics supplies is, first, the spectral setting: the Neumann eigenmodes of \(-\nabla^2_g\) on \(\mathcal{M}\), whose eigenvalues are fixed on rigid domains, drift by a fraction of the order of the shear strain under the torsional perturbation of 4.1, and converge as \(g \to G_\infty\). Second, it supplies a physical low-pass behaviour of \(\mathcal{F}\): under the balance law, fine spatial structure in \(\mathcal{F}\) relaxes faster than coarse structure. That behaviour is mapped via Spine \(\mathcal{T}\) to the gate and is not itself the gate. Temporal smoothing of fluctuating supply by the tissue is a property in time and is not the spatial gate. Attentional filtering is not a property of these domains; it is mapped homologically in 4.3 and never identified with the gate of an empirical aperture.
    18. Cover, overlaps, and coupling (\(O_5\), \(O_6\)).
    19. The cover \(\{\mathcal{U}_i\}\) consists of \(\tau\)-independent material subregions of \(\mathcal{M}\) with Lipschitz boundaries, and every overlap \(\mathcal{U}_{ij}\) is convex at \(\tau_0\). Where \(\partial\mathcal{M}\) has concave parts (the undulating dermal–epidermal junction and the duct walls in 4.2; the walls of the excised vessels in 4.4), each concave part lies in a single cover set, which need not be convex, and every overlap stays clear of the concave parts: r7 requires convex overlaps, not convex cover sets. Each \(g^{(i)}\) is the restriction of the one physical metric, so \(g^{(i)} = g^{(j)}\) on every overlap, the geometric mean returns the common metric (\(g \# g = g\)), and the gluing of \(O_5\) Eq. [3] holds identically.
    20. The anisotropy of a tissue lives in \(\mathfrak{K}\), never in \(g\). Effective-medium averages of diffusivities or conductances, including geometric-mean averages, are statements about \(\mathfrak{K}\) and are not mapped to \(O_5\) Eq. [1].
    21. Partner fields. In every empirical aperture the partner fields are the rate of change of the free-energy flux read in the two orientations, \(\eta_{i\to j}^\lambda = \partial_\tau J_W^\lambda\) and \(\eta_{j\to i}^\lambda = -\partial_\tau J_W^\lambda\), defined on all of \(\mathcal{M}\), so \(\eta_{[i\to j]}^\lambda = \partial_\tau J_W^\lambda\). They decay with the transient, which gives the integrable coupling that Branch II requires; a steady flux would give \(\eta \equiv 0\), and a sustained coupling field is never declared.
    22. Zero sets. \(\partial_\tau J_W\) is not one-signed in general.     Aperture 4.2: Vestibular Equilibrium & Sensorimotor Contact (\(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{kinesthetic}}\))¶

    1. Epistemological & Physiological Scope¶

    Aperture 4.2 maps the physiology of balance and of the body's contact with its support onto Spine \(\mathcal{T}\): vestibular sensing of head motion and gravity, the sensorimotor control that keeps the centre of mass over the base of support, cutaneous sensing of contact, and the load that balance places on the skin of the contact. Its empirical domain is the oxygen supply of the skin under that load: oxygen from the dermal capillaries diffuses across the dermal–epidermal junction into the avascular epidermis and is consumed by keratinocytes, while the support occludes the skin from the air. Lexicon is strictly restricted to vestibular physiology, posturography, sensorimotor control, cutaneous physiology, and contact mechanics.

    Declaration (aperture_generation_guidance.md §2.3; Branch II).

    • Domain \(\mathcal{M}\) (\(n = 3\)) and boundary \(\partial\mathcal{M}\). The viable epidermis (the basal, spinous, and granular layers) of a load-bearing skin site in contact with a support, over a declared contact area, in one of two forms:
    • in vivo, at a support contact under a held load;
    • ex vivo, an epidermal sheet separated from its dermis, whose basal face is bathed by a medium at held oxygen and whose outer face rests on the support under a held load.

    \(\partial\mathcal{M}\) consists of: * the dermal–epidermal junction (supply), across which oxygen enters from the papillary capillaries (in vivo) or the bathing medium (ex vivo); it undulates around the dermal papillae; * the outer face of the viable epidermis, at the base of the stratum corneum (outflow), across which oxygen passes through the stratum corneum to the support; * the walls of the epidermal ducts of sweat glands (and, at hair-bearing sites, of follicles) that pierce the epidermis (outflow, see below); * lateral faces at the edge of the declared contact area, closed within tolerance (ex vivo by a seal; in vivo by lateral uniformity of the contact, precondition 3).

    The stratum corneum is a different storage class (its water fraction is far lower) and lies outside \(\mathcal{M}\) as a thin barrier compartment. The support is occlusive and holds the skin-surface oxygen at the datum (for example a contact plate flushed with an oxygen-free gas). Occlusion removes the supply of oxygen from the air, so all supply crosses the dermal–epidermal junction; the datum at the surface gives the field an outflow, without which the occluded face would be a zero-flux face on which \(\nabla\mathcal{F} = 0\). The dermis, its capillaries, and the support lie outside \(\mathcal{M}\). The epidermis contains no capillaries and no oxygen-producing process, so there are no interior sources. Balance control and contact loading enter as boundary conditions and through \(\mathcal{H}\) (below). * Duct walls. The duct lumens lie outside \(\mathcal{M}\). With no sweat secretion within the window (precondition 5), the lumen fluid is static and receives oxygen only from the surrounding epidermis, and it drains toward the surface at the datum; exchange across a duct wall is therefore one-signed, out of \(\mathcal{M}\), and never a source. These fluxes are not measured. Their total is bounded by a declared estimate (duct density, lumen cross-section, and the lumen's diffusive conductance to the surface) and must lie below the flux tolerance. * Window. A relaxation window (§0.1 item 1), opened from a prior steady state by a declared increase of the oxygen supplied at the dermal–epidermal junction, after which the supply data, the contact load, and skin temperature are held within tolerance. The contact load is applied before the prior steady state, and the skin's creep under it is complete within tolerance before \(\tau_0\) (precondition 6). Ex vivo the increase is a step of the bathing medium's oxygen.     Aperture 4.3: Neuro-Attentional & Autonomic Regulation (\(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{neuro}}\); provisionally homological)¶

    1. Epistemological & Physiological Scope¶

    Aperture 4.3 maps how nervous systems select and regulate: attention, which selects among competing signals and sets their gain; arousal and its neuromodulatory control; and the autonomic nervous system, whose sympathetic and parasympathetic branches regulate the internal milieu through reflex loops such as the baroreflex, and whose afferents carry interoceptive signals to the brain. Lexicon is strictly restricted to cognitive neuroscience, electrophysiology, neurodynamics, and autonomic and interoceptive physiology.

    Status (aperture_generation_guidance.md §2.3; provisionally homological). This aperture declares no empirical domain and makes no empirical claim in this epoch (§0.1 item 12). The systems it describes do not fit r7's balance law literally. The electrical potentials of neural tissue are quasi-static: in volume conduction the potential follows its current sources without a storage term, so it is not a stored field with a finite relaxation time, which aperture_generation_guidance.md §2.3 item 3 requires. Attention and autonomic regulation are, besides, properties of whole circuits and of the body, not fields on one declared tissue domain. Each coordinate is therefore mapped as a conceptual correspondence, mapped via Spine \(\mathcal{T}\) and never a reductive identity. Populations are described at cohort level; practices that train attention or regulate arousal are context and are not mapped. Test 2 (spatial gating) remains deferred while this aperture is homological (Annex B).

    Aperture 4.4: Affective Regulation & Psychophysiological Resilience (\(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{affective}}\))¶

    1. Epistemological & Physiological Scope¶

    Aperture 4.4 maps the bodily side of affect onto Spine \(\mathcal{T}\): the autonomic expression of emotion and arousal, its regulation, and the capacity of physiological systems to recover after stressors (psychophysiological resilience). A principal route by which affective and autonomic states act on tissues is the tone of small arteries and arterioles, which sets how blood is distributed through the microcirculation. Its empirical domain is the perfusion of a capillary bed: blood enters from the feeding arterioles, spreads through the capillary network, which stores blood in its compliance, and leaves through the draining venules, while part of the plasma filters into the tissue. Lexicon is strictly restricted to affective neuroscience, autonomic psychophysiology, and microvascular physiology.

    Declaration (aperture_generation_guidance.md §2.3; Branch II).

    • Domain \(\mathcal{M}\) (\(n = 3\)) and boundary \(\partial\mathcal{M}\). A tissue region containing a capillary bed, treated at the network scale as a porous medium, with the lumens and walls of the feeding arterioles and draining venules that run through it excised from \(\mathcal{M}\), together with any larger vessels. The capillaries and the post-capillary segments of a single compliance class form the medium. \(\partial\mathcal{M}\) consists of:
    • the arteriolar boundary (supply): the walls of the terminal arterioles where the capillaries arise, across which blood enters at a pressure set by arteriolar tone;
    • the venular boundary (outflow at the datum): the walls of the collecting venules into which the capillaries drain, at venular pressure \(p_{\mathrm{ven}}\), held within tolerance;
    • the remaining boundary, closed: tissue surfaces, or surfaces across which no blood crosses within tolerance, located by a declared perfusion measurement.

    All supply crosses the arteriolar boundary; the capillary bed contains no other inflow, so there are no interior sources. The arterioles, venules, and interstitium have compliance or storage of a different class (§0.1 item 3) and lie outside \(\mathcal{M}\); the interstitium receives the filtrate, which lymphatics drain outside \(\mathcal{M}\). The domain may be observed in vivo by intravital microscopy, or in an isolated perfused tissue preparation. * Window. A relaxation window (§0.1 item 1), opened from a prior steady state by a declared step (or monotone ramp) increase of the pressure at the arteriolar boundary, after which arteriolar pressure, venular pressure, and temperature are held within tolerance. The increase may come from dilation of the feeding arterioles (withdrawal of vasoconstrictor tone, or a vasodilator applied to the feeding arterioles only) or, in a perfused preparation, from a raised perfusion pressure. Capillary pressure then rises at every point, so filtration rises and remains a sink, and the tissue's volume change is non-negative (vascular distension and filtrate). Windows that open with a fall of arteriolar pressure are excluded, because falling capillary pressure shrinks the tissue (\(\theta < 0\)); that alone justifies the exclusion. Physically, a fall can also turn filtration into reabsorption from the interstitium, an interior source. The declared datum form cannot show this: with \(p_{\mathrm{f}} = p_{\mathrm{ven}}\) and \(p \ge p_{\mathrm{ven}}\) the declared filtration never reverses. This second reason therefore rests on the physical Starling balance, not on the declared law, and it is why the declared form would misrepresent such a window. Windows in which myogenic or metabolic autoregulation returns the arteriolar pressure beyond tolerance are excluded. * Metric. Flat and rigid (§0.1 item 2): \(g_{\mu\nu} = \delta_{\mu\nu}\) in a Cartesian chart, with the tissue volume, measured by plethysmography or tissue thickness, within the rigid tolerance; any residual change is non-negative in the declared direction. * Cone speed. \(c_*\) is the longitudinal sound speed of the tissue. Blood velocities in the microcirculation are \(\ll c_*\). * Storage. \(C_{\mathrm{v}}\): the compliance of the capillary bed per unit tissue volume, the blood volume stored per unit volume per unit rise of capillary pressure (m³ m⁻³ Pa⁻¹). It is declared uniform over \(\mathcal{M}\) within tolerance and linear over the window's range of pressures (§0.1 item 3). * Free energy \(\mathcal{F}\) and datum. \(\mathcal{F} = p - p_{\mathrm{ven}}\) (J m⁻³), with \(p\) the network-scale capillary blood pressure. The datum is the venular pressure, held at the venular boundary. Blood flows from the arteriolar to the venular boundary, so \(p \ge p_{\mathrm{ven}}\) throughout \(\mathcal{M}\) is a checkable precondition (precondition 2), not a triviality. \(\mathcal{F}\) is linear in the stored blood: the blood stored per unit volume above the datum is \(C_{\mathrm{v}}\mathcal{F}\), so \(\mathcal{F}\) is that stored blood times the declared conversion factor \(1/C_{\mathrm{v}}\). The relaxation time \(\ell^2C_{\mathrm{v}}/k_{\mathrm{b}}\) is finite, and it is required to exceed the time resolution of the pressure instruments by a declared factor (precondition 8). * Transport \(\mathfrak{K}\). \(\mathfrak{K}^{\alpha\beta} = k_{\mathrm{b}}^{\alpha\beta}/C_{\mathrm{v}}\), with \(k_{\mathrm{b}}^{\alpha\beta}\) the network-scale (Darcy) conductance tensor of the capillary bed for blood at its effective viscosity, parametric: for example one conductance along and one across the dominant capillary orientation (as along muscle fibres), with isotropic conductance as the default. With \(q\) the Darcy blood flux (m s⁻¹), \(J_W = q/C_{\mathrm{v}}\); \(1/C_{\mathrm{v}}\) is the declared conversion factor. * Sink \(\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{F}}\). Transcapillary filtration, first order relative to the datum: \(\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{F}} = k_{\mathrm{f}}(\mathcal{F} - \mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{ref}}) \ge 0\), with \(k_{\mathrm{f}} = L_{\mathrm{p}}S_{\mathrm{v}}/C_{\mathrm{v}}\) (\(L_{\mathrm{p}}\) the hydraulic conductivity of the capillary wall, \(S_{\mathrm{v}}\) the capillary surface area per unit tissue volume). The physical filtration follows the Starling balance of hydrostatic and oncotic pressures across the wall, which vanishes at some capillary pressure \(p_{\mathrm{f}}\); the first-order datum form declares \(p_{\mathrm{f}} = p_{\mathrm{ven}}\) within tolerance, a declared idealization. It is admitted only under the binding closure, and it is rejected if the default fits at least as well (closure (iv)). \(L_{\mathrm{p}}S_{\mathrm{v}}\) is estimated per closure (ii) by a declared method on the same tissue (for example the filtration response to venous pressure steps before the window, a declared perturbation regime), and \(p_{\mathrm{f}}\) is checked against \(p_{\mathrm{ven}}\) in the same regime. * Drive. Default, \(a^\mu_{\mathrm{d}} = 0\) (§0.1 item 5). Red cells move with the blood under pressure and drag; no drag drive is declared, and no cell is mapped as a tracer. * Branch II evidence (closure (v)). * The trend of \(\langle\theta\rangle\) and a bound on \(\int\langle\theta\rangle\,d\tau\) come from the measured tissue volume, which must stay within the rigid tolerance. * \(\lim\langle\Theta_E\rangle > 0\) is estimated from measured dissipation (§0.1 item 7): * the supply term uses the inflow through the feeding arterioles (red-cell velocimetry times lumen cross-section in vivo, or the perfusion flow of a perfused preparation) and \(\mathcal{F}\) at the arteriolar boundary from servo-null micropressure measurement; * the venular boundary, at the datum, contributes nothing; * the consumption term uses the declared, independently estimated \(k_{\mathrm{f}}\) with \(\mathcal{F}\) from a second set of capillary pressure measurements; * the storage term uses repeated pressure measurements.

    The $O_1$ test uses an interior section and its own instruments.

    • Titled phenomena outside the domain.
    • Affect and its regulation. Emotions, their appraisal, and their regulation are processes of the person, outside \(\mathcal{M}\); they are context. Their autonomic expression reaches the bed through sympathetic vasoconstrictor tone on the feeding arterioles, which sets the arteriolar boundary data. Within windows those data are held, so affect enters only through their held level and through the step that opens the window.
    • Autonomic rhythms. Heart-rate variability, respiratory modulation, and vasomotion are temporal quantities; they are excluded from windows by tolerance and are never mapped to \(\theta\), \(\sigma\), or any spatial operator.
    • Psychophysiological resilience. Recovery of physiological variables after a stressor is a property of the person's regulatory systems, outside \(\mathcal{M}\). It is not mapped to \(\gamma_\sigma\), to the relaxation of the pressure field, or to any rate of this aperture. Repeated stress shapes the microvasculature between windows (remodelling, changes of capillary density), which enters through the declared parameters and is mapped via Spine \(\mathcal{T}\) to \(\mathcal{H}\), not identified with it.
    • Lymphatic drainage and oedema. Drainage of the filtrate and the accumulation of interstitial fluid lie outside \(\mathcal{M}\); windows end before interstitial pressure changes beyond tolerance (precondition 2).

    \(O_0\) — Exogenous Flux & Emergent Foliation Time¶

    • Spine Equations: \(\theta \equiv -N K \overset{N=1,\,\beta^\mu=0}{=} \frac{1}{2}g^{\mu\nu}\frac{\partial g_{\mu\nu}}{\partial\tau} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\vert g\vert}}\frac{\partial\sqrt{\vert g\vert}}{\partial\tau} = \nabla_\mu J_{\mathrm{geom}}^\mu \ge 0\); \(\Theta_E \equiv \frac{\dot{s}_{\mathrm{prod}}}{s_0} = -\frac{\beta_{\mathrm{th}}}{s_0}J_E^\mu\nabla_\mu\mathcal{F}\); \(\Theta_{\mathrm{tot}} \equiv \sqrt{\theta^2 + \Theta_E^2}\), \(\tau_\Theta(\tau) \equiv \int_{\tau_0}^{\tau}\langle\Theta_{\mathrm{tot}}\rangle_{\mathcal{M}}\,d\tau'\); \(\frac{dV_{\mathcal{M}}}{d\tau} = \int_{\mathcal{M}}\theta\sqrt{\vert g\vert}\,d^nx = \oint_{\partial\mathcal{M}}J_{\mathrm{geom}}^\mu\,d\Sigma_\mu \ge 0\), with \(\lim_{\tau\to\infty}V_{\mathcal{M}}(\tau) = V_\infty < \infty\) (Branch II); \(\frac{\partial\mathcal{H}}{\partial\tau} \equiv J_E^\mu\nabla_\mu q\), \(q(\mathbf{x}, \tau) \equiv -\ln(v_0Q(\mathbf{x}, \tau))\).
    • Physical Instantiation: The tissue is rigid within tolerance, so \(\theta \equiv 0\), \(dV_{\mathcal{M}}/d\tau = 0\), and \(V_{\mathcal{M}} = V_\infty\). The blood entering across the arteriolar boundary is throughput and is never mapped to \(J_{\mathrm{geom}}\) (§0.1 item 6); the cyclic pulsation of vessels with the heartbeat is not \(\theta\). Viscous flow of blood down its pressure gradient dissipates energy at every point, so \(\Theta_E > 0\) (by \(O_1\)), \(\Theta_{\mathrm{tot}} = \Theta_E\), and \(\tau_\Theta\) counts entropy production per \(s_0\). The record accumulates by \(\partial_\tau\mathcal{H} = J_E^\mu\nabla_\mu q\) along the throughput current. The bed's structural adaptation (capillary density, vessel calibre, the history of its tone) is mapped via Spine \(\mathcal{T}\) to \(\mathcal{H}\) and is not identified with it; it enters each window through \(\mathcal{H}_0\).

    \(O_1\) — Non-Equilibrium Thermodynamic Openness¶

    • Spine Equations: \(\dot{W}_{\mathrm{ext}} = p_0\dot{V}_{\mathcal{M}} = p_0\oint_{\partial\mathcal{M}}J_{\mathrm{geom}}^\mu\,d\Sigma_\mu \ge 0\); \(\dot{W}_{\mathrm{diss}} \equiv \int_{\mathcal{M}}(-J_E^\mu\nabla_\mu\mathcal{F})\sqrt{\vert g\vert}\,d^nx > 0\); \(\dot{W}_{\mathrm{tot}} \equiv \dot{W}_{\mathrm{ext}} + \dot{W}_{\mathrm{diss}} > 0\); \(\nabla_\mu\mathcal{F} \neq 0\); \(J_E^\mu \equiv \frac{J_W^\mu}{p_0} = -\frac{1}{p_0}\mathfrak{K}^{\mu\nu}\nabla_\nu\mathcal{F}\) (Branch II, fixed); \(J_E^\mu\nabla_\mu\mathcal{F} < 0\); \(\dot{s}_{\mathrm{prod}} = -\beta_{\mathrm{th}}J_E^\mu\nabla_\mu\mathcal{F} > 0\).
    • Physical Instantiation: The maintained arteriovenous pressure difference, together with filtration, holds the bed far from equilibrium. With \(J_E^\mu = -p_0^{-1}(k_{\mathrm{b}}^{\mu\nu}/C_{\mathrm{v}})\nabla_\nu\mathcal{F}\), \(J_E^\mu\nabla_\mu\mathcal{F} < 0\) holds identically wherever \(\nabla\mathcal{F} \neq 0\) (precondition 1), so \(\dot{s}_{\mathrm{prod}} > 0\) and \(\dot{W}_{\mathrm{diss}} > 0\). Blood flows down its pressure gradient from the arteriolar to the venular boundary, in the direction of \(J_E^\mu\). \(\dot{W}_{\mathrm{ext}} = 0\), so \(\dot{W}_{\mathrm{tot}} = \dot{W}_{\mathrm{diss}} > 0\). The empirical content of \(O_1\) is the flux-prediction test (aperture_generation_guidance.md §2.3, §5.5 item 6). \(k_{\mathrm{b}}\) is estimated per closure (ii) from the bed's pressure–flow relation at several steady perfusion pressures before the window (a declared perturbation regime), and \(C_{\mathrm{v}}\) from the blood-volume response to a pressure step measured optically (a different modality). The declared law must predict \(J_W^{\mathrm{meas}} = q^{\mathrm{meas}}/C_{\mathrm{v}}\) across a declared interior section \(S\), with \(q^{\mathrm{meas}}\) from the summed flows of the capillaries crossing \(S\) (red-cell velocity and lumen cross-section by intravital microscopy, per unit area), and \(\nabla\mathcal{F}\) from micropressure measurements on both sides of \(S\); neither uses the other or the declared \(k_{\mathrm{b}}\) or \(k_{\mathrm{f}}\). No current test exercises this aperture (empirical_validation_protocol.md §3.3).

    \(O_2\) — Unimodular Weyl Factorization, Constraint Precision & Trajectories¶

    • Spine Equations: \(g_{\mu\nu}(\tau, \mathbf{x}) = \Omega^2(\tau, \mathbf{x})\hat{g}_{\mu\alpha}(\mathbf{x})(\exp\hat{h}(\tau, \mathbf{x}))^\alpha_{\;\nu}\) with \(\det\hat{g} = 1\), \(\mathrm{Tr}(\hat{h}) = 0 \implies \det g = \Omega^{2n}\), \(\frac{\partial\ln\Omega}{\partial\tau} = \frac{1}{n}\theta\); \(\Pi_{\mu\nu} = \nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu\Phi\); \(G_{\mu\nu} = \kappa_G T_{\mu\nu}^{\mathrm{mat}}\), \(R = \kappa_G\nabla_\alpha\xi^\alpha\); \(\frac{2(n-1)}{n}\nabla^2\theta + \frac{2R}{n}\theta = -\kappa_G\frac{\partial(\nabla_\alpha\xi^\alpha)}{\partial\tau} + 2\nabla^\alpha\nabla^\beta\sigma_{\alpha\beta} - 2\sigma^{\alpha\beta}R_{\alpha\beta}\); \(\left.\left(\frac{dv^\mu}{d\tau} + \Gamma^\mu_{\rho\epsilon}v^\rho v^\epsilon + g^{\mu\lambda}\left(\frac{\partial g_{\lambda\nu}}{\partial\tau}\right)v^\nu - \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial g_{\rho\epsilon}}{\partial\tau}v^\rho v^\epsilon\right)v^\mu\right)\right\vert_{N=1,\,\beta^\mu=0} = f^\mu_{\mathrm{drive}}\), \(f^\mu_{\mathrm{drive}} = (1-u^2)\left(a^\mu_{\mathrm{d}} - c_*^{-2}(g_{\alpha\beta}a^\alpha_{\mathrm{d}}v^\beta)v^\mu\right)\), \(u^2 = g_{\mu\nu}v^\mu v^\nu/c_*^2 < 1\), with the default \(a^\mu_{\mathrm{d}} = 0\).
    • Physical Instantiation: On the rigid tissue the unimodular Weyl split is trivial (\(\Omega \equiv 1\), \(\hat{h} \equiv 0\), \(\partial_\tau\ln\Omega = 0\)). The split isolates the volume element only algebraically; \(\theta\) remains coupled to \(\xi_\alpha = \hat{\Pi}_{\alpha\lambda}\eta^\lambda\) and \(\sigma_{\alpha\beta}\) through the traced elliptic \(\tau\)-compatibility equation (r10 Decision 2), whose two sides vanish identically here (§0.1 items 2 and 4). Vessel-wall stresses and the tone of vascular smooth muscle are Cauchy stresses and active tensions, not \(T_{\mu\nu}^{\mathrm{mat}}\); tone acts through the arteriolar boundary data. The architecture of the network enters through \(k_{\mathrm{b}}\) and the declared geometry of \(\mathcal{M}\), not through \(g\). \(\Pi_{\mu\nu}\) is the Hessian of a declared precision potential and enters only the coupling sector (\(O_6\)). Eq. [3] is instantiated with the default drive and maps no tracer.

    \(O_3\) — Shear Damping & Hysteresis Consolidation¶

    • Spine Equations: \(\left.\frac{\partial g_{\mu\nu}}{\partial\tau}\right\vert_{N=1,\,\beta^\mu=0} = \frac{2}{n}\theta g_{\mu\nu} + 2\sigma_{\mu\nu}\); \(\nabla_\tau\sigma^\mu_{\;\nu} \equiv \partial_\tau\sigma^\mu_{\;\nu} = -\gamma_\sigma\sigma^\mu_{\;\nu}\) (\(\gamma_\sigma = \mathrm{const} > 0\), \(\sigma^\mu_{\;\mu} = 0\)) \(\implies \lim_{\tau\to\infty}\Vert\sigma^\mu_{\;\nu}\Vert_{C^2(\mathcal{M})} = 0\); \(\hat{h}(\tau, \mathbf{x}) \xrightarrow{C^2(\mathcal{M})} \hat{h}_\infty(\mathbf{x})\), \((\hat{g}_\infty(\mathbf{x}))_{\mu\nu} \equiv \hat{g}_{\mu\alpha}(\mathbf{x})(\exp\hat{h}_\infty(\mathbf{x}))^\alpha_{\;\nu}\) (\(\det\hat{g}_\infty = \det\hat{g} = 1\)); \(V_{\mathrm{local}}(\tau) \equiv \int_{\mathcal{U}_i}\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{x}, \tau)\sqrt{\vert g\vert}\,d^nx\).
    • Physical Instantiation: On the rigid tissue \(\sigma^\mu_{\;\nu} \equiv 0\), so the shear law holds trivially for any \(\gamma_\sigma > 0\), \(\hat{h} = \hat{h}_\infty = 0\), and \(\hat{g}_\infty = \hat{g} = \delta\); this aperture supports no shear-relaxation test (§0.1 item 2). The relaxation of the pressure field toward its new steady state is a property of the \(O_4\) balance law and is not mapped to \(\sigma\). The haemodynamic shear stress on vessel walls is a stress, not the shear rate \(\sigma\) of the metric. Recovery after a stressor lies outside \(\mathcal{M}\) and is not mapped here. \(V_{\mathrm{local}}\) is the hysteresis content of a cover set.

    \(O_4\) — Free-Energy Balance, Spectral Low-Pass Gating & Boundary Stress Conservation¶

    • Spine Equations: \(\frac{\partial\mathcal{F}}{\partial\tau} + \mathcal{F}\theta = -\nabla_\alpha J_W^\alpha - \mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{F}}\), \(J_W^\alpha \equiv -\mathfrak{K}^{\alpha\beta}\nabla_\beta\mathcal{F}\), \(\mathfrak{K}^{\alpha\beta} = \mathfrak{K}^{\beta\alpha} \succ 0\), \(\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{F}} \ge 0\); \(\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{\Lambda_{\mathrm{gate}}} \equiv \mathbf{1}_{(-\nabla^2 \le \Lambda_{\mathrm{gate}}^2)}\); \(Q(\mathbf{x}, \tau) = \langle\mathbf{x}\vert\rho_{\mathrm{local}}(\tau)\vert\mathbf{x}\rangle > 0\); \(\dot{W}_{\mathrm{var}} = \frac{1}{t_0\beta_{\mathrm{th}}}D_{\mathrm{KL}}(Q \parallel P) > 0\); \(\nabla^2\xi_\nu + R_\nu^{\;\alpha}\xi_\alpha = 0 \iff \nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu}^{\mathrm{mat}} = 0\).
    • Physical Instantiation: With \(\theta \equiv 0\) the balance law reads \(\partial_\tau\mathcal{F} = \nabla_\alpha((k_{\mathrm{b}}^{\alpha\beta}/C_{\mathrm{v}})\nabla_\beta\mathcal{F}) - k_{\mathrm{f}}\mathcal{F}\): the storage–conductance equation of a compliant porous capillary bed with first-order filtration, in pressure form. Compliance gives the finite relaxation time, so the field is stored, not quasi-static (precondition 8). The anisotropic \(k_{\mathrm{b}}\) and the filtration law depart from the default and satisfy the binding closure, and each is rejected if the default fits at least as well (closure (iv)). The gate sector follows §0.1 item 8: the Neumann eigenmodes of \(-\nabla^2\) on the rigid tissue are fixed, so the gate does not drift, and the faster decay of fine spatial structure in the pressure field is mapped via Spine \(\mathcal{T}\) to the gate without being the gate. The selective permeability of the capillary wall to water and solutes is a property of the sink's parameters, not the spatial gate. The stress-conservation equivalence holds identically (§0.1 item 4).

    \(O_5\) — Riemannian Geometric Mean Gluing, Percolation & Harmonic Circulation¶

    • Spine Equations: \(g_{\mu\nu}^{(ij)} \equiv (g^{(i)} \# g^{(j)})_{\mu\nu}\); \(\mathcal{U}_{ij}\) geodesically convex for \(g^{(ij)}(\tau) \; \forall \tau \ge \tau_0\); \(\phi_{\mathrm{perc}}(\mathcal{K}) = \vert\mathcal{K}\vert/\vert\mathcal{K}_0\vert > \phi_c\); \(\Delta_{\mathrm{Hodge}}\mathbf{J}_{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{harm}} = 0\); \(\partial_\tau\sqrt{\vert g^{(i)}\vert} \ge 0\); \(g_{\mu\nu}^{(i)} \xrightarrow{C^2(\mathcal{U}_i)} G_{\infty,\mu\nu}^{(i)} \succ 0\) (standing hypothesis with \(H_0 = 0\)).
    • Physical Instantiation: The cover consists of \(\tau\)-independent subregions of the tissue: one cover set around each excised vessel segment, containing its wall (a concave part of \(\partial\mathcal{M}\)), and further sets that are intersections of \(\mathcal{M}\) with strictly convex bodies clear of those walls. Every overlap is declared clear of the concave parts and convex on the rigid metric (§0.1 item 9). Each \(g^{(i)}\) is the restriction of \(g\), so the gluing holds identically, \(\partial_\tau\sqrt{\vert g^{(i)}\vert} = 0\), and \(g^{(i)} = G_\infty^{(i)}\) at every \(\tau\) (\(H_0 = 0\)). Every overlap is crossed by the transient, so \(\phi_{\mathrm{perc}} = 1 > \phi_c\). The connectivity of the capillary network is a property of the bed that enters through \(k_{\mathrm{b}}\): a network below its percolation threshold has no network-scale conductance and is not an admissible domain. That connectivity is mapped via Spine \(\mathcal{T}\) to the condition \(\phi_{\mathrm{perc}} > \phi_c\) and is not identified with it; anastomoses between vascular territories are context. Each excised vessel segment that passes through \(\mathcal{M}\) adds a non-contractible loop, so \(b_1(\mathcal{M}) \ge 1\) in general, and harmonic Neumann fields circulate around these segments. No blood flow or measured field is identified with them, and no claim is made about them (§0.1 item 9).

    \(O_6\) — Asymmetric Phase-Lead Coupling & Trace-Free Deformation Operator¶

    • Spine Equations: \((J_{[i\to j]})_\nu = -(J_{[j\to i]})_\nu\); \(M^{(ij)}(\mathbf{x}) \neq 0 \; \forall \mathbf{x} \in \bar{\mathcal{U}}_{ij}\) (hypothesis) \(\wedge \; \mathrm{Tr}\,M^{(ij)} = 0 \implies m_+ > 0 > m_-\); \(\frac{d}{d\tau}\mathcal{I}(Q_i; Q_j) = k_{\mathcal{I}}\oint_{\partial\mathcal{U}_{ij}} S_{\mu\nu}^{(ij)}\hat{n}^\mu\hat{n}^\nu\,dA > 0\) (hypothesis, \(\langle i,j\rangle \in \mathcal{K}\)), with this boundary flux integrable on \([\tau_0, \infty)\) (Branch II).
    • Physical Instantiation: Take two adjacent cover sets along the flow path between a feeding arteriole and a draining venule, sharing an overlap \(\mathcal{U}_{ij}\). The partner fields are the rate of change of the free-energy flux of blood in the two orientations (§0.1 item 9), so \(\eta_{[i\to j]} = \partial_\tau J_W\): a spatially resolved flux field, not a temporal lag between scalar signals such as heart rate and blood pressure.
    • The arteriolar boundary holds the raised pressure and the venular boundary the datum, so after the step the flux near the arterioles decays from an initial overshoot while nearer the venules it rises, and the zero set between them travels toward the venules during the early window.
    • The excised vessels make the geometry far from a slab, so the zero set is located only by the predicted solution, from the independently estimated \(k_{\mathrm{b}}\), \(C_{\mathrm{v}}\), and \(k_{\mathrm{f}}\) and the declared boundary data.
    • The declared overlap is one that the predicted zero set does not reach during the overlap's window, with the detection threshold of §0.1 item 9; on it \(\eta_{[i\to j]}\) keeps one direction, which the declaration states from the predicted solution.

    The odd 1-form \(J_{[i\to j]} = -J_{[j\to i]}\) is its image under the declared non-singular precision map, with distinct Hessians, and is non-zero there. Under the non-degeneracy hypothesis \(M^{(ij)} \neq 0\), built per r7 \(O_6\) Eq. [3], \(\mathrm{Tr}\,M^{(ij)} = 0\) gives \(m_+ > 0 > m_-\), with \(m_+ \neq -m_-\) in general. The eigendirections are directions of the operator \(M^{(ij)}\), not sympathetic and parasympathetic influences, and not directions of enhanced or suppressed perfusion. Under the flux-positivity hypothesis the coupling transmits correlation between the subsystem marginals of the declared state sector (\(\frac{d}{d\tau}\mathcal{I}(Q_i; Q_j) > 0\)). Both are standing hypotheses, not consequences of perfusion. In Branch II the transient decays as the bed settles, so the coupling flux is integrable on \([\tau_0, \infty)\). With \(\sigma \equiv 0\) the overlaps keep their declared convex shape, and the r10 §5.12 constraint has no counterpart here. No range of autonomic perturbation, clinical or otherwise, is mapped to a convexity threshold.

    \(O_7\) — Principal-Bundle Gauge Coherence, Impedance Decay & Hysteresis Alignment¶

    • Spine Equations: \(D_\mu = \partial_\mu - i g_{\mathrm{YM}}\,\mathrm{ad}_{A_\mu}\); \(\Vert\hat{\mathcal{E}}_\mu\Vert_{C^1(\mathcal{M})} \to 0 \implies [F_{\mu\nu}^{\mathrm{YM}}, \hat{\chi}] \to 0\); \(\chi_E \equiv 1 + \frac{n\,\sigma^\mu_{\;\nu}\sigma^\nu_{\;\mu}}{\theta^2 + \langle\Theta_E\rangle_{\mathcal{M}}^2} \to 1\), \(\hat{\chi} \equiv \chi_E\hat{u}\) (Branch II); \(D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\hat{\rho}_g(\tau) \parallel \hat{\rho}_{\mathcal{H}}(\tau)) \to 0\).
    • Physical Instantiation: The gauge sector is the trivial declaration of §0.1 item 10, so \([F_{\mu\nu}^{\mathrm{YM}}, \hat{\chi}] = 0\) identically. With \(\sigma \equiv 0\), \(\chi_E \equiv 1\) and \(\hat{\mathcal{E}}_\mu \equiv 0\) at every \(\tau\), and \(\chi\) is undefined (\(\theta = 0\)). \(\hat{\rho}_g = \frac{1}{n}\delta^\mu_\nu\) at every \(\tau\), and alignment with \(\hat{\rho}_{\mathcal{H}}\) holds where the Hessian of the declared convex record becomes isotropic, an asymptotic condition stated and not tested. The \(O_7\) Eq. [3] identities hold with divergence-free \(J_{\mathrm{geom}}\) and \(u_{\mathrm{exp}}\), bounded \(J_E\), and \(\hat{h} \equiv \hat{h}_\infty\). Coherence between cardiac, respiratory, and vasomotor rhythms, and physiological synchrony between persons, are not mapped to the gauge connection; they are context, and no coherence or synchrony claim is made.

    \(O_\Omega\) — Topological Asymptote, Branch II: Bounded Non-Equilibrium Steady State¶

    • Spine Equations: Branch II (\(H_0 = 0\)): \(\theta \xrightarrow[\tau\to\infty]{C^2(\mathcal{M})} 0\) with \(\int_{\tau_0}^{\infty}\langle\theta\rangle_{\mathcal{M}}\,d\tau < \infty\) and \(\langle\Theta_E\rangle_{\mathcal{M}} \to \Theta_\infty > 0\); \(\sigma^\mu_{\;\nu} \to 0\); \(\partial_\tau g_{\mu\nu} \to 0\); \(V_{\mathcal{M}} \to V_\infty\) and \(\mathcal{I}(\tau) \to s_0V_\infty\); \(\left.\hat{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathrm{stat}}(\mathbf{x})\Psi[g_{\mu\nu}, \mathcal{H}]\right\vert_{\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{sol}}[g]} = (J_E^\lambda\nabla_\lambda q)\frac{\delta\Psi}{\delta\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{x})} \xrightarrow[\tau\to\infty]{} 0 \iff J_E^\lambda\nabla_\lambda q \to 0 \iff \frac{\partial\mathcal{H}}{\partial\tau} \to 0\) (with \(\vert\delta\Psi/\delta\mathcal{H}\vert\) bounded and bounded away from \(0\) uniformly as \(\tau \to \infty\)).
    • Physical Instantiation: Under held arteriolar and venular pressures, the bed approaches a steady pressure field that carries a steady flow of blood from the arterioles to the venules, with steady filtration: a bounded non-equilibrium steady state. \(\theta \equiv 0\), \(V_{\mathcal{M}} = V_\infty\), \(\langle\Theta_E\rangle \to \Theta_\infty > 0\) while perfusion is maintained, \(\sigma \equiv 0\), and \(\partial_\tau g \equiv 0\). The transient coupling decays, so \(\mathcal{I} \to s_0V_\infty\). The generator condition requires \(J_E^\lambda\nabla_\lambda q \to 0\), an asymptotic hypothesis on the declared state sector that no finite window tests. The state is neither static equilibrium, which would require uniform pressure with no flow and no filtration (the arrest of circulation), nor a periodic return: the cardiac and respiratory cycles are excluded from windows by tolerance, and successive changes of autonomic tone form a sequence of windows. A calm or regulated affective state of the person is not mapped to this limit. The limit belongs to the declared idealization of held tone and constant interstitial pressures; the physical bed leaves it as tone changes or oedema develops, which bounds the usable window.
    • Within windows the supported tests (aperture_generation_guidance.md §5.5) are the non-degeneracy check (4), the signs of \(\Theta_E\), \(\dot{s}_{\mathrm{prod}}\), and \(\dot{W}_{\mathrm{tot}}\) (5), the flux prediction (6), and the Branch II evidence (7).
