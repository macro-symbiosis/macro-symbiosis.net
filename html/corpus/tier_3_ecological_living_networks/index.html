    Document ID: tier_3_ecological_living_networks.md

    0. Tier Architecture & Four-Part Functional Homology¶

    Tier 3 maps vascular plant physiology, movement ecology, benthic and edaphic biogeochemistry, and chronobiology onto Spine \(\mathcal{T}\). All four apertures are empirical apertures declared in Branch II of r7, the bounded non-equilibrium steady state (aperture_generation_guidance.md §2.3). Each declares, in its §1, a domain, physical metric, cone speed, storage coefficient, free energy with datum, transport tensor, interior sink, drive, and Branch II evidence, and instantiates all nine coordinates \(O_0 \to O_\Omega\) on that domain. Each declared domain is of one kind: a stored quantity (xylem water, prey biomass, a dissolved oxidant, a signal molecule) spreading through a living or biologically structured medium, with supply crossing the boundary and consumption, where declared, by a named interior sink. The wider phenomena named in the aperture titles enter the mappings through the declared parameters, as boundary conditions, through the hysteresis record \(\mathcal{H}\), or as illustrative context, and each §1 states which. Each aperture is formulated at the level of general physiology, ecology, and biogeochemistry, so that it applies across terrestrial, freshwater, and marine ecosystems without presupposing a specific habitat or site inventory.

    0.1 Conventions Common to the Four Tier 3 Apertures¶

    The following conventions apply to every aperture of this tier. Each aperture's §1 and coordinate blocks state its specific values and cite these items where they apply. Where an item repeats a convention of tier_2_abiotic_continuum_physics.md §0.1, it is restated here so that this tier can be read on its own.

    1. Branch and windows. Branch II is declared before any test, with the estimators of item 7 and §1, and no Tier 3 domain is re-evaluated in Branch I on the same data (closure (v)). Statements that hold in Branch I only (r10 Decision 6) are never applied. Every observation window is a relaxation window: it begins at a declared \(\tau_0\) from a prior steady state, a declared transient starts at \(\tau_0\), the external data are then held within declared tolerances, and the window follows the domain as it settles.
    2. In 3.1, 3.3, and 3.4 the transient is a declared step of the boundary data in the direction that raises \(\mathcal{F}\): a step decrease of evaporative demand at the leaves (3.1), and a step increase of the oxidant concentration of the overlying water (3.3) or of the signal concentration at the supply interface (3.4). Each declared balance law is a scalar parabolic equation whose sink is Lipschitz and non-decreasing in \(\mathcal{F}\) (zero, first order, Michaelis–Menten), so the comparison principle holds. The prior steady state is a subsolution for the raised data, so \(\mathcal{F}(\cdot, \tau_0 + h) \ge \mathcal{F}(\cdot, \tau_0)\); the held data make the problem invariant under time translation, so \(\mathcal{F}(\cdot, \tau + h) \ge \mathcal{F}(\cdot, \tau)\), that is, \(\partial_\tau\mathcal{F} \ge 0\) at every point. Where the step is in fact a ramp (non-decreasing boundary data that reach their held value over a finite rise time, as for secretion in 3.4), the same conclusion follows without time translation: \(\partial_\tau\mathcal{F}\) obeys the linearized equation with zero initial data (the prior steady state) and non-negative boundary data, so it stays non-negative. In 3.1 the xylem also exchanges water with capacitive living tissue outside the \(\mathcal{F}\) field; that coupled linear system is cooperative, and the same conclusion holds for it (3.1 §1). The argument is exact on rigid domains. Under the optional torsional perturbation of 3.1 the operator depends on \(\tau\) through \(g(\tau)\) at the order of the shear strain, so there the conclusion holds only to that order, and 3.1 checks it from its water-potential record.
    3. In 3.2 the prey data are held at their prior steady values, so \(\mathcal{F}\) is steady within the window, which aperture_generation_guidance.md §2.3 item 3 admits. The transient is the mixing of two predator groups, labelled at \(\tau_0\) by the patch each animal occupies (item 9 and 3.2 \(O_6\)).
    4. The rate of change of the flux, \(\partial_\tau J_W\), is not one-signed in general (item 9).
    5. Metric. The physical metric is the pull-back of the Euclidean metric to the material points of the declared body under its deformation map (for 3.2, to a fixed region of space). It is flat at every \(\tau\), so \(G_{\mu\nu} \equiv 0\) and \(R_{\mu\nu} \equiv 0\) (aperture_generation_guidance.md §2.3 item 2). No Tier 3 aperture declares a non-Euclidean material metric in this epoch: the growth material metric that the master table admits for 3.1 is discussed in 3.1 §1 and not declared. The curved configurations of the r10 §5.11 and §5.13 witnesses are therefore realized by none of these apertures, and r7's curvature sector is exercised by no Tier 3 aperture.
    6. Rigid domains (3.2, 3.3, 3.4). In a Cartesian chart \(g_{\mu\nu} = \delta_{\mu\nu}\), so \(\Omega \equiv 1\), \(\hat{h} \equiv 0\), \(\theta \equiv 0\), and \(\sigma^\mu_{\;\nu} \equiv 0\). For 3.2 this holds by construction, because the declared domain is a fixed region of space. For 3.3 and 3.4 the body is declared rigid within a pre-declared tolerance on its measured volumetric strain, and windows in which the measured volume decreases beyond that tolerance are excluded, so that \(\theta \ge 0\) is not violated. With \(\sigma \equiv 0\) the \(O_3\) shear law holds trivially, and these apertures support no shear-relaxation test.
    7. Volume-rigid domain (3.1). The volumetric strain of the conducting xylem stays within a declared tolerance, so \(\theta \equiv 0\) in the declared metric. Without the optional torsional perturbation, the shear strain also stays within tolerance and 3.1 is treated as rigid. With it, the metric is the pull-back under an isochoric twist: in a Cartesian material chart \(g(\tau) = \mathbf{F}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{F}\) with \(\det\mathbf{F} = 1\), so \(\Omega \equiv 1\), \(\theta \equiv 0\), \(\exp\hat{h} = g\), and \(\sigma^\mu_{\;\nu} \neq 0\). On that deforming domain every covariant quantity (Laplacian, Hessians, convexity, harmonic fields) is evaluated with \(g(\tau)\).
    8. Storage. The r7 \(O_4\) balance law carries unit storage: \(\partial_\tau\mathcal{F}\) is the rate of change of the stored quantity per unit volume. A living medium stores its diffusing quantity \(u\) with a coefficient \(s\) (stored amount per unit volume per unit of \(u\)): the hydraulic capacitance of stem sapwood in 3.1, unity in 3.2, the sediment porosity in 3.3, and the extracellular volume fraction times a binding retardation factor in 3.4. With \(s\) uniform, the physical balance \(s\,\partial_\tau u = \nabla_\alpha(k^{\alpha\beta}\nabla_\beta u) - r\) becomes the r7 law for \(\mathcal{F} = \epsilon u\) (with \(\epsilon\) a declared constant yield), \(\mathfrak{K} = k/s\), and \(\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{F}} = \epsilon r/s\), and the r7 flux is \(J_W = (\epsilon/s)\,j\), with \(j = -k\nabla u\) the physical flux. The factor \(\epsilon/s\) is a declared conversion factor, estimated independently (closure (ii)). If \(s\) varies in space, \(s^{-1}\nabla_\alpha(k^{\alpha\beta}\nabla_\beta u) = \nabla_\alpha(s^{-1}k^{\alpha\beta}\nabla_\beta u) + s^{-2}k^{\alpha\beta}\nabla_\alpha s\,\nabla_\beta u\), and the last term is a drift term that the r7 balance law does not contain. Each aperture therefore declares \(s\) uniform on \(\mathcal{M}\) within a tolerance (a declared, checkable precondition), or, in 3.3, states where the declared law departs from the physical one and bounds that departure.
    9. Stress sector. With \(G_{\mu\nu} \equiv 0\), \(T_{\mu\nu}^{\mathrm{mat}} \equiv 0\); its trace gives \(\nabla_\alpha\xi^\alpha = 0\) for \(n = 3\), and then \(\nabla_{(\mu}\xi_{\nu)} = 0\), so \(\xi\) is a Killing field. No Tier 3 medium has an identified global control field in this epoch, so Tier 3 declares \(\eta^\lambda \equiv 0\), hence \(\xi_\nu \equiv 0\) and \(R = \kappa_G\nabla_\alpha\xi^\alpha = 0\), and the \(O_4\) equivalence \(\nabla^2\xi_\nu + R_\nu^{\;\alpha}\xi_\alpha = 0 \iff \nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu}^{\mathrm{mat}} = 0\) holds identically. The \(\tau\)-compatibility law then reduces to \(\mathbb{L}_{\mathrm{Ein}}[\sigma]_{\mu\nu} = \frac{n-2}{n}(\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu\theta - g_{\mu\nu}\nabla^2\theta)\), and its trace, the traced elliptic equation, to \(\frac{n-1}{n}\nabla^2\theta = \nabla^\alpha\nabla^\beta\sigma_{\alpha\beta}\). These are the Saint-Venant compatibility conditions of the strain rate, which every deformation of an embedded body satisfies because its metric stays flat; with \(\theta \equiv 0\) (all Tier 3 domains) they read \(\mathbb{L}_{\mathrm{Ein}}[\sigma] = 0\) and \(\nabla^\alpha\nabla^\beta\sigma_{\alpha\beta} = 0\). The mechanical stresses of living media (turgor, growth stresses in wood, tissue and sediment stresses) are not \(T_{\mu\nu}^{\mathrm{mat}}\); they enter as boundary conditions, through the declared parameters, and through \(\mathcal{H}\). The partner fields \(\eta_{i\to j}\) of \(O_6\) (item 9) are distinct objects from the global \(\eta\) of \(O_4\).
    10. Cone speed and trajectories. Each aperture declares \(c_*\) as a characteristic signal speed of its medium (a sound or pressure-wave speed). Rays of that signal are null for the declared cone and, like all null rays, are not trajectories of \(O_2\) Eq. [3]. Every material and animal speed in \(\mathcal{M}\) is \(\ll c_*\), so \(u^2 \ll 1\) and \(f_{\mathrm{drive}} \approx a_{\mathrm{d}}\). The trajectory equation is displayed in physical units: both \(u^2 = g_{\mu\nu}v^\mu v^\nu/c_*^2\) and the projection term \(c_*^{-2}(g_{\alpha\beta}a^\alpha_{\mathrm{d}}v^\beta)v^\mu\) carry \(c_*\), and with \(c_* = 1\) the display reduces to the r7 form. Apertures 3.1, 3.3, and 3.4 instantiate Eq. [3] with \(a^\mu_{\mathrm{d}} = 0\) and map no tracer. Aperture 3.2 declares a taxis drive for predator trajectories; the driven trajectory equation is untested in this epoch (empirical_validation_protocol.md §3.3).
    11. Currents. In Branch II, \(J_E^\mu = J_W^\mu/p_0\) is fixed. The expansion current \(J_{\mathrm{geom}}\) enters only through its divergence \(\theta\) and its boundary flux \(dV_{\mathcal{M}}/d\tau\) (r7 \(O_0\) branch scope), and no supply current is ever mapped to it. In general \(\nabla_\mu J_E^\mu = -p_0^{-1}(\partial_\tau\mathcal{F} + \mathcal{F}\theta + \mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{F}}) \neq \theta\).
    12. Measured dissipation. The Branch II evidence requires \(\lim\langle\Theta_E\rangle > 0\) to be estimated from measured dissipation, never from \(\mathfrak{K}\nabla\mathcal{F}\). Integrating \(-J_E^\mu\nabla_\mu\mathcal{F}\) by parts and using the \(O_4\) balance law gives the identity $\(p_0\dot{W}_{\mathrm{diss}} = \oint_{\partial\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{F}\,(-J_W^\mu)\,d\Sigma_\mu - \int_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{F}\,\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{F}}\sqrt{\vert g\vert}\,d^nx - \frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{d\tau}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{F}^2\sqrt{\vert g\vert}\,d^nx - \frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{F}^2\theta\sqrt{\vert g\vert}\,d^nx\)$
    13. Where an outflow face is held at the datum \(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{ref}} = 0\) (3.2, 3.3, 3.4), it contributes nothing and the boundary term reduces to the supply face. The outflow faces of 3.1 (crown base, cambial interface) are not at the datum, and their terms are evaluated from the measured outflow and the measured \(\mathcal{F}\) there.
    14. Each term is evaluated from measured \(\mathcal{F}\), measured boundary fluxes, and measured or independently declared consumption; none uses the declared \(\mathfrak{K}\). Each §1 names instruments and surfaces different from those of the \(O_1\) flux-prediction test, so that the evidence is independent of that test; 3.3 states where this independence is weaker.
    15. Because the identity uses the balance law, the estimate is conditional on it. Each §1 also names a directly measured physical dissipation whose sign the estimate must share. Every such dissipation is non-negative, by the second law (3.1, 3.3, 3.4) or because a living system's metabolic power is positive (3.2), so this comparison tests only the sign of the identity-based estimate, and it is stated as such. Only signs and \(s_0\)-independent rates of \(\Theta_E\) are tested.
    16. Gate sector (\(O_4\)). \(\rho_{\mathrm{total}}(\tau)\) is a declared positive trace-class state on \(L^2(\mathcal{M}, d\mathrm{vol}_g)\), and \(Q\), \(P\), and the \(O_6\) marginals \(Q_i\) and joints \(Q_{ij}\) are densities of this declared state sector. No Tier 3 aperture identifies them with \(\mathcal{F}\) or with any measured field in this epoch, and none makes an empirical claim about the gate (empirical_validation_protocol.md §3.3). What each domain's physics supplies is, first, the spectral setting: the Neumann eigenmodes of \(-\nabla^2_g\) on \(\mathcal{M}\), whose eigenvalues are fixed on rigid domains, drift by a fraction of the order of the shear strain under the torsional perturbation of 3.1, and converge as \(g \to G_\infty\). Second, it supplies a physical low-pass behaviour of \(\mathcal{F}\): under the balance law, fine spatial structure in \(\mathcal{F}\) relaxes faster than coarse structure. That behaviour is mapped via Spine \(\mathcal{T}\) to the gate and is not itself the gate. Temporal smoothing of fluctuating supply by the medium is a property in time and is not the spatial gate.
    17. Cover, overlaps, and coupling (\(O_5\), \(O_6\)).
    18. The cover \(\{\mathcal{U}_i\}\) consists of \(\tau\)-independent material subregions of \(\mathcal{M}\) (for 3.2, fixed subregions of space) with Lipschitz boundaries, and every overlap \(\mathcal{U}_{ij}\) is convex at \(\tau_0\). Where \(\partial\mathcal{M}\) has concave parts (the core boundary of the conducting xylem in 3.1), each concave part lies in a single cover set, which need not be convex: r7 requires convex overlaps, not convex cover sets. Each \(g^{(i)}\) is the restriction of the one physical metric, so \(g^{(i)} = g^{(j)}\) on every overlap, the geometric mean returns the common metric (\(g \# g = g\)), and the gluing of \(O_5\) Eq. [3] holds identically.
    19. The anisotropy of a medium lives in \(\mathfrak{K}\), never in \(g\). Effective-medium averages of conductivities or diffusivities, including geometric-mean averages, are statements about \(\mathfrak{K}\) and are not mapped to \(O_5\) Eq. [1].
    20. Partner fields. In 3.1, 3.3, and 3.4 the partner fields are the rate of change of the free-energy flux read in the two orientations, \(\eta_{i\to j}^\lambda = \partial_\tau J_W^\lambda\) and \(\eta_{j\to i}^\lambda = -\partial_\tau J_W^\lambda\), defined on all of \(\mathcal{M}\), so \(\eta_{[i\to j]}^\lambda = \partial_\tau J_W^\lambda\). In 3.2 they are the movement-flux fields of the two labelled predator groups (3.2 \(O_6\)), which are not antisymmetric by construction.
    21. Zero sets. \(\partial_\tau J_W\) is not one-signed in general. Where the supply boundary holds a value of \(\mathcal{F}\) (3.3, 3.4), the flux near the supply decays after the step from an initial overshoot, so there \(\partial_\tau J_W\) points toward the supply, while farther downstream the flux rises toward its new steady value, so there it points away from the supply. Between the two lies the zero set of \(\partial_\tau J_W\). It travels during the early window, possibly overshooting its late position, and in the late single-mode regime it settles on a fixed surface. In a laterally uniform column, a precondition that 3.3 and 3.4 declare for this purpose, that surface is the level at which the slowest relaxation mode of the linearized problem peaks; without lateral uniformity it is located only by the predicted solution. In 3.1 the boundary data are of mixed kind (3.1 \(O_6\)), and the zero set is located only by the predicted solution. On the zero set \(\eta_{[i\to j]} = 0\) and \(J_{[i\to j]} = 0\), so a construction that included it would itself decide the non-degeneracy hypothesis there.
    22. Each aperture therefore declares overlaps \(\bar{\mathcal{U}}_{ij}\) that the zero set, predicted before the window from the independently estimated parameters, does not reach during the window. Before the transient arrives, the partner field on a downstream overlap is non-zero but exponentially small, so the check is made against a pre-declared detection threshold: each overlap's window starts once the predicted \(\vert\eta_{[i\to j]}\vert\) exceeds that threshold on \(\bar{\mathcal{U}}_{ij}\), and the data must then show \(\vert\eta_{[i\to j]}\vert\) above it there (a declared, checkable precondition). On such an overlap \(\eta_{[i\to j]}\) keeps one direction throughout the overlap's window, which each aperture states.
    23. Precision potentials. The potentials \(\Phi^{(i)}\) are declared before data collection as quadratic potentials (in a Cartesian chart, or in the Cartesian material chart for 3.1, with Hessians computed with \(g(\tau)\) under the torsional perturbation). Their trace-free parts \(\hat{\Pi}^{(i)}\) cannot be positive definite, because their eigenvalues sum to zero, so \(J_{[i\to j]} = \frac{1}{2}(\hat{\Pi}^{(i)} + \hat{\Pi}^{(j)})\eta_{[i\to j]}\) carries no sign relation to \(\eta_{[i\to j]}\).     Aperture 3.1: Vascular Plant Physiology & Rhizosphere Coupling (\(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{botanical}}\))¶

    1. Epistemological & Biological Scope¶

    Aperture 3.1 resolves how vascular plants lift water from the soil, through the rhizosphere, roots, and conducting xylem, to the leaves, where it is transpired: water moves under the tension that evaporation generates (the cohesion–tension mechanism), is buffered by the water stored in tissues, is regulated at the leaves by stomata, and is coupled at the roots to the rhizosphere and its mycorrhizal symbionts. Lexicon is strictly restricted to plant physiology, plant hydraulics, dendrology, plant biomechanics, and rhizosphere and mycorrhizal ecology.

    Declaration (aperture_generation_guidance.md §2.3; Branch II).

    • Domain \(\mathcal{M}\) (\(n = 3\)) and boundary \(\partial\mathcal{M}\). The conducting xylem (sapwood) of the main stem, between the root collar and the crown base: the functional conduits and fibres of a single organ class, with the living ray and axial parenchyma embedded among them, treated at the tissue scale as a porous continuum. The domain is confined to one organ class because xylem capacitance per unit volume differs systematically between trunk, branches, petioles, and veins: a capacitance jump at an organ junction would make \(J_W = q/C\) discontinuous where \(q\) is continuous, which the unit-storage balance law reads as an interface source or sink (§0.1 item 3). \(\partial\mathcal{M}\) consists of:
    • the root collar (supply face), across which water enters from the root system;
    • the crown-base cross-section (outflow), across which water leaves \(\mathcal{M}\) for the crown: the branch, petiole, and vein xylem and the leaf mesophyll, from which it is transpired, all of which lie outside \(\mathcal{M}\);
    • the cambial interface with the bark, across which water is exchanged with the bark's living tissue;
    • the boundary with the non-conducting core (pith or heartwood), which is impermeable.

    All supply crosses the root collar; there are no interior sources. The soil, the rhizosphere, and the roots lie outside \(\mathcal{M}\) and enter through the root-collar boundary condition: water enters at the rate set by the soil water potential, held during the window, and by declared root and rhizosphere conductances.     Aperture 3.2: Movement Ecology & Ecotone Foraging Dynamics (\(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{trophic}}\))¶

    1. Epistemological & Biological Scope¶

    Aperture 3.2 maps the movement ecology of foraging animals (random search, area-restricted search, taxis toward resources, and movement across habitat edges and ecotones) and the trophic dynamics of predator and prey onto Spine \(\mathcal{T}\). Its empirical domain is the redistribution and consumption of mobile prey in a three-dimensional foraging volume. There, random prey dispersal is a storage–diffusion process and predation is an interior sink, while the predators' trajectories follow a declared taxis drive. Lexicon is strictly restricted to movement ecology, behavioural ecology, trophic dynamics, and spatial population biology.

    Declaration (aperture_generation_guidance.md §2.3; Branch II).

    • Domain \(\mathcal{M}\) (\(n = 3\)) and boundary \(\partial\mathcal{M}\). A three-dimensional foraging volume within one habitat (a layer of vegetation canopy or a body of the water column), fixed in space over one foraging window. \(\partial\mathcal{M}\) consists of:
    • an ecotone boundary with an adjacent prey-source habitat (supply), across which prey disperse into \(\mathcal{M}\);
    • an outflow boundary with a habitat into which prey that cross are lost to \(\mathcal{M}\) without return, so that prey density there is held at the datum within tolerance;
    • closed faces with no prey flux.

    No prey reproduce inside \(\mathcal{M}\) and none enter except across the ecotone boundary, so all supply crosses \(\partial\mathcal{M}\). Within \(\mathcal{M}\), patches of differing structure (vegetation strata or plant types, or water layers) meet along internal transition zones. * Window. A foraging window (§0.1 item 1). It begins from a prior steady prey distribution, with prey supply at the ecotone, predator numbers, and habitat structure held within declared tolerances, so that \(\mathcal{F}\) is steady within the window. At \(\tau_0\) each tracked predator is labelled by the patch it occupies, and the window follows the mixing of the two labelled groups. The window is shorter than the prey's generation time and than the time over which predator numbers respond to prey. * Metric. Flat and rigid (§0.1 item 2): the domain is a fixed region of space, with \(g_{\mu\nu} = \delta_{\mu\nu}\) in a Cartesian chart, so \(\theta \equiv 0\) and \(\sigma \equiv 0\) by construction. Vegetation or water moving within the region is part of the medium and enters through \(\mathfrak{K}\) and as context. * Cone speed. \(c_*\) is the speed of sound in the ambient medium (air or water). Prey and predator speeds are \(\ll c_*\). * Storage. Unity: \(\mathcal{F}\) is itself the stored prey energy per unit volume, so no storage factor enters (§0.1 item 3). * Free energy \(\mathcal{F}\) and datum. \(\mathcal{F} = \epsilon_{\mathrm{p}}b\) (J m⁻³), with \(b\) the prey biomass density (g m⁻³) and \(\epsilon_{\mathrm{p}}\) the declared energy content of prey biomass (J g⁻¹), from calorimetry of the prey population. The datum is zero (\(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{ref}} = 0\)), and \(\mathcal{F} \ge 0\) holds trivially. \(\mathcal{F}\) is linear in prey biomass. The relaxation time \(\ell^2/D\) of prey dispersal across the volume is finite and resolvable. * Transport \(\mathfrak{K}\). \(\mathfrak{K}^{\alpha\beta} = D_{\mathrm{p}}^{\alpha\beta}\), the prey dispersal tensor, parametric: for example one horizontal and one vertical dispersal coefficient in a canopy layer or water column. Isotropic dispersal is the default. With \(j_b\) the prey biomass flux, \(J_W = \epsilon_{\mathrm{p}}j_b\). * Sink \(\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{F}}\). Predation with a type II functional response: $\(\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{F}} = \frac{aP\,\mathcal{F}}{1 + (ah/\epsilon_{\mathrm{p}})\,\mathcal{F}}\)$ Here \(a\) is the attack rate (volume searched per predator per unit time), \(h\) the handling time, and \(P\) the predator density, uniform within the window at an independently estimated value. \(\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{F}}\) is continuous, non-negative, non-decreasing in \(\mathcal{F}\), and zero at the datum. \(a\) and \(h\) are estimated from functional-response trials with the same predator individuals in a declared perturbation regime before the window (controlled prey densities), and \(P\) from a census, per closure (ii). * Drive. Predator taxis toward prey gradients, a named law (linear taxis with velocity relaxation, the deterministic part of a biased Ornstein–Uhlenbeck movement model): $\(a^\mu_{\mathrm{d}} = \tau_v^{-1}\left(\chi_{\mathrm{P}}\,g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_\nu b - v^\mu\right)\)$ Predators relax their velocity, at the rate \(\tau_v^{-1}\), toward a taxis velocity proportional to the prey-density gradient. The taxis sensitivity \(\chi_{\mathrm{P}}\) (the measured sensitivity of the master table) and \(\tau_v\) are estimated per closure (ii) from the same individuals' responses to an imposed prey gradient, in a declared perturbation regime before the window, never from the trajectories the law predicts. \(b \in C^2\) on the steady prey field of the window, so \(a^\mu_{\mathrm{d}}\) is jointly continuous and locally Lipschitz in \((\mathbf{x}, v)\), as r7 \(O_2\) Eq. [3] requires. * Eq. [3] is deterministic, so the declared reading is that \(f^\mu_{\mathrm{drive}}\) is the mean acceleration of predators conditional on position and velocity. The variability of search about that mean lies outside Eq. [3] and is not mapped. * The drive acts on individual trajectories. The uniform predator density declared for the sink is compatible with it only where taxis-driven redistribution of predators within the window stays below the uniformity tolerance (precondition 3). * Coupling-sector data. A predator search diffusivity \(D_{\mathrm{P}}\), estimated from predator tracks in a declared period before the window, is used, with the drive, only to predict the labelled-flux fields of \(O_6\) and their zero set. * Branch II evidence (closure (v)). * The trend of \(\langle\theta\rangle\) and \(\int\langle\theta\rangle\,d\tau\) are zero by construction for a fixed region of space; this part of the evidence holds trivially and is stated as such. * \(\lim\langle\Theta_E\rangle > 0\) is estimated from measured dissipation (§0.1 item 7): * the supply term uses the prey flux across the ecotone boundary, from bidirectional interception traps or counts at that boundary, with \(\mathcal{F}\) there from a census; * the outflow face, at the datum, contributes nothing; * the consumption term uses predation rates from observed kills or feeding records of tracked predators, weighted by the local \(\mathcal{F}\); * the storage term is checked to be zero by repeated census.

    None of these instruments or surfaces is that of the $O_1$ section.
    • \(\mathcal{F}\) is a resource energy density, not a thermodynamic potential of the dispersing medium, so \(\dot{s}_{\mathrm{prod}}\) is the entropy production of the declared mapping, not a measured physical entropy production. The physical dissipation named for the sign comparison is the metabolic power of the foraging community (by respirometry or isotope turnover), which is positive in any living system. The comparison therefore holds trivially and is stated as such.
    • Admissible domains (declared, checkable preconditions):
    • prey movement is unbiased random dispersal at the declared scale, with no drift from predator avoidance or resource tracking beyond tolerance, and prey numbers are large enough for a continuum density;
    • no prey reproduction, immigration other than across the ecotone boundary, or mortality other than predation within the window;
    • predator density uniform within tolerance and predator numbers constant throughout the window, checked by census.     Aperture 3.3: Benthic & Edaphic Biogeochemical Metabolism (\(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{metabolic}}\))¶

    1. Epistemological & Biological Scope¶

    Aperture 3.3 maps the metabolism of sediments and water-saturated soils onto Spine \(\mathcal{T}\): microbial respiration, the sequence of terminal electron acceptors, redox stratification, the decomposition of detritus, and the exchange of solutes across the sediment–water interface. Its empirical domain is the supply of a dissolved oxidant from overlying water, across the diffusive boundary layer (DBL), into a sediment column where microbes consume it. Lexicon is strictly restricted to biogeochemistry, microbial ecology, aquatic and soil metabolism, and bioenergetics.

    Declaration (aperture_generation_guidance.md §2.3; Branch II).

    • Domain \(\mathcal{M}\) (\(n = 3\)) and boundary \(\partial\mathcal{M}\). A column of sediment or water-saturated soil, for example an intact core, with the DBL of water above its surface. \(\mathcal{M}\) is the DBL together with the sediment down to a declared lower face. \(\partial\mathcal{M}\) consists of:
    • the top of the DBL, where the overlying water is well mixed (supply), held at a declared oxidant concentration \(c_w\);
    • the lower face, at the base of the zone that the oxidant penetrates, where it is consumed by reduced species diffusing up from below in a thin reaction zone outside \(\mathcal{M}\), so that the oxidant concentration is held at zero there (outflow at the datum);
    • closed lateral walls.

    The sediment–water interface lies inside \(\mathcal{M}\), with a declared smooth transition zone. There are no interior sources: all oxidant crosses the supply face. * Window. A relaxation window (§0.1 item 1), opened by a declared step increase of \(c_w\) from a prior steady state, after which \(c_w\) is held within tolerance. The window ends before the lower face moves beyond a declared tolerance, and before microbial activity changes beyond tolerance. * Metric. Flat and rigid (§0.1 item 2): \(g_{\mu\nu} = \delta_{\mu\nu}\) in a Cartesian chart, with compaction and swelling of the sediment within the rigid tolerance. * Cone speed. \(c_*\) is the compressional wave speed of the water-saturated sediment. Diffusive speeds are \(\ll c_*\). * Storage. In the sediment the oxidant's storage coefficient is the porosity \(\varphi_{\mathrm{s}}\), declared uniform over the sediment part of \(\mathcal{M}\) within tolerance; in the DBL it is unity. The declared law is normalized by \(\varphi_{\mathrm{s}}\) (§0.1 item 3). It is then exact in the sediment and keeps \(J_W\) continuous across the interface, as the physical flux is. Its only departure from the physical law is in the DBL, where it stores oxidant with the coefficient \(\varphi_{\mathrm{s}}\) instead of \(1\). Relative to the oxidant stored in the sediment, about \(\varphi_{\mathrm{s}}c\,\ell_{\mathrm{p}}\) per unit area against the excess \((1 - \varphi_{\mathrm{s}})c\,\delta\), that departure is of relative order \((1 - \varphi_{\mathrm{s}})\delta/(\varphi_{\mathrm{s}}\ell_{\mathrm{p}})\) in the storage rate, with \(\delta\) the DBL thickness and \(\ell_{\mathrm{p}}\) the oxidant penetration depth, and the pre-declared flux tolerance must exceed it (precondition 3). * Free energy \(\mathcal{F}\) and datum. \(\mathcal{F} = c\,(-\Delta G_r^{\circ\prime})\) (J m⁻³), with \(c\) the pore-water (or, in the DBL, water) concentration of the oxidant (mol m⁻³) and \(-\Delta G_r^{\circ\prime} > 0\) the declared standard-state free-energy yield per mole of oxidant reduced in the declared respiration reaction (J mol⁻¹), taken from tabulated standards. This is the declared linear form of aperture_generation_guidance.md §2.3 item 3: the actual reaction free energy depends on the concentrations, and the standard-state yield is used as a declared constant. The datum is zero (\(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{ref}} = 0\)), and \(\mathcal{F} \ge 0\) holds trivially. The relaxation time \(\ell_{\mathrm{p}}^2/D_{\mathrm{s}}\) is finite and resolvable. * Transport \(\mathfrak{K}\). In the sediment, \(\mathfrak{K}^{\alpha\beta} = D_{\mathrm{s}}^{\alpha\beta}\), the effective (tortuosity-corrected) pore-water diffusion tensor, parametric with layering: \(\mathrm{diag}(D_{\mathrm{H}}, D_{\mathrm{H}}, D_{\mathrm{V}})\) in a chart aligned with the bedding for laminated sediment, with isotropic \(D_{\mathrm{s}}\) as the default. In the DBL, \(\mathfrak{K} = (D_0/\varphi_{\mathrm{s}})\,g^{\alpha\beta}\), with \(D_0\) the molecular diffusivity of the oxidant in water, tabulated at the declared temperature and salinity. The two are joined smoothly across the declared transition zone, so \(\mathfrak{K} \in C^1\).     Aperture 3.4: Chronobiology & Phenological Synchrony (\(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{chrono}}\))¶

    1. Epistemological & Biological Scope¶

    Aperture 3.4 maps the temporal organization of living systems onto Spine \(\mathcal{T}\): circadian and other endogenous oscillators, their entrainment by environmental cues, the humoral and paracrine signals by which pacemaker tissues synchronize peripheral tissues, and the seasonal timing of development (dormancy, vernalization, flowering, mast seeding). Its empirical domain is the spatial delivery of an entraining signal into a target tissue: a signal molecule secreted by pacemaker cells outside the tissue diffuses into it and is degraded there. The oscillators and their phase dynamics lie outside the domain, and temporal cycles are never mapped to spatial operators. Lexicon is strictly restricted to chronobiology, the endocrinology of timing signals, phenology, nonlinear oscillator theory, and dynamical systems ecology.

    Declaration (aperture_generation_guidance.md §2.3; Branch II).

    • Domain \(\mathcal{M}\) (\(n = 3\)) and boundary \(\partial\mathcal{M}\). An avascular layer of target tissue, or a tissue slice or explant, that receives a humoral or paracrine entraining signal secreted by cells outside \(\mathcal{M}\). \(\partial\mathcal{M}\) consists of:
    • a supply interface (vascular or fluid) at which the signal concentration is set by the secretory output of the pacemaker cells and held during the window;
    • a drainage interface (vascular, lymphatic, or fluid) that holds the signal at the datum within tolerance (in a two-chamber explant preparation, the receiving medium);
    • closed faces.

    The tissue contains no cells that secrete the signal and no capillaries, so there are no interior sources and all supply crosses \(\partial\mathcal{M}\). * Window. A relaxation window (§0.1 item 1), opened by a declared step increase of the signal concentration at the supply interface from a prior steady state, after which the concentration is held at its new level within tolerance. In vivo the step is the rise of secretion at a declared phase of the pacemaker's cycle, which is a ramp with a finite rise time; \(\partial_\tau\mathcal{F} \ge 0\) then follows from the ramp form of the comparison argument (§0.1 item 1), not from time translation. In vitro it is a change of the supplying medium. The window ends before the pacemaker's output changes again. Each window therefore covers one monotone segment of the signal's cycle. The cycle itself is a sequence of windows and is never mapped to \(\theta\) or read as a return. * Metric. Flat and rigid (§0.1 item 2): \(g_{\mu\nu} = \delta_{\mu\nu}\) in a Cartesian chart, with swelling or shrinkage of the tissue within the rigid tolerance. * Cone speed. \(c_*\) is the longitudinal sound speed of the tissue. Diffusive speeds are \(\ll c_*\). * Storage. \(s = \alpha(1 + B)\): the extracellular volume fraction \(\alpha\) times the retardation factor \(1 + B\) of fast, linear, reversible binding of the signal (to extracellular matrix or to receptors). It is declared uniform over \(\mathcal{M}\) within tolerance (§0.1 item 3); saturable binding is excluded. * Free energy \(\mathcal{F}\) and datum. \(\mathcal{F} = RT\,c\) (J m⁻³), with \(c\) the extracellular concentration of the free signal (mol m⁻³) and the declared constant yield \(RT\) (J mol⁻¹) at the declared tissue temperature \(T\). \(RT\,c\) is the van 't Hoff osmotic pressure of the signal, an energy density linear in \(c\). It is the declared linear form of aperture_generation_guidance.md §2.3 item 3; the chemical potential itself is logarithmic in \(c\). The datum is zero (\(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{ref}} = 0\)), and \(\mathcal{F} \ge 0\) holds trivially. The relaxation time \(\ell^2/D^*\) across the layer is finite and resolvable. * Transport \(\mathfrak{K}\). \(\mathfrak{K}^{\alpha\beta} = D^{*\alpha\beta}/(1 + B)\), with \(D^{*\alpha\beta}\) the effective extracellular diffusivity tensor (the free diffusivity reduced by the tortuosity of the extracellular space). It is parametric along declared tissue axes (for example a fibre direction), with isotropic diffusivity as the default. With \(j\) the molar flux per unit tissue area, \(J_W = RT\,j/s\). The conversion factor \(RT/s\) is declared. \(\alpha\) and \(D^*\) are estimated per closure (ii) on the same tissue, for example from the real-time diffusion of an inert probe ion (a different modality), and \(B\) from binding assays of the same tissue after the window. * Sink \(\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{F}}\). Enzymatic degradation, first order: \(\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{F}} = k_{\mathrm{d}}\mathcal{F}\), with \(k_{\mathrm{d}} = \ln 2/t_{1/2}\) from the measured half-life of the signal in the same tissue, after its supply is removed in a declared perturbation regime. \(k_{\mathrm{d}}\) is the effective rate in the storage-normalized law: the degradation rate of free signal divided by \(1 + B\) if bound signal is protected. Cellular uptake and receptor internalization are included in \(k_{\mathrm{d}}\) where they are first order, and otherwise must be negligible. * Drive. Default, \(a^\mu_{\mathrm{d}} = 0\) (§0.1 item 5). * Branch II evidence (closure (v)). * The trend of \(\langle\theta\rangle\) and a bound on \(\int\langle\theta\rangle\,d\tau\) come from measurements of tissue thickness (optical or acoustic), which must stay within the rigid tolerance. * \(\lim\langle\Theta_E\rangle > 0\) is estimated from measured dissipation (§0.1 item 7): * the supply term uses the depletion record of the supplying compartment (or, in vivo, the difference between arterial and venous concentrations times the flow), with \(\mathcal{F}\) at the supply interface; * the drainage interface, at the datum, contributes nothing; * the consumption term uses the declared \(k_{\mathrm{d}}\) with \(\mathcal{F}\) from tissue concentration measurements that are not those of the \(O_1\) profile; * the storage term uses repeated measurements.

    The $O_1$ test uses the drainage interface, a different surface.
    • The physical dissipation is the entropy production of the signal's diffusion, which is non-negative by the second law, so the comparison tests only the sign of the estimate.
    • Admissible domains (declared, checkable preconditions):
    • an avascular tissue with no secretion of the signal and no other interior source;
    • \(\nabla\mathcal{F} \neq 0\) throughout \(\bar{\mathcal{M}}\);
    • uniform \(\alpha\) and fast, linear, reversible binding with constant \(B\) (or none), and no saturable uptake, within tolerance;
    • supply-interface concentration held at its new level, and drainage interface at the datum, within tolerance;
    • tissue dimensions within the rigid tolerance, and isothermal conditions within tolerance;
    • purely diffusive extracellular transport, with interstitial convection below tolerance;
    • for the one-dimensional statements of \(O_6\) only: a laterally uniform supply and tissue.
    • Titled phenomena outside the domain.
    • Pacemakers. The pacemaker oscillators and their cycles lie outside \(\mathcal{M}\), and their output sets the supply data window by window. Oscillator phase, period, and amplitude are temporal quantities and are not mapped to \(\theta\), \(\sigma\), or any spatial operator.
    • Entrainment. The entrainment of the target cells' own clocks by the delivered signal (phase response, phase locking) is a response in the target cells. It enters through \(\mathcal{H}\) and as context, and no synchronization claim is made (§0.1 item 10).
    • Environmental cues. Cues that entrain the pacemakers act outside \(\mathcal{M}\) and are context.
    • Phenology. Dormancy, vernalization, flowering time, and mast seeding are seasonal developmental timing, between windows. The memory of accumulated cues (for example accumulated chilling) is mapped via Spine \(\mathcal{T}\) to \(\mathcal{H}\) and is not identified with it.
    • Long cycles. Succession and multi-year cycles are context.

    2. Coordinate-by-Coordinate Mapping (\(O_0 \to O_\Omega\))¶

    \(O_0\) — Exogenous Flux & Emergent Foliation Time¶

    • Spine Equations: \(\theta \equiv -N K \overset{N=1,\,\beta^\mu=0}{=} \frac{1}{2}g^{\mu\nu}\frac{\partial g_{\mu\nu}}{\partial\tau} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\vert g\vert}}\frac{\partial\sqrt{\vert g\vert}}{\partial\tau} = \nabla_\mu J_{\mathrm{geom}}^\mu \ge 0\); \(\Theta_E \equiv \frac{\dot{s}_{\mathrm{prod}}}{s_0} = -\frac{\beta_{\mathrm{th}}}{s_0}J_E^\mu\nabla_\mu\mathcal{F}\); \(\Theta_{\mathrm{tot}} \equiv \sqrt{\theta^2 + \Theta_E^2}\), \(\tau_\Theta(\tau) \equiv \int_{\tau_0}^{\tau}\langle\Theta_{\mathrm{tot}}\rangle_{\mathcal{M}}\,d\tau'\); \(\frac{dV_{\mathcal{M}}}{d\tau} = \int_{\mathcal{M}}\theta\sqrt{\vert g\vert}\,d^nx = \oint_{\partial\mathcal{M}}J_{\mathrm{geom}}^\mu\,d\Sigma_\mu \ge 0\), with \(\lim_{\tau\to\infty}V_{\mathcal{M}}(\tau) = V_\infty < \infty\) (Branch II); \(\frac{\partial\mathcal{H}}{\partial\tau} \equiv J_E^\mu\nabla_\mu q\), \(q(\mathbf{x}, \tau) \equiv -\ln(v_0Q(\mathbf{x}, \tau))\).
    • Physical Instantiation: The tissue is rigid, so \(\theta \equiv 0\), \(dV_{\mathcal{M}}/d\tau = 0\), and \(V_{\mathcal{M}} = V_\infty\). The signal supplied across the supply interface is throughput and is never mapped to \(J_{\mathrm{geom}}\) (§0.1 item 6). Diffusion of the signal from the supply interface toward the drainage interface, with degradation along the way, produces entropy at every point, so \(\Theta_E > 0\) (by \(O_1\)), \(\Theta_{\mathrm{tot}} = \Theta_E\), and \(\tau_\Theta\) counts entropy production per \(s_0\). \(\tau\) remains laboratory proper time, and the oscillators' cycles run outside \(\mathcal{M}\) within this foliation; they are not \(\theta\), which never changes sign. The record accumulates by \(\partial_\tau\mathcal{H} = J_E^\mu\nabla_\mu q\) along the throughput current. The entrainment state of the target tissue (the phase of its cellular clocks) and its memory of accumulated seasonal cues are mapped via Spine \(\mathcal{T}\) to \(\mathcal{H}\) and are not identified with it; they enter each window through \(\mathcal{H}_0\).

    \(O_1\) — Non-Equilibrium Thermodynamic Openness¶

    • Spine Equations: \(\dot{W}_{\mathrm{ext}} = p_0\dot{V}_{\mathcal{M}} = p_0\oint_{\partial\mathcal{M}}J_{\mathrm{geom}}^\mu\,d\Sigma_\mu \ge 0\); \(\dot{W}_{\mathrm{diss}} \equiv \int_{\mathcal{M}}(-J_E^\mu\nabla_\mu\mathcal{F})\sqrt{\vert g\vert}\,d^nx > 0\); \(\dot{W}_{\mathrm{tot}} \equiv \dot{W}_{\mathrm{ext}} + \dot{W}_{\mathrm{diss}} > 0\); \(\nabla_\mu\mathcal{F} \neq 0\); \(J_E^\mu \equiv \frac{J_W^\mu}{p_0} = -\frac{1}{p_0}\mathfrak{K}^{\mu\nu}\nabla_\nu\mathcal{F}\) (Branch II, fixed); \(J_E^\mu\nabla_\mu\mathcal{F} < 0\); \(\dot{s}_{\mathrm{prod}} = -\beta_{\mathrm{th}}J_E^\mu\nabla_\mu\mathcal{F} > 0\).
    • Physical Instantiation: The maintained difference in signal concentration between the supply and drainage interfaces, together with degradation, holds the tissue far from equilibrium. With \(J_E^\mu = -p_0^{-1}\mathfrak{K}^{\mu\nu}\nabla_\nu\mathcal{F}\), \(J_E^\mu\nabla_\mu\mathcal{F} < 0\) holds identically wherever \(\nabla\mathcal{F} \neq 0\) (precondition 2), so \(\dot{s}_{\mathrm{prod}} > 0\) and \(\dot{W}_{\mathrm{diss}} > 0\). The signal flux runs down its gradient from the supply interface toward the drainage interface, in the direction of \(J_E^\mu\). \(\dot{W}_{\mathrm{ext}} = 0\), so \(\dot{W}_{\mathrm{tot}} = \dot{W}_{\mathrm{diss}} > 0\). The empirical content of \(O_1\) is the flux-prediction test (aperture_generation_guidance.md §2.3, §5.5 item 6). \(D^*\), \(\alpha\), and \(B\) are estimated on the same tissue as declared above. The declared law must predict \(J_W^{\mathrm{meas}} = RT\,j^{\mathrm{meas}}/s\) at the drainage interface, with \(j^{\mathrm{meas}}\) from the accumulation of the signal in the receiving compartment, and \(\nabla\mathcal{F}\) from a profiling method within the window that uses neither \(j^{\mathrm{meas}}\) nor the declared \(D^*\) or \(k_{\mathrm{d}}\). No current test exercises this aperture (empirical_validation_protocol.md §3.3). Each oscillator outside \(\mathcal{M}\) is a dissipative limit cycle that requires a continuous supply of free energy; that is context, not a coordinate of the declared domain.

    \(O_2\) — Unimodular Weyl Factorization, Constraint Precision & Trajectories¶

    • Spine Equations: \(g_{\mu\nu}(\tau, \mathbf{x}) = \Omega^2(\tau, \mathbf{x})\hat{g}_{\mu\alpha}(\mathbf{x})(\exp\hat{h}(\tau, \mathbf{x}))^\alpha_{\;\nu}\) with \(\det\hat{g} = 1\), \(\mathrm{Tr}(\hat{h}) = 0 \implies \det g = \Omega^{2n}\), \(\frac{\partial\ln\Omega}{\partial\tau} = \frac{1}{n}\theta\); \(\Pi_{\mu\nu} = \nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu\Phi\); \(G_{\mu\nu} = \kappa_G T_{\mu\nu}^{\mathrm{mat}}\), \(R = \kappa_G\nabla_\alpha\xi^\alpha\); \(\frac{2(n-1)}{n}\nabla^2\theta + \frac{2R}{n}\theta = -\kappa_G\frac{\partial(\nabla_\alpha\xi^\alpha)}{\partial\tau} + 2\nabla^\alpha\nabla^\beta\sigma_{\alpha\beta} - 2\sigma^{\alpha\beta}R_{\alpha\beta}\); \(\left.\left(\frac{dv^\mu}{d\tau} + \Gamma^\mu_{\rho\epsilon}v^\rho v^\epsilon + g^{\mu\lambda}\left(\frac{\partial g_{\lambda\nu}}{\partial\tau}\right)v^\nu - \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial g_{\rho\epsilon}}{\partial\tau}v^\rho v^\epsilon\right)v^\mu\right)\right\vert_{N=1,\,\beta^\mu=0} = f^\mu_{\mathrm{drive}}\), \(f^\mu_{\mathrm{drive}} = (1-u^2)\left(a^\mu_{\mathrm{d}} - c_*^{-2}(g_{\alpha\beta}a^\alpha_{\mathrm{d}}v^\beta)v^\mu\right)\), \(u^2 = g_{\mu\nu}v^\mu v^\nu/c_*^2 < 1\), with the default \(a^\mu_{\mathrm{d}} = 0\).
    • Physical Instantiation: On the rigid tissue the unimodular Weyl split is trivial (\(\Omega \equiv 1\), \(\hat{h} \equiv 0\), \(\partial_\tau\ln\Omega = 0\)). The split isolates the volume element only algebraically; \(\theta\) remains coupled to \(\xi_\alpha = \hat{\Pi}_{\alpha\lambda}\eta^\lambda\) and \(\sigma_{\alpha\beta}\) through the traced elliptic \(\tau\)-compatibility equation (r10 Decision 2), whose two sides vanish identically here (§0.1 items 2 and 4). Tissue architecture enters through the axes of \(D^*\), not through \(g\). \(\Pi_{\mu\nu}\) is the Hessian of a declared precision potential and enters only the coupling sector (\(O_6\)). Eq. [3] is instantiated with the default drive and maps no tracer: signal transport is diffusive and is described by the \(O_4\) balance law. Developmental and life-history trade-offs are not mapped to \(\hat{h}\).

    \(O_3\) — Shear Damping & Hysteresis Consolidation¶

    • Physical Instantiation: On the rigid tissue \(\sigma^\mu_{\;\nu} \equiv 0\), so the shear law holds trivially for any \(\gamma_\sigma > 0\), \(\hat{h} = \hat{h}_\infty = 0\), and \(\hat{g}_\infty = \hat{g} = \delta\); this aperture supports no shear-relaxation test (§0.1 item 2). The relaxation of the signal profile toward its new steady state is a property of the \(O_4\) balance law and is not mapped to \(\sigma\). The phase relationships that the target tissue retains after entrainment lie in \(\mathcal{H}\). \(V_{\mathrm{local}}\) is the hysteresis content of a layer \(\mathcal{U}_i\).

    \(O_4\) — Free-Energy Balance, Spectral Low-Pass Gating & Boundary Stress Conservation¶

    • Spine Equations: \(\frac{\partial\mathcal{F}}{\partial\tau} + \mathcal{F}\theta = -\nabla_\alpha J_W^\alpha - \mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{F}}\), \(J_W^\alpha \equiv -\mathfrak{K}^{\alpha\beta}\nabla_\beta\mathcal{F}\), \(\mathfrak{K}^{\alpha\beta} = \mathfrak{K}^{\beta\alpha} \succ 0\), \(\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{F}} \ge 0\); \(\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{\Lambda_{\mathrm{gate}}} \equiv \mathbf{1}_{(-\nabla^2 \le \Lambda_{\mathrm{gate}}^2)}\); \(Q(\mathbf{x}, \tau) = \langle\mathbf{x}\vert\rho_{\mathrm{local}}(\tau)\vert\mathbf{x}\rangle > 0\); \(\dot{W}_{\mathrm{var}} = \frac{1}{t_0\beta_{\mathrm{th}}}D_{\mathrm{KL}}(Q \parallel P) > 0\); \(\nabla^2\xi_\nu + R_\nu^{\;\alpha}\xi_\alpha = 0 \iff \nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu}^{\mathrm{mat}} = 0\).
    • Physical Instantiation: With \(\theta \equiv 0\) the balance law reads \(\partial_\tau\mathcal{F} = \nabla_\alpha(\mathfrak{K}^{\alpha\beta}\nabla_\beta\mathcal{F}) - k_{\mathrm{d}}\mathcal{F}\): the reaction–diffusion equation of a signal delivered across a boundary and degraded in the tissue, normalized by its storage coefficient. Its finite relaxation time makes the field stored, not quasi-static. The anisotropic \(D^*\) and the degradation law depart from the default and satisfy the binding closure, and each is rejected if the default fits at least as well (closure (iv)). The gate sector follows §0.1 item 8: the Neumann eigenmodes of \(-\nabla^2\) on the rigid tissue are fixed, so the gate does not drift, and the faster decay of fine spatial structure in the signal field is mapped via Spine \(\mathcal{T}\) to the gate without being the gate. The tissue also attenuates fast temporal fluctuations of its supply; that is a filter in time, distinct from the spatial gate, and it is not mapped to it. The stress-conservation equivalence holds identically (§0.1 item 4).

    \(O_5\) — Riemannian Geometric Mean Gluing, Percolation & Harmonic Circulation¶

    • Spine Equations: \(g_{\mu\nu}^{(ij)} \equiv (g^{(i)} \# g^{(j)})_{\mu\nu}\); \(\mathcal{U}_{ij}\) geodesically convex for \(g^{(ij)}(\tau) \; \forall \tau \ge \tau_0\); \(\phi_{\mathrm{perc}}(\mathcal{K}) = \vert\mathcal{K}\vert/\vert\mathcal{K}_0\vert > \phi_c\); \(\Delta_{\mathrm{Hodge}}\mathbf{J}_{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{harm}} = 0\); \(\partial_\tau\sqrt{\vert g^{(i)}\vert} \ge 0\); \(g_{\mu\nu}^{(i)} \xrightarrow{C^2(\mathcal{U}_i)} G_{\infty,\mu\nu}^{(i)} \succ 0\) (standing hypothesis with \(H_0 = 0\)).
    • Physical Instantiation: The cover consists of \(\tau\)-independent layers of the tissue at increasing distance from the supply interface, with slab-shaped overlaps \(\mathcal{U}_{ij}\) that stay convex on the rigid metric. Each \(g^{(i)}\) is the restriction of \(g\), so the gluing holds identically, \(\partial_\tau\sqrt{\vert g^{(i)}\vert} = 0\), and \(g^{(i)} = G_\infty^{(i)}\) at every \(\tau\) (\(H_0 = 0\); §0.1 item 9). Every overlap is crossed by the transient, so \(\phi_{\mathrm{perc}} = 1 > \phi_c\). The connectivity of the extracellular space is a property of the tissue that enters through \(D^*\): a tissue whose extracellular space does not percolate has no continuum diffusivity and is not an admissible domain. That connectivity is mapped via Spine \(\mathcal{T}\) to the condition \(\phi_{\mathrm{perc}} > \phi_c\) and is not identified with it. The declared tissue is simply connected, so \(\mathbf{J}_{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{harm}} \equiv 0\) (§0.1 item 9).

    \(O_6\) — Asymmetric Phase-Lead Coupling & Trace-Free Deformation Operator¶

    • Spine Equations: \((J_{[i\to j]})_\nu = -(J_{[j\to i]})_\nu\); \(M^{(ij)}(\mathbf{x}) \neq 0 \; \forall \mathbf{x} \in \bar{\mathcal{U}}_{ij}\) (hypothesis) \(\wedge \; \mathrm{Tr}\,M^{(ij)} = 0 \implies m_+ > 0 > m_-\); \(\frac{d}{d\tau}\mathcal{I}(Q_i; Q_j) = k_{\mathcal{I}}\oint_{\partial\mathcal{U}_{ij}} S_{\mu\nu}^{(ij)}\hat{n}^\mu\hat{n}^\nu\,dA > 0\) (hypothesis, \(\langle i,j\rangle \in \mathcal{K}\)), with this boundary flux integrable on \([\tau_0, \infty)\) (Branch II).
    • Physical Instantiation: Take two layers that share a transition zone \(\mathcal{U}_{ij}\). The partner fields are the rate of change of the signal's free-energy flux in the two orientations (§0.1 item 9), so \(\eta_{[i\to j]} = \partial_\tau J_W\): a spatially resolved flux field. Temporal phase lags between oscillators are not spatial 1-forms and are not used as partner fields.
    • The supply interface holds a value of \(\mathcal{F}\) and the drainage interface holds the datum, so after the step the flux near the supply decays from an initial overshoot, while farther in it rises. There \(\partial_\tau J_W\) points toward the supply and away from it, respectively, and the zero set between them travels inward during the early window.
    • In a laterally uniform layer (precondition 7) the late zero set is the level at which the slowest relaxation mode peaks. Without lateral uniformity it is located only by the predicted solution.
    • The declared overlap is one that the predicted zero set does not reach during the overlap's window, with the detection threshold of §0.1 item 9; on it \(\eta_{[i\to j]}\) keeps one direction, which the declaration states from the predicted solution.

    The odd 1-form \(J_{[i\to j]} = -J_{[j\to i]}\) is its image under the declared non-singular precision map, with distinct Hessians, and is non-zero there. Under the non-degeneracy hypothesis \(M^{(ij)} \neq 0\), built per r7 \(O_6\) Eq. [3], \(\mathrm{Tr}\,M^{(ij)} = 0\) gives \(m_+ > 0 > m_-\), with \(m_+ \neq -m_-\) in general. The eigendirections are directions of the operator \(M^{(ij)}\), not directions of enhanced or suppressed signalling. Under the flux-positivity hypothesis the coupling transmits correlation between the subsystem marginals of the declared state sector (\(\frac{d}{d\tau}\mathcal{I}(Q_i; Q_j) > 0\)). Both are standing hypotheses, not consequences of diffusion. In Branch II the transient decays as the tissue settles, so the coupling flux is integrable on \([\tau_0, \infty)\). With \(\sigma \equiv 0\) the overlaps keep their declared convex shape, and the r10 §5.12 constraint has no counterpart here.

    \(O_\Omega\) — Topological Asymptote, Branch II: Bounded Non-Equilibrium Steady State¶

    • Spine Equations: Branch II (\(H_0 = 0\)): \(\theta \xrightarrow[\tau\to\infty]{C^2(\mathcal{M})} 0\) with \(\int_{\tau_0}^{\infty}\langle\theta\rangle_{\mathcal{M}}\,d\tau < \infty\) and \(\langle\Theta_E\rangle_{\mathcal{M}} \to \Theta_\infty > 0\); \(\sigma^\mu_{\;\nu} \to 0\); \(\partial_\tau g_{\mu\nu} \to 0\); \(V_{\mathcal{M}} \to V_\infty\) and \(\mathcal{I}(\tau) \to s_0V_\infty\); \(\left.\hat{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathrm{stat}}(\mathbf{x})\Psi[g_{\mu\nu}, \mathcal{H}]\right\vert_{\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{sol}}[g]} = (J_E^\lambda\nabla_\lambda q)\frac{\delta\Psi}{\delta\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{x})} \xrightarrow[\tau\to\infty]{} 0 \iff J_E^\lambda\nabla_\lambda q \to 0 \iff \frac{\partial\mathcal{H}}{\partial\tau} \to 0\) (with \(\vert\delta\Psi/\delta\mathcal{H}\vert\) bounded and bounded away from \(0\) uniformly as \(\tau \to \infty\)).
    • Physical Instantiation: Under a held supply concentration the tissue approaches a steady signal profile that carries a steady flux from the supply interface, partly degraded within the tissue and partly drained: a bounded non-equilibrium steady state. \(\theta \equiv 0\), \(V_{\mathcal{M}} = V_\infty\), \(\langle\Theta_E\rangle \to \Theta_\infty > 0\) while supply is maintained, \(\sigma \equiv 0\), and \(\partial_\tau g \equiv 0\).
